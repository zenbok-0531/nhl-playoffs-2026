Game 1 in Denver  ·  15-goal track meet

The Track Meet.

Colorado beat Minnesota 9–6 in a Game 1 that looked nothing like either team's identity. 15 combined goals. Five in every period. 14 different goal-scorers. Makar twice after returning from injury. The Avalanche blew a 3–0 lead, fell behind 5–4, and won by three. The structural matchup we previewed never happened. The result was more revealing than the structural one would have been.

Two of the league's best defensive teams just played a 9–6 game. 15 combined goals. Five goals in every period. 14 different goal-scorers. Tied for second-most goals in a single playoff game ever. The Norris confrontation we previewed Sunday — Makar vs Hughes anchoring a structural matchup — got swept aside in favor of a track meet neither team is built around. And Colorado won it anyway. Wedgewood gave up more goals than he did in the entire LA series. Minnesota proved they can score on Colorado at will. They also proved they can't stop Colorado from doing the same.

Round 2 · Series 02 · West  ·  Game 1

Minnesota at Colorado

Series Standing
Avalanche 1 — Wild 0
  • Colorado 9, Minnesota 6. Avalanche set a franchise record for goals in a playoff game. They led 3–0 at 6:47 of the first, fell behind 5–4 in the second on a Foligno short-handed goal, and pulled away in the third on Makar's two goals. This was the 10th playoff game ever with 15+ combined goals — and only the second since 1994.
  • Cale Makar's night had everything: scary first-period hit from Foligno, leg flying into the air, left for the locker room. Came back. Did some twirls at the end of the first. Returned for the second. Got an assist on Blankenburg's goal. Then scored twice in the third, including the game-winner with 2:54 remaining to make it 8–6.
  • Devon Toews quietly had a 4-point night — game-high in points — including the goal that tied it 5–5 in the second period. Both Avalanche top defensemen produced. Makar 2G+1A, Toews 1G+3A. That's the Norris-tier defense pair the publication previewed Sunday — and they delivered exactly the kind of game-deciding production their reputation suggested.
  • The structural collapse on both sides: two of the league's best defensive teams couldn't generate any defensive structure at all. Wedgewood (the league's best regular-season GAA) gave up more goals tonight than in his entire 4-game LA sweep. Wallstedt stopped 34 shots but couldn't slow the bleeding. Both elite goalies got beaten. The defensive environment broke down completely.
  • The historical context that matters: Colorado is now 72–1 since moving to Denver in 1995–96 when leading a playoff game by 3 or more goals. The lone loss was Game 5 against St. Louis in 2022 — the year they won the Cup. That's the sample that defines Colorado's structural identity. Tonight they almost added to the loss column. They didn't.
  • Minnesota proved something concerning: they can break Colorado's lead, but they can't hold their own. Up 5–4 entering the late second, the Wild gave up the tying goal 23 seconds later (Toews) and never led again. That's the Dallas series in microcosm. Minnesota wins moments. They lose games.

What This Was
A complete identity collapse on both sides. Neither team played the kind of hockey they're built around. No structure. No defensive pressure on the puck. No clean breakouts. Just rush chances, broken plays, and goals. Minnesota's path to winning this series required forcing Colorado into structural hockey. Game 1 was the opposite of that, and Colorado won.
What This Says About Colorado
The Avalanche can win in any game script. They controlled the LA sweep on pace and structure. They won this game on chaos and recovery. That's the same trait Vegas demonstrated in Round 1 — the championship combination of "we can play your game and beat you at it." Colorado is now Tier 1 with no remaining caveats — they've shown it in two different game environments in two consecutive series.
What This Says About Minnesota
The disruptor read from Day 10 needs a caveat. Minnesota disrupted Dallas at 5v5 because Dallas couldn't out-skate them. Colorado can. Tonight Minnesota had the lead and couldn't hold it. They generated 6 goals against the league's best regular-season goalie. That's offensive credibility. But you don't get to 6 goals in a Round 2 win against Colorado. You get there in a 9–6 loss.
Makar's Night
A Norris-defining performance bookended by an injury scare. The Foligno hit could have been catastrophic — Makar's right leg went airborne before he hit the ice. The fact he returned, did diagnostic twirls, played the rest of the game, and scored the game-winner is the kind of star moment that sets a series tone. Hughes had a quiet game by comparison. The Norris confrontation went decisively to Makar in Round 1 of the matchup.
Series Outlook · Updated
Avalanche in 6, slight lean now — but Game 2 needs adjustments from Minnesota that aren't simple. The Wild can't trade chances with Colorado. They need Wallstedt to steal a game. They need to get Eriksson Ek back (out for Games 1 and 2 per pre-game reports). They need to slow the pace, which Colorado just demonstrated they can match either way. Minnesota's structural advantage was in Round 1. In Round 2, the structural advantage is Colorado's.
§ 02

The wider Round 2 picture.

Game 1 wasn't the only Round 2 news Sunday. Three injury notes shape the matchups going forward.

Round 2 Injury Notes

Joel Eriksson Ek out for Games 1 and 2

Minnesota's two-way center will miss the first two games against Colorado. Eriksson Ek is the Wild's most important defensive forward — the player who anchors their checking line and matches up against opposing top centers. His absence in Game 1 partially explains Minnesota's defensive collapse against Colorado's top line. If he returns by Game 3, the matchup looks fundamentally different. If he's out longer, Minnesota's path narrows considerably.

Edmonton Postscript

McDavid played through a fracture

Edmonton coach Kris Knoblauch confirmed Sunday that Connor McDavid played through a fracture during the Round 1 series against Anaheim. The publication's Day 12 obituary flagged the injuries as worse than reported, citing Knoblauch's admission that "multiple players were playing through fractures." The McDavid confirmation removes any ambiguity. Edmonton lost a series with their best player visibly compromised. The "average team all year" quote remains the structural truth, but the injury context now carries verified weight.

Other Notes

Carrick, Ostlund out for Round 2

Two depth forward names ruled out for Round 2 across multiple teams. Roster context worth tracking as the series develop.

§ 03

Tonight: two more Game 1s.

Carolina hosts Game 2 of their series with Philly. Anaheim opens at Vegas. By tomorrow morning, all four Round 2 series will have started.

  • PHI at CAR Game 2 — 4:00 PM ET. After Carolina's 3–0 statement opener, the Flyers need an unrecognizably better game just to stay in the series. Tippett's status remains the structural variable — without their fastest player, Philly couldn't break Carolina's forecheck in Game 1. Power play (2-for-21 across the postseason) has to wake up.
  • ANA at VGK Game 1 — 6:30 PM ET. The publication's most analytically interesting Round 2 matchup begins. Two evolving Tier 1 candidates with opposite playing styles. Vegas built theirs on experience and structure; Anaheim built theirs on speed and chaos that evolved into structure mid-Round 1. The matchup answers the question: can a young, fast, evolving team break a team that doesn't break?
  • The bracket reads: By Tuesday, every Round 2 series will have at least one game played. Game 1 results so far suggest Round 2 is going to be more chaotic than the structural Round 1 most of these teams won. Carolina dominated Game 1 (3–0). Colorado won 9–6. The next two might break either pattern.
The Take
Round 1 told us which teams could survive playoff hockey. Round 2's first two games are telling us which teams can shape it. Carolina dictated structurally Saturday. Colorado dictated chaotically Sunday — same result, different proof. Both teams won by being undeniable in the kind of game they were forced into. That's the Tier 1 trait the publication has been tracking since Day 1, and it's still the question Round 2 has to answer for the rest of the field.
