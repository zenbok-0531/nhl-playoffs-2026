The Maturity.

Colorado beat Minnesota 5–2 in Game 2. The track meet became a clinic. MacKinnon: 1G+2A, three straight 3-point playoff games. Wedgewood: 6–0 in his first 6 career playoff starts, joining a list of three goalies. Power play: 2-for-3. Game 1 was Colorado's ceiling. Game 2 was their maturity. The Avs and Canes are now both 6–0 — two undefeated teams left in the bracket.

Game 1 raised a question: was Colorado's 9–6 win against Minnesota a function of the Wild's defensive collapse, or could the Avalanche actually win a structural game? Game 2 answered it cleanly: 5–2, two power-play goals, MacKinnon putting up his third straight 3-point game, Wedgewood holding the line at 29 saves for his 6th straight playoff win. The track meet became a clinic. Colorado tightened structure, dominated special teams, and won by being precisely the kind of team they were built to be. Game 1 was their ceiling. Game 2 was their maturity. And the most concerning truth for the rest of the bracket: they have both.

§ 01

Colorado tightened up.

Bednar's words: "The details were much better in all situations." That's the difference. The talent was always there. The structure now matches it.

Round 2 · Series 02 · West  ·  Maturity

Minnesota at Colorado

Series Standing
Avalanche 2 — Wild 0
  • Colorado 5, Minnesota 2. Necas opened the scoring at 2:51 of the first on a backhand off Gustavsson's glove, fed by MacKinnon driving the play. Six seconds later, Kaprizov tied it 1–1 on a top-shelf breakaway. But the early back-and-forth was a feint — Colorado controlled the rest of the game.
  • Special teams decided everything. Colorado: 2-for-3 on the power play. Minnesota: 0-for-2. Both coaches named special teams as the difference postgame. Hynes: "I thought 'Gus' gave us a chance to win. The difference in the game was the special teams." Bednar: "The difference for me is special teams."
  • Landeskog's PP goal at 8:24 of the first restored the lead 2–1. Roy made it 3–1 early second. MacKinnon's PP one-timer late in the third made it 4–1 — the dagger goal that ended any Wild comeback hope. Nichushkin emptied it.
  • The MacKinnon historical context is meaningful: three consecutive 3-point playoff games — only the 6th player in 40 years. Joins Draisaitl (5 in 2022), Rantanen (4 in 2025), Pavelski (3 in 2010), Sakic (3 in 1997), Maruk (3 in 1986). He also passed Sakic for most multipoint playoff periods in franchise history (21) and passed Forsberg for 2nd-most playoff goals in Avalanche/Nordiques history (59).
  • Wedgewood's quietly spectacular run: 6–0 in his first 6 career playoff starts. Only the 4th goalie in NHL history to start that way — joining Cam Ward (7 in 2006, won Conn Smythe), Tiny Thompson (7 in 1929–30), and Jean-Sebastien Giguere (6 in 2003, won Conn Smythe). Two of the three precedents won Conn Smythes. The list is short and the company is decorated.
  • The structural truth Minnesota now lives with: Colorado fixed the exact things they did wrong in Game 1. Cleaner structure. Fewer odd-man rushes. Better defensive detail. And the offense didn't drop — they still scored 5 and dominated special teams. That's the championship combination: improving without sacrificing.

What This Was
The maturity Colorado has been building toward all season. In older Avs playoff runs, high offense often meant defensive looseness. Tonight the stars produced (MacKinnon, Necas, Landeskog) and the structure held simultaneously. That's a championship profile — and the kind of evolution that turns a Tier 1 team into a favorite.
What Minnesota Faces
After Game 1: "Maybe Colorado chaos isn't sustainable." After Game 2: "Oh — they can play disciplined too." That changes everything. The Wild competed at 5v5, got Gustavsson 18 saves on his playoff debut, didn't get blown out — and still lost by three. The margin for error has disappeared. They cannot beat Colorado at 5v5 trading chances and they cannot beat them on special teams.
What Has To Change
Per Hynes postgame: "Special teams need to be better." That's the headline issue. The deeper structural fix: slower pace, more net-front, longer offensive-zone cycles. Minnesota cannot beat Colorado in transition consistently — Game 1 proved that, Game 2 confirmed it. The Wild's path home to St. Paul for Game 3 Saturday is real, but it requires a fundamentally different game plan than what's worked so far this postseason.
Series Outlook · Updated
Avalanche in 5, strong lean. Colorado has now demonstrated they can win in three different game environments — chaos (Game 1), structure (Game 2), and special teams (both). Minnesota's path requires winning Game 3 at home and producing at least one stolen game in Denver. That's a tall ask against a team that hasn't lost in six tries. The Wild are a credible Tier 2 team. The Avalanche are operating at a different level entirely.

The Real Bracket Read

The remaining field has split into two categories. Teams that control environments — Colorado, Vegas, Carolina — decide how games are played. Teams reacting within environments — Montreal, Buffalo, Anaheim, Minnesota, Philly — can win games and stretches but can't dictate full structure consistently. The playoffs are increasingly rewarding teams that can win both structure and chaos. Right now Colorado does it best, Vegas does it most calmly, and Carolina does it most systematically.

§ 03

Tonight: Buffalo-Montreal G1.

The last Round 2 series finally starts. Two upstart teams meet for the first playoff matchup in their shared history. Buffalo speed vs Montreal resilience.

  • Buffalo's first home Round 2 game since 2007. The crowd at KeyBank Center will be a factor — Buffalo just snapped a 14-season playoff drought this year. The energy advantage is real.
  • Montreal arrives on a Game 7 high. The Habs flew home from Tampa late Sunday after winning a 2–1 nail-biter. They've now played 7 Round 1 games to Buffalo's 6 — a fatigue factor worth tracking. Bell Centre delivered home advantage in Round 1; KeyBank Center is the road test.
  • Stylistic contrast is real. Buffalo wants to dictate pace through speed and active defense. Montreal wants to win at the margins through Dobeš and structural discipline. The team that imposes their style first probably wins Game 1 — and possibly the series.
  • Dobeš vs Lyon is the goalie matchup. Both rookies (relatively). Both swing performers. Neither has been tested by an elite offensive team yet — Tampa's pressure was structural, Boston's was situational. Buffalo's pace is a different test entirely.
  • The ranking question: the publication's #4 (Montreal) meets #5 (Buffalo). The series winner becomes a credible Cup contender; the loser ends a successful but capped playoff run. Either way, this is the most evenly-matched series of Round 2.
The Take
Round 1 told us which teams could survive playoff hockey. Round 2 is teaching us which teams can dictate it. Colorado just demonstrated both versions of themselves in two consecutive games. Carolina did the same Saturday and Monday. Vegas did it Round 1. The teams advancing now aren't just competitive — they're composers, deciding the kind of game that gets played and winning whatever it turns into. Two undefeated teams, three Tier 1 teams in firm control, five Tier 1.5+ teams chasing. The Cup picture has clarified. Three teams have separated. Buffalo-Montreal tonight could narrow the chase.
