Carolina sweeps Philly  ·  8–0 in playoffs

The Closeout.

Carolina beat Philadelphia 3–2 in overtime to complete the sweep. The Hurricanes are now 8–0 in the postseason — the second team in the past 30 years to start a playoff run with eight straight wins. They're the first team through to the Conference Final. Stankoven (his 7th playoff goal) and Hall continued their hot run. Andersen weathered Philly's best push. The publication has been calling Carolina the structural standard since Round 1 Day 1. Today they earned the matching result. Colorado-Minnesota Game 3 tonight in St. Paul.

The Carolina Hurricanes are 8–0 in the Stanley Cup Playoffs and the first team through to the Conference Final. Today's closeout in Philadelphia wasn't easy — Carolina trailed in the third before Stankoven's 7th playoff goal, watched Philly tie it back at 2–2 less than two minutes later, and had to grind out an overtime win against a Flyers team finally playing their best hockey of the series. But the result was consistent with everything the Hurricanes have shown for two weeks: depth scoring (Stankoven to 7 goals, Hall continuing his career-best run), Andersen weathering pressure, and the structural identity that's now defining Round 2. Carolina has played 8 games. They've won all 8. They've now demonstrated 5 different ways to win a playoff game: domination, special teams, recovery, grind, and overtime closeout. The publication's Day 7 framing called Carolina "The Standard." Today they made it literal.

· · · First Team Through To Conference Final · · ·

Carolina advances.

The Hurricanes finish their second-round series 4–0 against the Philadelphia Flyers and become the first team through to the Eastern Conference Final — their second consecutive trip to the round. They'll wait for the Buffalo-Montreal series winner. Game 1 of the East Conference Final won't be until at least the middle of next week. Carolina has played 8 playoff games and won 8. The bracket has 7 other teams still trying to advance.

§ 01

Carolina closed it.

Philly played their best game of the series. They had the lead twice. They were a post away from a 3–2 lead late in the second. None of it mattered. Carolina found a way — again — and ended Philly's season in overtime.

Round 2 · Series 01 · East  ·  Advanced

Carolina at Philadelphia

Series Final
Hurricanes 4 — Flyers 0
  • Carolina 3, Philadelphia 2 in overtime. Series sweep, 4–0. The Flyers led after the first, came back from a Stankoven third-period lead to tie it 2–2 less than two minutes later, and pushed Carolina to overtime in their best showing of the series. It still wasn't enough.
  • The structural backdrop nobody at Xfinity Mobile Arena could change: Carolina out-shot Philly 36–15 in regulation. Even in their best game of the series, the Flyers couldn't out-generate the Hurricanes. They could match Carolina's score for stretches. They couldn't match the underlying possession. 2026 Carolina has been the same all postseason — 70%+ xG share is not a fluke; it's an identity.
  • Stankoven's 7th playoff goal at 4:13 of the third put Carolina up 2–1: a one-timer from outside the crease off a beautiful cross-ice pass from Taylor Hall. Stankoven now leads or shares the playoff goal lead among forwards. The Stankoven-Blake-Hall line that the publication's been tracking since Day 1 of Round 2 has now produced through 8 games of two different series.
  • Philly's tying goal 1:39 later was Bump's: shot past Andersen's glove side from the slot, set up by Konecny from behind the net. The kind of clinical chance Philly didn't generate enough of in Games 1–3. It was Bump's 2nd of the series and his second-period chance that hit the post on Andersen's glove side was the Flyers' best look at a multi-goal lead all night.
  • The disallowed Jankowski goal early in the third was the moment that mattered most: Tocchet challenged for goalie interference (Carrier interfering with Vladar) and won. Without that overturn, Carolina goes up 3–1 and the closeout follows quickly. The Flyers got every break tonight that wasn't an actual goal.
  • Carolina's PK kept its postseason streak alive: now 31-of-33 in the playoffs. Philly's PP went 1-for-3 tonight (3-for-36 across the playoffs). The Flyers' best-converting playoff PP game of the series — and they still lost. That's the structural truth Carolina has been imposing for two weeks.
  • Andersen weathered Philly's best push in the second period: Dvorak's wrist shot off the post in the closing seconds, two prime late-2nd chances Philly couldn't convert. The Athletic flagged it earlier in the series — Carolina's structure slips occasionally, and Andersen has answered every time. Today was the loudest version of that pattern yet.

What This Was
The hardest closeout Carolina has had all postseason — and they still won. Philly genuinely outplayed Carolina for stretches of the third period, got the disallowed-goal break, fought through to overtime with their season on the line. And it changed nothing. That's the most concerning thing about Carolina for the rest of the bracket: their floor is so high that even an opponent's best night still ends in a Carolina win.
The Stankoven-Hall Story
Stankoven hit 7 playoff goals on a beautiful cross-ice setup from Taylor Hall. Hall now has 10 points in 8 playoff games — a personal playoff career-high already, with the Conference Final still to come. The line that includes Jackson Blake has produced 5 of Carolina's 8 series-deciding goals through two rounds. The publication has been tracking the Stankoven-Blake-Hall line as the bracket's most consistent producing line since Round 2 Day 1. Today's evidence: still true.
Philly's Season
It ended in overtime, in their building, in the kind of game they wished they'd played from Game 1. The Flyers genuinely improved across the series — Game 1 was a structural blowout, Game 2 was an OT loss, Game 3 was a special-teams meltdown, Game 4 was their best showing. The arc of the series was the Flyers learning how to play Carolina hockey at exactly the rate Carolina was already winning. First playoff appearance since 2020. Conference Final not yet. But a young core got a meaningful baseline.
What's Next
Carolina to the Eastern Conference Final. They'll wait for the Buffalo-Montreal winner — series tied 1–1, Game 3 Sunday at Bell Centre. Buffalo and Montreal look likely to go 6 or 7 games, which means Carolina gets at least a week of rest before the Conference Final begins. The publication's pre-Round 2 read on Carolina was "structural standard with championship trajectory." Today the trajectory got measurably steeper.
§ 02

The 8–0 club.

Day 7 named the 7–0 club: only the third NHL team in 30 years to win their first seven games of a postseason. Today Carolina extended it to eight. The club is even smaller now.

Historical Context

Carolina is the second NHL team in 30 years to begin a postseason 8–0.

Two days ago, Carolina joined the 7–0 club — three teams in 30 years. Today they shrunk that club further. Starting a playoff run 8–0 has happened twice in three decades. Carolina is the second. The structural story has been visible for weeks: 70%+ xG share, depth scoring across two complete lines plus activated bottom-six, an elite PK (now 31-of-33), and an Andersen run that's lifted them through every moment of Round 2 friction. The bracket question now becomes whether anyone can beat them — not whether Carolina is real.

§ 03

Tonight: Colorado-Minnesota G3.

Wild's first home Round 2 game. Down 2–0. Need a Wallstedt steal. Eriksson Ek status pending. The game most likely to reshape the Western bracket.

  • Game 3 in St. Paul, Minnesota. The Wild are home for the first time in Round 2 and trying to cut Colorado's series lead in half. Down 2–0 is recoverable. Down 3–0 against the publication's #1-ranked team is functionally a death sentence.
  • Eriksson Ek's status is the structural variable. He missed Games 1 and 2 with a lower-body injury, and his absence has been most felt on the checking line against MacKinnon. If he returns tonight, Minnesota's matchup options expand meaningfully. If not, the path narrows further.
  • Wallstedt vs Wedgewood (or Mrazek). Wedgewood was 6–0 entering Round 2 and is now playoff-tested. Wallstedt has been credible but hasn't yet stolen a game. Tonight is when Minnesota needs a goalie steal — or a 5v5 dominance they haven't shown all series — to keep this competitive.
  • Per The Athletic, the Wild's PK "has to shape up." Colorado has scored in 5 PP opportunities across Games 1–2; Minnesota's PK has cracked. The structural special-teams gap mirrors the Anaheim-Vegas series that just happened — the team falling behind on special teams is falling behind on results.
  • The bracket question: if Colorado wins tonight, they go to 7–0 and join Carolina in even rarer 30-year company. If Minnesota wins, the Wild get back into the series and the publication's #1 ranking gets its first real challenge of Round 2. Either result is editorially significant.
The Take
The bracket has 8 teams left, but only one of them has finished its second-round series. Carolina's 8–0 isn't just a good start — it's the rarest start of any team in 30 years still alive when this round began. The publication's read on Carolina hasn't moved since Round 1 Day 1. Today the result finally caught up to the analytical voice. The Eastern Conference Final is set on one side. The other seven teams — Colorado, Vegas, Anaheim, Buffalo, Montreal, Minnesota, and the trailing Flyers — are all still trying to do what Carolina just finished.
