The Separation.

Saturday gave the bracket its sharpest day yet. Carolina swept Philly with a 3–2 OT win at 3 PM ET — 8–0 in the playoffs, second team in 30 years to start a postseason that way, first team through to the Conference Final. Six hours later, Minnesota beat Colorado 5–1 to end the Avalanche's 6-game streak and cut the series lead to 2–1. Kaprizov took the NHL playoff scoring lead. The two-undefeated narrative the publication has been tracking since Day 4 just collapsed into one. Carolina is now alone at the top.

Six hours apart. Two streaks. One survived; one didn't. At 3 PM Eastern, Carolina completed the sweep of Philadelphia in overtime — survived Philly's best showing of the series, won 3–2 on a Stankoven third-period goal and an OT closeout, and became the first team through to the Eastern Conference Final at 8–0. The second team in 30 years to start a postseason that way. At 9 PM Eastern, Minnesota beat Colorado 5–1 in St. Paul — outhit them 39–25, scored on the 4-on-4 and again on the 4-on-3, chased Wedgewood after three goals on twelve shots, and ended the Avalanche's six-game playoff streak. Kaprizov took the NHL playoff scoring lead. The "two undefeated teams" framing the publication has been tracking since Day 4 just collapsed into one. Carolina now stands alone in the rarest 30-year company in hockey. The other seven teams in the bracket are all playing real series.

· · · First Team Through To Conference Final · · ·

Carolina advances.

The Hurricanes finish their second-round series 4–0 against the Philadelphia Flyers and become the first team through to the Eastern Conference Final — their second consecutive trip to the round. They'll wait for the Buffalo-Montreal series winner. Game 1 of the East Conference Final won't be until at least the middle of next week. Carolina has played 8 playoff games and won 8. The bracket has 7 other teams still trying to advance.

§ 01

Carolina closed it.

Philly played their best game of the series. They had the lead twice. They were a post away from a 3–2 lead late in the second. None of it mattered. Carolina found a way — again — and ended Philly's season in overtime.

Round 2 · Series 01 · East  ·  Advanced

Carolina at Philadelphia

Series Final
Hurricanes 4 — Flyers 0
  • Carolina 3, Philadelphia 2 in overtime. Series sweep, 4–0. The Flyers led after the first, came back from a Stankoven third-period lead to tie it 2–2 less than two minutes later, and pushed Carolina to overtime in their best showing of the series. It still wasn't enough.
  • The structural backdrop nobody at Xfinity Mobile Arena could change: Carolina out-shot Philly 36–15 in regulation. Even in their best game of the series, the Flyers couldn't out-generate the Hurricanes. They could match Carolina's score for stretches. They couldn't match the underlying possession. 70%+ xG share is not a fluke; it's an identity.
  • Stankoven's 7th playoff goal at 4:13 of the third put Carolina up 2–1: a one-timer from outside the crease off a beautiful cross-ice pass from Taylor Hall. Stankoven now leads or shares the playoff goal lead among forwards. The Stankoven-Blake-Hall line that the publication's been tracking since Day 1 of Round 2 has now produced through 8 games of two different series.
  • Philly's tying goal 1:39 later was Bump's: shot past Andersen's glove side from the slot, set up by Konecny from behind the net. The kind of clinical chance Philly didn't generate enough of in Games 1–3.
  • The disallowed Jankowski goal early in the third was the moment that mattered most: Tocchet challenged for goalie interference (Carrier interfering with Vladar) and won. Without that overturn, Carolina goes up 3–1 and the closeout follows quickly. The Flyers got every break tonight that wasn't an actual goal.
  • Andersen weathered Philly's best push in the second period: Dvorak's wrist shot off the post in the closing seconds, two prime late-2nd chances Philly couldn't convert. Carolina's structure slipped occasionally; Andersen answered every time. Today was the loudest version of that pattern yet.

What This Was
The hardest closeout Carolina has had all postseason — and they still won. Philly genuinely outplayed Carolina for stretches of the third period, got the disallowed-goal break, fought through to overtime with their season on the line. And it changed nothing. That's the most concerning thing about Carolina for the rest of the bracket: their floor is so high that even an opponent's best night still ends in a Carolina win.
The Stankoven-Hall Story
Stankoven hit 7 playoff goals on a beautiful cross-ice setup from Taylor Hall. Hall now has 10 points in 8 playoff games — a personal playoff career-high already, with the Conference Final still to come. The line that includes Jackson Blake has produced 5 of Carolina's 8 series-deciding goals through two rounds. The publication has been tracking this line as the bracket's most consistent producing line since Round 2 Day 1. Today's evidence: still true.
Philly's Season
It ended in overtime, in their building, in the kind of game they wished they'd played from Game 1. The Flyers genuinely improved across the series — Game 1 was a structural blowout, Game 2 was an OT loss, Game 3 was a special-teams meltdown, Game 4 was their best showing. The arc of the series was the Flyers learning how to play Carolina hockey at exactly the rate Carolina was already winning. First playoff appearance since 2020. Conference Final not yet. But a young core got a meaningful baseline.
What's Next
Carolina to the Eastern Conference Final. They'll wait for the Buffalo-Montreal winner — series tied 1–1, Game 3 Sunday at Bell Centre. Buffalo and Montreal look likely to go 6 or 7 games, which means Carolina gets at least a week of rest before the Conference Final begins. The publication's pre-Round 2 read on Carolina was "structural standard with championship trajectory." Today the trajectory got measurably steeper.
§ 02

The 8–0 club.

Day 7 named the 7–0 club: only the third NHL team in 30 years to win their first seven games of a postseason. Today Carolina extended it to eight. The club is even smaller now.

Historical Context

Carolina is the second NHL team in 30 years to begin a postseason 8–0.

Two days ago, Carolina joined the 7–0 club — three teams in 30 years. Today they shrunk that club further. Starting a playoff run 8–0 has happened twice in three decades. Carolina is the second. The structural story has been visible for weeks: 70%+ xG share, depth scoring across two complete lines plus activated bottom-six, an elite PK (now 31-of-33), and an Andersen run that's lifted them through every moment of Round 2 friction. The bracket question now becomes whether anyone can beat them — not whether Carolina is real.

§ 03

Wild imposed.

The team that came in with the worst PK in 30+ years just made the team that came in undefeated look ordinary. Minnesota outhit Colorado 39–25, drove the game from the opening shift, and finally got their special teams to win a night.

Round 2 · Series 02 · West  ·  The Wild Get In

Colorado at Minnesota

Series Standing
Avalanche 2 — Wild 1
  • Minnesota 5, Colorado 1. Wild scorers: Kaprizov, Hughes, Hartman (PP), Faber, Boldy (EN). A Hartman one-timer through Wedgewood early in the second period chased the goalie. The Avalanche's first loss in seven playoff games.
  • The 4-on-4 sequence that opened the game: Kaprizov scored 17 seconds into a 4-on-4 at the end of the first. Faber up the ice, handed off to Kaprizov, who took it through the middle. 1:33 later, Toews hooked Boldy to make it 4-on-3, and Hughes toe-dragged to the middle and wristed inside the far post for 2–0. Colorado got beaten at structural defending in their own kind of game.
  • Hartman's PP goal early second made it 3–0 and chased Wedgewood. Wedgewood: 3 goals on 12 shots, pulled. The 6–0 historical streak that the publication tracked through Day 5 has now turned into "pulled in Game 3." Mackenzie Blackwood — first action since April 14 — finished the game in net.
  • The PK that won the game was historically improbable: Wild entered with a 59.1% kill rate — the worst by any team that advanced past Round 1 since the league began charting in 1978. Tonight: 2-for-3, including 2-for-2 in the first period with Wallstedt making 4 saves. The MacKinnon PP goal in the second came on contested goalie interference (Daemon Hunt pushing Landeskog into Wallstedt). The Wild didn't challenge.
  • The instant momentum reclaim: 20 seconds after MacKinnon's PP goal cut it 3–1, Faber's deflection (off a Tarasenko shot during a delayed penalty) made it 4–1. The Wild didn't let Colorado's only goal become anything. Same trait Carolina demonstrated against Philly today.
  • The Wild's 39–25 hits advantage was the structural truth of the night — the Foligno brothers combined for 11. Minnesota was the more physical team, the harder team to play against, and (for one night) the more dangerous team. The Athletic's framing: "Looking much more like themselves and playing with a Game 7 mentality."
  • Wallstedt 34 saves after sitting Game 2 — including a critical glove save on a Parker Kelly partial breakaway with the game still 0-0. The puck hit the post and went wide. If that goes in, the entire Wild emotional map of this night looks different.
  • The historical parallel worth knowing: The Wild's lone series win when trailing 2-0 came in 2014 — against Colorado, in seven games. Same matchup. Same situation. 12 years apart. Not destiny. Editorial coincidence with story potential.

What This Was
The Wild finally playing the game they're built for. Heavy. Physical. Forecheck pressure. Wallstedt comfortable. Special teams winning. Their Round 1 identity from the Dallas series finally made it to Round 2. Two games of getting overwhelmed by Colorado's pace turned into a game where Minnesota imposed pace instead. The Wild are now what they always were — a structural Tier 2 team capable of a Tier 1 game on the right night.
What Colorado Faces
For the first time all postseason, Colorado has a real problem. Wedgewood's structural reliability is now a question; Bednar may turn to Blackwood for Game 4. The 6-0 streak that put them in 30-year company is over. Manson is still out. Landeskog moved up to the top line, didn't finish. Colorado is still the better team in this series. They're now a meaningfully more vulnerable better team.
The Norris Confrontation
Day 2's preview framing was Makar vs Hughes — whichever defenseman dictated would dictate the series. Through Game 1, Makar dominated (2G+1A on a leg-injury return). Through Game 2, Makar stayed productive. Tonight, Hughes scored on the 4-on-3 PP and quarterbacked Minnesota's transition game. The confrontation has now seen both Norris-tier defensemen produce series-deciding moments — which is exactly the matchup the publication previewed Sunday.
Series Outlook · Updated
Series goes 6 or 7 now, real read. Minnesota proved they can play a Colorado-level game. Whether they can repeat that effort on Monday is the question — Game 3 was their season-on-the-line response, and emotional games are hard to replicate. If Eriksson Ek returns Monday, the matchup math improves further. If Wedgewood gets swapped for Blackwood, Colorado's structural anchor changes. The series the publication wrote off two days ago is now the most analytically interesting in the bracket.

Sunday's slate

  • BUF @ MTL Game 3 — 4 PM ET, Bell Centre. Series tied 1–1 after Montreal's Day 8 5–1 statement. Bell Centre delivered Montreal three home wins against Tampa in Round 1. If the alternating-wins pattern continues, Buffalo wins Sunday and the structural read tilts back.   • VGK @ ANA Game 4 — 9:30 PM ET, Honda Center. Vegas leads 2–1 after Marner's hat trick. The Stone injury status is the wildcard the publication flagged Day 8 — if he plays Sunday and produces, Vegas pushes for a 3–1 lead heading home. If he sits and Anaheim's PP finally cracks Vegas's PK, the series resets at 2–2. Anaheim's PP is 0-for-11 in the series; that has to change.
  • Colorado-Minnesota Game 4 is Monday in St. Paul. If Bednar starts Blackwood, that's the Wedgewood story closing. If he starts Wedgewood, that's Bednar betting on the regular-season Jennings winner over the rookie Wallstedt. Either choice is a meaningful structural read on where Colorado thinks the series is.
  • The bracket reality: Three series remain undecided. Carolina is already through. By Sunday night, two of the remaining three could break open meaningfully.
The Take
One day. Two streaks. One survived; one didn't. Carolina swept Philadelphia at 3 PM Eastern and became the first team through to a Conference Final at 8–0 — the second team to start a postseason that way in 30 years. Six hours later, Colorado's matching 6–0 streak ended at the hands of a Wild team that came in with the worst PK in 30+ years to advance past Round 1. The "two undefeated teams" framing the publication has been tracking since Day 4 just collapsed into one. The other seven teams have real problems: Colorado has a goaltending question, Vegas has a Stone injury, Anaheim has a 0-for-11 power play, Buffalo has a Lyon question, Montreal has a top-line drought, Minnesota has a still-thin margin, and Philadelphia is going home. This is what Round 2 was supposed to be: every team except one playing real hockey.
