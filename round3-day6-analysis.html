The Habs first SOG came at 8:07 of the 1st — Carolina had 6 by then. By the time the Bell Centre had finished settling in, Montreal had seven failed zone exits and a three-goal deficit. § 01

Carolina's most complete game.

Forecheck. Veterans. Depth. Goaltending. Defensive pairing performance. Special teams. Every piece of Carolina's structural identity arrived simultaneously in one game — at the perfect leverage moment of the series.

Round 3 · ECF · Game 4  ·  CAR Leads 3–1

Carolina at Montreal

Series Standing
Hurricanes 3 — Canadiens 1
  • Carolina 4, Montreal 0. CAR scorers: Aho (PP, 15:00 of 1st, cross-ice one-timer off Bolduc hooking call), Staal (net-front battle vs Anderson, banked off skate from Miller pass), Stankoven (2-on-1 capper, Blake feed, his first point of the series), Svechnikov (EN late). Carolina leads series 3-1. Game 5 Friday in Raleigh — Carolina can clinch.
  • The 1st-period burst was structural devastation. 3 goals in 2:47. All three Carolina top centers (Aho, Staal, Stankoven) tallied in succession. 17:46 into the game it was 3-0 — Carolina's first 3-goal lead of the series. Montreal had a massive hole, and unlike the Habs' famous comebacks in Tampa and Buffalo, Carolina doesn't give holes back.
  • The structural truth that defined Game 4: Montreal had ONE shot on goal in 32:57 of game time (from the 3rd period of Game 3 through the first 8 minutes of Game 4). That's more than half a game's worth of game time with one shot. The publication has never seen a stat like that against a competitive Conference Final opponent. Carolina's forecheck broke Montreal's puck-possession identity entirely.
  • The 7-failed-exits stat. By Basu's count, Montreal had 7 failed zone exits in the opening 8 minutes alone. That's a structural collapse, not a slump. Carolina retrieved, recycled, attacked, and forced Montreal into hesitation decisions. Per Basu: "The Canadiens' inability to clear the puck from their own zone under the relentless Hurricanes forecheck was going to be the biggest adjustment we would see in Game 4. Or so we thought."
  • The Aho PP goal followed a questionable Bolduc hooking call. Carolina's top PP unit made very short work of it — cross-ice one-timer with 5 min left in the opening frame. Carolina entered the game with their PP working at higher than expected efficiency this postseason; tonight they capitalized when given the chance.
  • The Staal goal was the editorial detail. Net-front battle with Josh Anderson. Miller carried the puck from blue line to goal line on the wide-side, drawing Dobes' attention before banking the puck off Staal's skate back toward the Canadiens' net. Per Gentille: "It was a slick play, the sort of thing Miller does with some degree of regularity, and it certainly came at a crucial moment for the Hurricanes."
  • The Stankoven 2-on-1 was the symbolic moment. Capped by a Blake feed. Stankoven's FIRST point of the series. The publication's MTL-BUF/COL-MIN recap analyses noted that the Stankoven-Blake-Hall line generated 58% of Carolina's R1-R2 goals — and was structurally invisible through ECF Games 1-2. Tonight Stankoven arrived. Per his postgame: "Sometimes you gotta find the body blows to get to the head."
  • The St. Louis line shuffle came too late. Down 3-0 to start the 2nd, St. Louis shuffled all 4 forward lines: Newhook with Suzuki/Caufield, Bolduc with Danault/Anderson, Slafkovsky with Demidov. Per Basu: "Considering the Canadiens put 10 shots on goal in the second period alone after putting up 13 and 12 in the previous two games, which each went to overtime, it is a fair question why he began the game with the same line combinations." Game 4 might have been different had the Canadiens started this way — but the emotional damage was already done.
  • The Bell Centre chant is the editorial moment of the series. "Shoot the puck! Shoot the puck!" from Habs fans in their own building with under 7 minutes left in regulation. Per St. Louis postgame: "It's not fun to hear that. But they're not wrong." A head coach acknowledging his crowd's frustration was justified. That's the structural truth of the series.
  • Andersen had a remarkably easy night. 18 saves on Montreal's 18 shots. The publication's Day 2 ("Counter") flagged Andersen's career 4-10 conference final record. After Game 1's rust-related troubles, he settled and Carolina protected him. Tonight Carolina protected him completely — Montreal didn't manage a single SOG in the 3rd period until 2:55 remained.
  • Carolina shots: 43. Montreal shots: 18. The 25-shot differential is the structural definition of dominance. Per Gentille: "It might have been the strongest game of the postseason for the Hurricanes."

§ 02

Montreal's structural collapse.

The Habs had ONE shot on goal across 32:57 of game time. Seven failed zone exits in the opening 8 minutes. The Bell Centre crowd chanted "Shoot the puck!" in their own building. St. Louis: "They're not wrong."

Montreal · Structural Collapse · 32:57 Window

The "Shoot the puck" chant.

The Bell Centre crowd chanted "Shoot the puck!" with under 7 minutes left in regulation. Montreal had ZERO shots on goal in the 3rd period until 2:55 remained. St. Louis postgame: "It's not fun to hear that. But they're not wrong." A head coach acknowledging his crowd's frustration was justified — that's the structural editorial moment of the series.

The longer-window stat is more damning: Going back to the 3rd period of Game 3 (from a Dobson SOG at 10:06), Montreal had ONE shot on goal across 32:57 of game time. More than half a game's worth. Against a Conference Final opponent. Carolina's forecheck broke Montreal's puck-possession identity completely.

The 7 failed zone exits in the opening 8 minutes is the structural definition of collapse. Per Basu: "The Canadiens' inability to clear the puck from their own zone under the relentless Hurricanes forecheck was going to be the biggest adjustment we would see in Game 4. Or so we thought." The adjustment never came.

The 2nd-period line shuffle (Newhook with Suzuki/Caufield; Bolduc with Danault/Anderson) generated 10 SOG — but came too late. The damage was done in the 1st period. Once Carolina established the lead, structural collapse compounded with emotional collapse.

Jakub Dobes · 39 saves · Birthday Game · The Only Reason

Dobes was the only reason it wasn't 7-0.

39 saves on Carolina's 43 shots. Multiple breakaway stops — including Jackson Blake and Seth Jarvis. Multiple huge 2nd-period saves. The 25-year-old rookie celebrated his birthday by being the only Hab who showed up structurally.

The publication has tracked Dobes since Round 2 — his Game 7 OT win vs Buffalo, his 40+ saves in ECF Game 1, his survival in ECF Game 2's 11-3 shot deluge. Tonight may have been his best performance of the postseason despite Montreal losing 4-0. Per Dobes postgame: "I feel like we've been underdogs the whole season, the whole playoffs. So we are a super underdog right now. We'll go to Carolina and try to win Game 5, come back and try to win (Game 6), and hopefully we'll get to Game 7."

That's belief. But belief without the structural underpinning becomes the wrong kind of confidence — Montreal's exits aren't getting fixed by belief. The Habs need cleaner puck support, simpler exits, less east-west play, and more emotional composure. None of that came tonight.

§ 03

K'Andre Miller emerges.

The offseason acquisition from the New York Rangers may be the structural piece that makes Carolina Cup Final-ready. Elite skating. Elite reach. Erases scoring chances by all 3 of Carolina's postseason opponents.

K'Andre Miller · CAR · Primary Assist on Staal Goal

The acquisition that completed Carolina's identity.

Carolina acquired K'Andre Miller from the New York Rangers in the offseason for first- and second-round picks and defenseman Scott Morrow. Miller, 26, had flashed potential with the Rangers but also struggled at times, playing for three coaching staffs and frequently being deployed in matchup minutes with mismatched partners. Carolina saw a player who could thrive in their pressure ecosystem.

Tonight's Staal goal: Miller carried the puck from the blue line to the goal line, going wide on Danault. That drew Dobes' attention away from the traffic in the crease — Miller, with plenty of space, banked the puck off Staal's skate back toward the Canadiens' net. Per Gentille: "It was a slick play, the sort of thing Miller does with some degree of regularity."

Per Aho: "He kind of does it all. He skates really well. He has really good offensive instincts as well. He's been a great player for us."

Per the Athletic: "his skating and reach are elite, and he's used both to regularly erase scoring chances by all three of Carolina's postseason opponents, including several by Montreal throughout Game 4." That's a player who has elevated Carolina's structural ceiling. If Carolina meets Vegas in the Cup Final, Miller's defensive activation against Vegas's transition forwards (Marner, Eichel, Dorofeyev) becomes one of the key matchup variables.

Carolina's depth identity in full

  • All three top centers scored within 2:47. Aho (1st PP unit), Staal (depth/leadership C), Stankoven (formerly the line that generated 58% of CAR R1-R2 goals). Carolina rolls 4 lines — the depth identity that wins long playoff series.
  • Ehlers continues to deliver since the Day 4 emergence. 2 assists tonight (Aho's PP goal, Staal's net-front goal). 3 G + 2 A in his last 2 games. The $8.5M FA acquisition the publication had been tracking as an underperformer through Day 2 has now structurally arrived. He's the missing offensive game-breaker.
  • Andersen had his easiest night of the postseason. 18 saves on 18 shots. The Day 2 framing of his 4-10 career conference final record now reads differently — he didn't need to win games, Carolina needed to protect him. They did.
  • The 4-line depth is the Cup Final argument: Per the analyst: "Not just stars dominating. The Staal line, Robinson line, Stankoven line all tilted the ice. That means Montreal cannot simply shut down one line. Carolina's pressure comes everywhere." That depth is exactly the kind of identity Vegas just used to demolish Colorado. The Cup Final matchup (if Carolina closes Friday) becomes the deepest-team-wins debate.
§ 04

Vegas waits.

Carolina is one win from the Stanley Cup Final. If they close Game 5 Friday in Raleigh, the publication's Day 5 power rankings prediction from way back in early Round 2 produces its second confirmed call: Vegas vs Carolina in the Stanley Cup Final.

· Stanley Cup Final Preview · If CAR Closes ·

Vegas vs Carolina · the matchup the publication called.

The publication's Day 5 Round 2 power rankings ("Maturity") predicted Carolina + Colorado + Vegas + Montreal as the Final Four. Colorado was wrong — but the Vegas + Carolina + Montreal calls have held. If Carolina closes Friday, the Cup Final is exactly the matchup the publication has been pointing to since the COL-MIN Series Recap shifted the Cup conversation to a 3-team race (CAR + COL + VGK).

The structural matchup is fascinating: Two of the deepest 4-line teams left. Two veteran rosters. Two elite-tier goaltenders (Hart for Vegas, Andersen for Carolina). Two coaching identities built on territorial pressure and emotional composure. This isn't the kind of Cup Final where one team has talent and the other has structure — both teams have both. It's a matchup that could be decided by line-matching, special teams, or one goaltender stealing a game.

The Marner question: Marner's 21 playoff points (NHL lead, career high) earned him the publication's "Toronto-to-Vegas career renaissance" framing. Carolina's hard-match defensive identity (which neutralized the Habs' Suzuki line in Games 2-4) becomes the structural test of whether Marner's run continues into the Cup Final or whether Carolina is the matchup that finally answers Vegas's top line.

The K'Andre Miller variable: Miller's defensive activation against Vegas's transition forwards is the kind of structural answer Colorado tried to provide without Makar and couldn't. If Miller plays the matchup-killer role Carolina acquired him for, the Cup Final tilts toward Carolina. If Vegas's depth (Howden, Dorofeyev, Hertl) overwhelms Carolina's matchup math, Vegas's "Inevitability" thesis from the WCF Recap delivers a Cup.

But first Carolina has to close Friday. Montreal's belief identity has survived elimination twice this postseason (Game 7 OT wins in Tampa and Buffalo). Per Stankoven: "That fourth game to win is always the toughest." Carolina's Game 5 Friday in Raleigh is now the most important game of their playoff run.
