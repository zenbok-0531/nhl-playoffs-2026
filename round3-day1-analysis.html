The Blueprint.

Vegas survived Colorado's opening storm, completely took over the middle 25 minutes, and walked out of Denver with home-ice advantage. Coghlan opened the scoring with his first NHL goal since December 17, 2021 — 1,615 days ago. Dorofeyev scored his NHL-leading 10th playoff goal off a Marner feed. Vegas may have just shown the rest of the league how to frustrate the Cup favorite.

Round 3 began the way Round 2 ended — with Vegas controlling the moments that mattered while Colorado played the higher-event hockey. The Avs dominated the opening 5 minutes, forcing Vegas to scramble out of its own end. Then Vegas weathered it, settled the game, and from the middle of the second period onward, completely controlled the structural battle. Dylan Coghlan — a 28-year-old defenseman who'd spent most of this season in the AHL and hadn't scored an NHL goal since December 2021 — opened scoring at 12:29 of the second. Less than 4 minutes later, Dorofeyev scored his NHL-leading 10th playoff goal off a Marner feed. Less than 5 minutes into the third, Howden batted his 9th of the playoffs out of the air after a Vegas penalty kill ended. The Avs pushed late — Nichushkin and Landeskog cut it to 3-2 with 2:21 remaining — but Dowd's empty-netter sealed it. Vegas: home-ice advantage in Denver. The Athletic called this "a composure win more than a skill win." That's the perfect framing for the publication's "precision team" identity carrying forward from Round 2.

§ 01

Vegas found the blueprint.

Survive the opening storm. Compress middle ice. Block shots (22). Force one-and-dones. Punish turnovers. Stay emotionally calm. That's the structural identity Vegas brought into Game 1 — and it worked against the Cup favorite in their own building.

Round 3 · WCF · Game 1  ·  VGK Leads 1–0

Vegas at Colorado

Series Standing
Golden Knights 1 — Avalanche 0
  • Vegas 4, Colorado 2. VGK scorers: Coghlan (1st NHL goal in 4 years, 5-hole on Wedgewood), Dorofeyev (PP, off Marner cross-seam feed), Howden (batted out of air post-PK), Dowd (EN). COL: Nichushkin (between his own legs off a Colton feed), Landeskog (PP, off a MacKinnon dance through McNabb). VGK takes home-ice advantage. Game 2 Friday in Denver.
  • The structural truth: The opening 5 minutes were everything Colorado is supposed to be. Heavy forecheck, swarming pressure, neutral-zone speed. Vegas was scrambling. And then Vegas settled. Hart made saves. The neutral zone tightened. The middle ice compressed. Colorado's transition game stopped functioning by the late first period.
  • The middle 25 minutes flipped the game. From late 1st through much of the 3rd: Vegas tilted the ice, generated cleaner entries, controlled rebounds, and won leverage moments. Per the analyst: "That's where the series may actually swing — the 25-minute stretch where playoff games are usually decided."
  • 22 blocked shots from Vegas. Colorado's first-two-period offense: 3 of their best chances came from fourth-line winger Logan O'Connor — not what you want from a team with MacKinnon, Necas, and the deepest forward room in the league. Per Bednar postgame: "I didn't love our puck play tonight — like, our execution coming out of our zone, through the neutral zone a little bit, even O-zone play. We gave them a handful of odd-man rushes that came off our turnovers and missed execution."
  • The Coghlan opener is editorial gold. 1,615 days since his last NHL goal (December 17, 2021). Spent most of this season with the Henderson Silver Knights (AHL). Played only 3 regular-season NHL games. Inserted into the Vegas lineup during the Anaheim series, replacing Kaedan Korczak. Per Tortorella: "I'm happy for him, because he's plugged along, plugged along. With his background, it's a huge goal for us. He's an easy guy to pull for."
  • Dorofeyev's 10th playoff goal — NHL-leading. Marner cross-seam feed, Dorofeyev finish on the PP. The 2nd goal in 4 minutes that broke the game open. The Marner-Dorofeyev partnership the publication has been tracking since Day 8 is now structurally the most productive forward pairing in the NHL playoffs.
  • The Howden goal is becoming routine. 9th playoff goal — 2nd in NHL behind only Dorofeyev. Came on a 2-on-1 with Hutton seconds after a Vegas PK ended. Wedgewood blocked Hutton's shot; Howden knocked the puck out of the air with his glove and tapped it across the goal line. Howden had 12 goals in the entire regular season. He has 9 in 14 playoff games. The publication has been tracking him since Day 10 — he is now a structural certainty as an X-factor.
  • Colorado's late push showed why they're dangerous. Nichushkin at 5:53 of the third (between-the-legs finish off a Colton feed). Landeskog PP goal at 17:39 (MacKinnon dance through McNabb, backdoor feed). That's the explosive offense that Colorado can still produce on demand. But the structural problem stood: too little, too late, against a team that doesn't panic.
  • Hart was the difference at the right moments. 36 saves, including a terrific stop on a Nazem Kadri PP shot at the first-period horn. Per the Athletic: "Vegas never needed him to steal games constantly. They needed stability. He gave them exactly that." Same framing the publication used after the Anaheim series.
  • Special teams: Vegas won the structural battle. Dorofeyev PP goal. Killed off both Colorado PPs (Howden's goal came moments after the kill had ended). Vegas has now played opponents to a 4-4 draw when short-handed in this postseason. Colorado entered with the 27th-best PP in the regular season; they were scoring at 25% in Rounds 1-2 only against teams that couldn't structurally defend them. Vegas can.
  • Wedgewood started for Colorado — the call Bednar made from the COL-MIN Series Recap's "Wedgewood-vs-Blackwood question." Tight early (stopped Kolesar breakaway, kicked aside Hertl shot), but allowed 3 through 40 minutes. The Blackwood call could come Friday.

What This Was
The first time Colorado has looked genuinely uncomfortable for extended stretches in this postseason. Vegas didn't out-skate them. Vegas didn't out-talent them. Vegas simply absorbed Colorado's opening push, tightened the neutral zone, blocked 22 shots, and converted the turnovers they generated. That's the structural identity the publication has been calling "precision hockey" since the VGK-ANA Series Recap. Carolina now has it confirmed against a team many had as the structural Cup favorite.
The Blueprint Question
Per the analyst: "You cannot out-skate Colorado consistently. You cannot out-talent Colorado consistently. But Vegas may be proving you can FRUSTRATE Colorado structurally." How? Compress middle ice. Force dump-ins. Block lanes. Punish turnovers. Stay emotionally calm. That's exactly what happened after the opening 5-minute stretch. If Vegas can replicate this for 3 more wins, the WCF flips from "Cup Final preview" to "Cup Final-tier upset path."
Colorado's Reality
Colorado still showed why they're dangerous. Down 3-0 in the third, they pushed. The Nichushkin goal woke up the building. MacKinnon set up Landeskog's PP goal to make it 3-2 with 2:21 left. That's the explosive offense that can change a game on any shift. But the structural problem is real: when Vegas plays its best, Colorado's transition game stops functioning, and Colorado is currently without the player (Makar) who most reliably restarts it. Bednar's pregame "lot of work and a lot of sleep" comment may not have been about the right kind of work — the work needed is structural adjustment, and that has to happen between Game 1 and Game 2 Friday.
Series Outlook · Updated
Vegas leads 1-0 with home ice advantage. Game 2 Friday in Denver. The Makar return timeline is the biggest structural variable. If Makar is back for Game 2, Colorado gets its Norris-tier driver back and the structural ceiling rises immediately. If Makar misses another game, Vegas's blueprint just got another test run. The Stone return is more uncertain — he skated optionally pregame but Tortorella's commitment to his current lineup suggests he won't rush him back. Vegas can wait. Colorado cannot.
§ 02

Missing stars.

Both teams without their best player. Makar's absence cost Colorado more visibly than Stone's cost Vegas — but the rest of this series may turn on which one returns first.

Colorado · Cale Makar · OUT

Norris-tier absence.

Left Round 2 Game 5 with a right-arm injury after a late collision with Mats Zuccarello. Skated on his own pregame Wednesday. Per Bednar postgame: "Day-to-day. I expect him to return at some point."

Replacement: Jack Ahcan got the call for his 3rd career playoff appearance. Played fewest D-minutes of any Av. On ice for Vegas's opening goal. Devon Toews moved up to top PP. Manson moved up to top PK pair.

The structural cost: less clean zone exits, less transition support, less offensive activation on the back end, dynamically weaker PP. Colorado's neutral-zone game suffered all night.

Status · Day-to-Day · Possible Game 2
Vegas · Mark Stone · OUT

Captain's absence.

Hasn't played since leaving Round 2 Game 3 against Anaheim. Skated in Wednesday's optional morning skate at Ball Arena. Did not dress for Game 1.

His postseason: 3G, 4A in 9 games before the injury. Selke-caliber two-way center.

The structural reality: Vegas is now 5-1 without Stone in these playoffs (won 4 of 5 vs ANA and tonight). Howden, Dorofeyev, Hertl, Karlsson, and Saad have absorbed his minutes. Per Kolesar pregame: "It's always a next-man-up mentality. With guys like him, it's hard to do that, but guys took it upon themselves last series — like Mitch and Pavel — pulled their socks up."

Status · Unconfirmed return timeline

The structural asymmetry: Vegas has been winning without Stone for 6 straight playoff games and 6 of 8 total. Colorado has only been without Makar for one game — and they lost it on home ice. Vegas has built a depth identity that absorbs star absence. Colorado has built a star-driven identity that depends on Makar being there. If both stars return, the matchup question reverses. If Stone returns and Makar doesn't, the WCF could end fast.

The Take
Vegas didn't beat Colorado with talent tonight. They beat them by doing exactly what the publication's Round 2 thesis predicted they would do at the highest level: absorb pressure longer than the opponent could apply it, frustrate the structural identity of the better team, and convert the turnovers that pressure-resistant hockey produces. Coghlan — a 28-year-old AHL call-up who hadn't scored in 1,615 days — opened the scoring. Dorofeyev — the NHL playoff goal leader — added a Marner PP goal moments later. Howden — who already tied Gretzky's SHG record and has 9 playoff goals — knocked one out of the air post-PK. That's structural depth, leverage execution, and elite hockey IQ across the lineup. The Athletic called this game's framing "a composure win more than a skill win." The publication agrees, and adds: this may be the moment the Cup conversation stopped being "Colorado vs Carolina with Vegas as a 3rd option" and started being "Carolina vs Vegas with Colorado as a 3rd option." Game 2 Friday will tell us if tonight was a blueprint or a one-off.
