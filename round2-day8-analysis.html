Series tied 1–1 East. Vegas leads 2–1 West. Round 2 keeps teaching: identity is provisional, every game.

Day 6's thesis was that identity isn't permanent in Round 2. Tonight delivered the clearest possible proof, twice. Montreal — humbled by Buffalo's speed in Game 1 — produced what may have been their best period of the season en route to a 5–1 win. Vegas — pushed around by Anaheim's territorial pressure in Game 2 — answered with a 6–2 rout led by a Marner natural hat trick that vaulted him to the NHL playoff scoring lead. Two losing teams from Round 2's earlier inversions just took back environmental control of their respective series. The bracket has now seen seven different "this team is in control" moments since Round 2 started. Carolina and Colorado are still the only teams to keep their identity all postseason.

§ 01

Montreal dictated.

This wasn't margin hockey. Per Sportsnaut: "In every metric that mattered, the Canadiens were the better team." That's a different kind of Montreal win than what got them past Tampa — and a different problem for Buffalo than the one they thought they were facing.

Round 2 · Series 03 · East  ·  The Reply

Montreal at Buffalo

Series Standing
Sabres 1 — Canadiens 1
  • Montreal 5, Buffalo 1. Newhook scored at 1:36 of the first to flip Game 1's script — Krebs turnover in Buffalo's zone, Hutson and Guhle assisting, Newhook sneaking it under Lyon's blocker. Matheson made it 2–0 just 2:51 later on a floater from the left point. The 2–0 first-period start that won Buffalo Game 1 belonged to Montreal tonight.
  • The structural truth from the first period: Montreal owned a 7–0 high-danger chance advantage at 5v5. Sportsnaut's read: "It may even have been Montreal's best period of the season." Not the playoffs — the season. This wasn't survival hockey extracted from 9 shots like Game 7 against Tampa. This was Montreal playing the game on their terms.
  • Buffalo's power play was the unit that defined Game 1 — 2-for-3 with the Byram top-corner snipe leading the way. Tonight: 0-for-5. The Sabres are now 3-for-32 in the playoffs (9.4%). Newhook's second goal came 4 seconds after Buffalo's PP failed on a Carrier hook. The momentum reversal couldn't have been more direct.
  • Lyon's slide continues. 4 goals on 27 shots tonight. Per AP: he'd already given up "seven goals in six-plus appearances since replacing Luukkonen" — 11 in his last 7-plus games now. The goalie change that won Buffalo Round 1 isn't holding up under Round 2 pressure.
  • Montreal's depth scoring keeps overcoming a quiet top line. Caufield extended his point drought to 5 games. Slafkovský has 1 assist in his last 8. And it doesn't matter — because Newhook (3 goals in playoffs), Matheson, Carrier, Suzuki (empty-netter), and the rest keep producing. Montreal's path to wins doesn't require the stars; it just needs the depth to activate. So far, every game.
  • The alternating-wins pattern is now a thing: Montreal's last 9 playoff games have alternated W-L-W-L through the Tampa series and now into Round 2. Game 1 BUF, Game 2 MTL. The Habs don't string wins together. They counter-punch every game. That's not ideal for a series-winning identity. But it's working.

What This Was
A complete environmental flip. Game 1 was Buffalo's structural game; Game 2 was Montreal's. Buffalo's defense corps that activated into the rush in Game 1 spent Game 2 chasing the play. The team that imposed pace and territory won — same lesson as Day 6, opposite winners. And critically: Montreal didn't beat Buffalo by playing Buffalo's game. They beat them by reasserting their own game with more conviction.
What This Says
Montreal isn't a one-trick playoff team. They survived structural pressure against Tampa. Tonight they dominated 5v5 against a faster opponent on the road. That's two distinct competitive identities the Habs can deploy. The publication's Day 5 ranking placed Montreal at #4 because they could only win one way (margins). Tonight earned them the upgrade. Whether the upgrade holds depends on Game 3 Sunday — but the ceiling case for Montreal just got demonstrably higher.
What Buffalo Faces
Two consecutive sloppy losses-on-home-ice patterns are emerging. Tuch's puck rolled off his stick on a chance. Quinn shot over the net alone in the slot. Tage Thompson lost his balance leading directly to Carrier's 4–1 dagger. Benson whiffed on a tip-in. That's not bad luck — that's a team rattled by a faster, more committed opponent. Buffalo's PP needs to recover or the structural advantage that won them Game 1 is gone for good.
Series Outlook · Updated
Series even, true 7-game read now. Bell Centre awaits Sunday for Game 3 — the same building that delivered Montreal three wins in the Tampa series. Buffalo dropped to 2–3 at home in this postseason. Game 3 in Montreal is the most important game of the series. If Montreal wins, the structural read tilts decisively; if Buffalo steals one in Bell Centre, the series resets again. Round 2's youngest, fastest, and least-expected series is now also its most volatile.
§ 02

Marner took over.

Six points in the previous two games combined; four points tonight including a natural hat trick. Marner has more goals in 9 playoff games with Vegas than in any single postseason in his 9 years in Toronto. The trade-deadline acquisition is now leading the playoffs in scoring.

Round 2 · Series 04 · West  ·  The Hat Trick

Vegas at Anaheim

Series Standing
Knights 2 — Ducks 1
  • Vegas 6, Anaheim 2. Theodore opened scoring 1:06 into the first off an Eichel feed. McNabb scored shorthanded at 12:13 — Marner picked off an Anaheim PP pass and threaded one back to McNabb in the left circle. Marner's PP goal with 5 seconds left in the first made it 3–0. Vegas led 5–0 in the second period before Anaheim got on the board.
  • Marner's first career playoff hat trick was structural, not opportunistic. Goals 1, 2, and 3 came in succession across the first and second periods — a PP rebound finish, a rush chance from Theodore on the weak side, and a sharp-angle short-side finish on Husso. Plus a primary assist on McNabb's SHG. 4 points. NHL postseason career-high. "He's a hell of a hockey player," Tortorella said. "We feel we're very fortunate to have him on this team."
  • The Toronto-Vegas career arc is the storyline: Marner has 6 goals in 9 playoff games with Vegas — already more than his most goals in any single postseason across 9 years in Toronto. 13 points (6G+7A) leads all NHL skaters this postseason. He's also got 3 short-handed assists, all primary, on Vegas's 3 SHGs in the playoffs. The trade-deadline acquisition the publication has been tracking since Round 1 is now the playoff scoring leader.
  • Anaheim's PP collapse continued and accelerated: 0-for-11 in the series. They were 8-for-16 (50%) in Round 1 against Edmonton. The structural identity that powered them past the Oilers has now disappeared for three straight games. Killorn, postgame: "If we're not going to score, we have to make sure we get some momentum. At least getting some shots." They're not even doing that.
  • Dostal pulled after the first period. 3 goals on 8 shots, including the second goal off McNabb (a 6-goal-in-113-game career playoff scorer) and the 5-second-left PP goal that Dostal couldn't secure on the rebound. Husso came in for the 2nd and 3rd. Quenneville on whether Dostal starts Game 4: "We'll see. We'll see." Goalie controversy in Anaheim now real.
  • Hart was steady — 30 of 32. Going back to Game 6 of Round 1, Hart has now strung four straight strong games together (.935 SV% in Games 1–2 against Anaheim). Vegas's structural advantage at goalie isn't wavering. Anaheim's is suddenly a question.
  • The Stone injury concern: Vegas captain pulled up on a first-period PP, went straight to the room. Came back briefly in the second, slammed his stick during a TV timeout, didn't return. Reaching for groin area on his way off. Stone has missed 22 games this season already with two separate injuries. Tortorella postgame: no update. Vegas's depth is real, but losing Stone for any meaningful stretch would change the series math.

What This Was
Vegas's best performance of the series, on the road, after the publication had named home-ice flipped to Anaheim 48 hours ago. 5–0 lead in the second period. The kind of statement game that quiets a series. Per Tortorella: "I thought we entered the series tonight, playing our game a little more than we have." Translation: the previous two games weren't us. Tonight was. Now we have to keep it up.
The Marner Storyline
The most analytically rich human-interest story of the playoffs. Marner spent 9 years in Toronto being criticized for "not showing up in the playoffs." Six goals through 9 games in his first Vegas postseason has already exceeded his single-postseason career high in Toronto. He's now leading the NHL in playoff scoring, has primary assists on every Vegas SHG, and just had his first career playoff hat trick. The career-arc redemption narrative writes itself — and the publication should let it.
What Anaheim Faces
Two structural problems went from "concerns" to "crises" tonight. The PP that powered them past Edmonton is now 0-for-11 in this series. Their starting goalie is unreliable enough to get pulled in a Game 3 their season was riding on. Plus Sennecke is the only consistent offensive contributor through three games. Quenneville's "let's get ready to go to war" quote is right — but the Ducks need answers on both special teams and goaltending in 48 hours.
Series Outlook · Updated
Knights in 6 now lean. Vegas reasserted environmental control in Anaheim's building, and the structural matchups (PP, goaltending) have meaningfully shifted in their favor. Game 4 Sunday at Honda Center is Anaheim's last realistic chance to even the series before heading back to Vegas down 3–1. The Stone injury status is the wildcard — if Vegas loses him for the rest of Round 2, the series math gets meaningfully tighter for Anaheim.
§ 03

Newhook & Hutson.

Two names that have quietly defined Montreal's postseason finally got their proper Day 8 framing. The Game 7 hero who keeps scoring. The Calder finalist who keeps driving play.

Playoff Hero · Newhook

The Newfoundlander has scored every game-changing goal of Montreal's playoff run.

Game 7 vs Tampa — series-winning goal at 11:07 of the third in a 2–1 game where Montreal got outshot 29–9. Tonight — opener at 1:36 of the first to flip Game 1's script, plus the dagger 4:47 into the second period. 3 goals in the playoffs, every one of them changed the trajectory of the game it was scored in. Newhook isn't Montreal's leading scorer (Suzuki, Caufield) or the offensive face (Demidov). He's becoming something more valuable — the guy who scores when it actually matters. The Athletic-style human-interest narrative writes itself: 25-year-old depth winger acquired from Colorado in 2023 keeps finding the moment. Postgame editor Marc Dumont titled his recap section "He's The B'y" — Newfoundland slang for the local hero. That's exactly what Newhook has become.

Quiet Driver · Hutson

The Calder finalist had "an absolutely fantastic outing" — and Montreal's first period was their best of the season.

Lane Hutson assisted on Newhook's opener. More importantly: he drove the play that produced 7–0 high-danger chances in the first period. Hutson finished his rookie season as a Calder Trophy finalist alongside Demidov, and his postseason has been quietly excellent — a 22-year-old running Montreal's puck-moving from the back end. The publication has been tracking Demidov as the marquee rookie storyline. Hutson is the structural rookie storyline. Tonight he ran the show. Combined with Dobson's return from his hand fracture (now playing through a "massive hit" he absorbed on Newhook's third goal), Montreal's defense corps is now meaningfully better than Buffalo's — which is the inverse of how Game 1 looked.

§ 04

Saturday's slate.

Two Game 4s tomorrow that could break the bracket open. Carolina trying to sweep. Colorado trying to take a stranglehold. The two undefeated teams aiming to extend their streaks against teams desperate to keep their seasons alive.

  • Carolina @ Philadelphia Game 4 — 3 PM ET. Carolina goes for the sweep. An 8–0 postseason start would put them in even more rarefied company than the 7–0 club they joined Thursday. Ehlers has been "showing a little different flair" per NHL.com — Carolina's depth scoring continues to activate. The publication's Day 7 framing of Carolina as "The Standard" gets its closeout test.
  • Colorado @ Minnesota Game 3 — 6 PM ET. The Wild are home for the first time this round, down 2–0, facing the publication's #1 ranked team. The Athletic's framing: "Wild penalty kill 'has to shape up' heading to Game 3." If Eriksson Ek returns from his Games 1–2 absence, Minnesota's checking line gets a meaningful upgrade against MacKinnon. If not, the Wild's path narrows further.
  • Two stakes — sweep vs survival. Carolina trying to make history. Minnesota trying to keep theirs alive. By Saturday evening, the Round 2 bracket may look fundamentally different than it does right now.
  • Sunday's slate matters too: Buffalo @ Montreal G3 (4 PM ET) and Vegas @ Anaheim G4 (9:30 PM ET). Both Game 4s in series that just had momentum reverse tonight. If the inversion pattern holds, Montreal and Anaheim win at home Sunday. If the structural reads from Day 8 hold, Buffalo and Vegas win on the road. Round 2 keeps proving it doesn't believe in patterns.
The Take
Round 2 is now the playoffs' most-honest round. Identity is provisional, environment is up for grabs every game, and the team that imposes pace wins until the other team imposes it back. Tonight Montreal flipped Buffalo's flip. Tonight Vegas flipped Anaheim's flip. Marner just took the playoff scoring lead in his first postseason out of Toronto. The two undefeated teams are still the only undefeated teams — because Carolina and Colorado have refused to let anyone flip them. That refusal is starting to look like the most championship trait in the bracket. Tomorrow they'll both try to extend it.
