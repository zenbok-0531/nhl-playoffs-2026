Montreal won a series they were structurally outplayed in. Tampa outshot Montreal 29–9 in Game 7, took 12 unanswered shots in a second period where Montreal didn't register one, and still went home. The Habs survived. They didn't dominate. They got 28 saves from rookie Jakub Dobeš, a Newhook goal at 11:07 of the third, and a Cooper line that names what happened more honestly than any analyst could: "Sometimes you win the game and not the score." Round 1 is finally complete. The Round 2 East-Atlantic matchup is set: Buffalo's pace and pressure against Montreal's resilience and goaltending. And tonight: Cale Makar meets Quinn Hughes in Denver, the most analytically interesting matchup the bracket has produced.

§ 01

Montreal survived.

Tampa played their best game of the series. They lost. Dobeš played his best game of the series. He won it. Sometimes playoff hockey is just that simple.

Round 1 · Series 05 · East  ·  Advanced

Montreal at Tampa

Series Final
Habs 4 — Lightning 3
  • Montreal 2, Tampa 1. Alex Newhook scored his first goal of the playoffs at 11:07 of the third to break a 1–1 tie. Suzuki opened the scoring in the first. Dominic James tied it for Tampa late in the second. Two-goal margin never happened all series — every game was one goal, four went to overtime.
  • The single most damning stat from Game 7: Tampa outshot Montreal 29–9. The Lightning controlled possession, generated chances, and dominated long stretches. Cooper said postgame: "We couldn't have played it any better. And it still wasn't good enough."
  • Dobeš was the difference. 28 saves on 29 shots. 11 saves in the second period when Tampa outshot Montreal 12–0. The rookie goaltender entered the series with a .916 save percentage and finished it as Montreal's best player by a meaningful margin. The Habs got the goalie performance every series-clinching team needs — except they got it from a rookie playing in his first Game 7.
  • The structural truth Tampa now lives with: fourth straight first-round exit. A team that was two wins from the Cup in 2022, and which entered this season with deep playoff run expectations, has now lost in Round 1 four years in a row. That's no longer a slump. That's a window question.
  • Hagel named the home-ice factor the publication has been tracking since Day 11: "At the end of the day, you lose three games at home, you're probably not going to win the series." Tampa lost Games 1, 2, and 4 at Amalie Arena — each in OT or close finishes. Montreal won 3 at Bell Centre. The home/road split was the series in five words.
  • St. Louis's coaching moment: after Tampa's brutal 12–0 second period, he came in "all fired up" between periods, per Suzuki. Montreal got out-chanced 12-0 in 20 minutes and didn't come apart. That's the kind of intermission moment young teams remember.

What This Was
A young team surviving a series they didn't structurally control. Montreal got dominated for stretches in nearly every game and won the series anyway. The trait that won it wasn't talent or experience — it was composure. The Habs didn't break in moments where young teams usually break. That's the maturation Tampa felt happening too late.
What This Says
Montreal earned the upset by playing margin hockey. Dobeš was elite when needed, the Bell Centre delivered home advantage, and the Suzuki-led leadership group held shape under pressure. They proved they can win a structurally lost series — that's a meaningful Round 2 trait. Whether they can win a series they're structurally favored in is the next question, and Buffalo will ask it loudly.
What Tampa Becomes
The same question that surrounded Edmonton three days ago now surrounds Tampa. Aging core. Four straight first-round exits. Cooper still in command but the structural patterns aren't changing. Tampa's Round 1 exit deserves a closer structural look — they've gone from two-time Cup champions to a team that can't get past Round 1 in four straight tries. That's a window question, and it's worth its own piece.
Round 2 Awaits
Montreal vs Buffalo. Two structurally similar teams — speed, depth, young cores — both on first real Round 2 runs in years. Buffalo's first series win since 2007. Montreal's first since 2021. This is going to be the most overlooked series of Round 2 and probably one of the most entertaining — neither team has ever beaten the other in playoff history. Buffalo gets two days off; Montreal earned a 7-game trip from Tampa. Series starts later this week.
§ 02

Tonight: Makar meets Hughes.

Two of the four best defensemen in the NHL meet at Ball Arena. The Norris confrontation isn't a side story — it's the structural question that decides this series.

Round 2 · Series 02 · West  ·  Game 1 Tonight

Minnesota at Colorado

Series Standing
Game 1 · Tonight at Ball Arena
Colorado · Norris Anchor

Cale Makar

  • 2024 Norris winner. Two-time finalist before that.
  • Round 1: Sweep of LA, controlled pace, Avalanche never trailed.
  • 13 days of rest by Game 1 tipoff.
  • Defines Colorado's identity — pace, transition, controlled aggression.
  • Generational skating ability. Best defenseman in the world when he's on.
vs
Minnesota · December Acquisition

Quinn Hughes

  • Acquired from Vancouver in December — the biggest trade in Wild franchise history.
  • Round 1: 2 goals in Game 6 vs Dallas including the GWG.
  • Drove Minnesota's 12–4 5v5 advantage over Dallas.
  • Made the Hughes trade pay for itself — Bill Guerin's mid-season Tier 1 bet.
  • Best playmaker on the Wild blue line. Series-defining at his ceiling.
  • The Norris confrontation is the structural axis of this series. Whichever defenseman dictates Round 2 dictates the West. Both teams won Round 1 by controlling 5v5. Both teams have a top-pair defenseman who tilts the ice. The team whose blue line wins this matchup wins the series.
  • The matchup the publication's been pointing toward: Tier 1 vs Tier 1 disruptor. Colorado is the Presidents Trophy winner with 13 days of rest. Minnesota is the team that out-executed Dallas at Dallas's own style. This is the deepest read on which form of contender hockey is more durable.
  • Colorado's Round 1: swept LA, never trailed once. Pace control, blue-line activation, depth scoring. Their structural identity has been intact for 16 days.
  • Minnesota's Round 1: controlled Dallas at 5v5 by 12–4. Wallstedt held when needed. Boldy delivered clutch. Hughes drove possession. Tarasenko scored his 50th career playoff goal. That's a four-deep contributor list.
  • The Wallstedt question becomes critical: can a rookie goalie hold Colorado's shot volume the way he held Dallas's? Colorado generates more dangerous chances than Dallas did. Minnesota's path requires Wallstedt to scale up too.
  • The home factor: Game 1 in Denver, where Colorado swept LA in two home games never trailing. Ball Arena hasn't seen the Avalanche play in 16 days. The crowd will be loud. The structural advantage is Colorado's.
Game 1 Outlook
Avalanche by 2, slight lean. Colorado's rest, home ice, and the depth of their offensive system favor them in the opener. Minnesota's path is structural: keep it close at 5v5, get Wallstedt heroics, neutralize Makar at even strength. The Hughes-vs-Makar matchup probably ends mostly even tonight — Game 1 is rarely where the marquee matchup decides itself. The deeper takeaway will be whether Minnesota can dictate pace at Ball Arena, or whether 13 days of Colorado rest produces the same kind of statement Carolina made Saturday.
§ 03

Round 2 fully formed.

Eight teams. Four series. The matchups are set, the storylines are clear, and the bracket is now structurally as deep as any Round 2 in years.

East · Metropolitan
Carolina vs Philadelphia
Canes lead 1–0 after Saturday's 3–0 statement. Game 2 Monday in Raleigh. Carolina set Round 2's early benchmark.
West · Central
Colorado vs Minnesota
Game 1 tonight in Denver. Makar vs Hughes. Tier 1 vs Tier 1 disruptor.
East · Atlantic
Buffalo vs Montreal
Series newly set. Two young, fast, depth-driven teams. Buffalo first series win since 2007 vs Montreal first since 2021. Series starts later this week.
West · Pacific
Anaheim vs Vegas
Game 1 Monday in Vegas. Two evolving Tier 1 candidates with opposite styles. Either result reshapes the contender map.

The Round 2 field reads structurally rich. Three of the four series are cleanly tier-defined matchups. The fourth (Buffalo-Montreal) is the upstart series — both teams making their first Round 2 runs in years, both built around speed and depth, neither carrying championship expectations. Round 1 took 13 days to resolve. Round 2 should reach Conference Finals by mid-May.

The Take
Montreal didn't win Round 1 by being better. They won by surviving long enough for the moment to come to them. Tampa's "we played our best game and lost" framing is the cruelest possible playoff truth — and the most accurate. Eight teams remain. Three of them (Carolina, Colorado, Vegas) won Round 1 by being structurally elite. Two (Anaheim, Minnesota) won by evolving mid-round into structurally elite. Two (Buffalo, Philly) won on speed and pressure. One (Montreal) survived. Round 2 will tell us which kind of advancement actually scales.
