§ 02

Three games, two closeouts, one survival.

Buffalo and Vegas closed by reasserting their identities. Tampa survived by imposing theirs on a Montreal team that couldn't push back. Same lesson Round 1 has been teaching for two weeks: in playoff hockey, identity is destiny.

Series 06 · East  ·  Advanced

Buffalo at Boston

Series Final
Sabres 4 — Bruins 2
  • Buffalo 4, Boston 1. The Sabres won their first playoff series since 2007 — closing the 14-season drought story that began with the 0-for-29 stretch they snapped earlier this year. Game 6 played out exactly the way Buffalo's series-defining template required: score early, force Boston to chase, punish their structural breakouts.
  • Buffalo struck within the first 3:25 and added another in the opening frame to take a 2–0 lead. That early lead neutralized everything Boston needed to win this series — the slow, low-event game where their veterans control margins. Boston's structure only worked when the score was tight; behind 2–0 in the first, it had no answer.
  • Boston cut it to 2–1 with a brief stretch of pressure in the middle frame. Buffalo's response: a turnover-into-rush goal that made it 3–1. That sequence was the series in microcosm — Boston pushes, Buffalo punishes immediately. The Bruins never threatened again.
  • Lyon finished the series as Buffalo's swing performer. The mid-series goalie change to Lyon over Luukkonen produced four wins in five starts. Day 5 of the publication flagged the swap as the inflection point of the entire series; Day 13 confirms it.
  • Buffalo's speed and active defense was the structural weapon. Defensemen joining the rush, sustained zone time, no perimeter passing. Boston coach Jim Montgomery acknowledged it post-game: Buffalo's pace was the difference, and Boston couldn't slow the game down enough to neutralize it.
  • The series read on Buffalo: they're a pace-and-pressure team that's most dangerous when leading. Their Game 5 loss happened because they let Pastrnak get a breakaway in OT — the rare moment when they couldn't dictate. Game 6 they re-established control and never gave it back. Round 2 against Tampa or Montreal is the test of whether their style scales against a Tier 2 opponent that won't fold the way Boston did.
Buffalo went back to what won them the series: speed, early pressure, net-front presence. They didn't try to win Boston's game again. They forced Boston back into theirs.
What This Says
Buffalo is now firmly Tier 2 with a real Tier 1 case in front of them. They dictated pace in 4 of 6 games, got depth scoring, and matured visibly between Games 5 and 6 — Game 5's loss to Pastrnak in OT was a structural lapse; Game 6 was the response. That's growth in real time.
What Boston Was
Situationally strong, structurally outmatched. Boston could win tight games but couldn't dictate them. When the series became about pace, they had no answer. Pastrnak's OT breakaway in Game 5 was their high-water mark; once Buffalo solved that puzzle, the series was over.
Round 2 Awaits
Buffalo gets the TBL/MTL Game 7 winner. If Tampa wins, it's Buffalo speed vs Tampa structure — the publication's most analytically interesting matchup possibility outside of Carolina-Philly. If Montreal wins, it's Buffalo pressure vs Montreal chaos — two upstart teams with depth and speed playing the same kind of hockey. Either matchup is genuinely hard to read.
Series 05 · East  ·  Forced G7

Tampa at Montreal

Series Standing
Tied 3 — 3
  • Tampa 1, Montreal 0 in OT. Goncalves buried a second-chance puck in overtime to send the series back to Bell Centre for a Game 7 Saturday. Vasilevskiy made 30 saves for the shutout — the swing performance Tampa had been waiting for.
  • This was a Game 7-style game played one game early. 0–0 through 60 minutes. 33–30 shots. Both goalies elite. No momentum swings, no emotional takeovers, no chaos hockey. Pure structural discipline from both teams — and Tampa won the kind of game Montreal can't dictate.
  • The pattern the publication has been tracking since Day 11: Montreal can win speed games. Tampa can win any kind of game. Montreal generated enough — they matched shots, stayed structured, didn't give up much. They couldn't finish. They couldn't create the chaos that defines their best hockey. They played a "perfect" game that still loses in playoffs.
  • Vasilevskiy's shutout is the single most important development in this series. For 5 games he was good; in Game 6 he was elite. The Bell Centre crowd never got the goal they needed to lift the building. Tampa's veteran goaltending finally produced the swing performance every contender needs.
  • The series-defining truth: both teams have now flipped the series four times. Tampa won Games 1–2 in OT (Hutson moments). Montreal won Games 3–4 (also in OT, also home-ice driven). Tampa flipped it Game 4. Montreal flipped Game 5. Tampa flipped Game 6. Game 7 is where the actual best version of each team shows up — or doesn't.
  • The 5v5 problem Montreal hasn't solved through six games: the Suzuki-Caufield-Slafkovský line still hasn't generated a 5v5 goal in this series. Caufield's PP goal in Game 5 was a release; it didn't fix the structural issue. For Game 7, Montreal needs that line to score at even strength — or Tampa's depth advantage takes over.
Tampa stayed patient. Trusted structure. Won a 1–0 game. That's veteran playoff DNA.
Tampa's Path
Game 7 in Montreal — the building Tampa hasn't won in this series. Cooper's playbook is now well-defined: Cirelli-Gourde shutting down Suzuki, Hagel driving offense, Vasilevskiy holding the line. If Tampa plays the Game 6 version of this matchup, they're built for tight playoff hockey better than Montreal is.
Montreal's Path
Bell Centre delivers what Bell Centre always delivers: 3 home wins in this series, all in OT or close finishes. The crowd is the variable. Montreal needs an early goal to ride the energy. They need the Suzuki line to score at 5v5 — and they need Hutson to make one more series-defining moment. Everything they need is plausible. Whether it all happens in 60 minutes is another question.
Game 7 Outlook
Coin flip with home-ice lean to Montreal. If the game stays tight, advantage Tampa. Their veterans and Vasilevskiy are built for low-event hockey. If the game opens up, advantage Montreal. Their speed, the Hutson factor, and the Bell Centre crowd are all built for chaos. Whoever can dictate the game's tempo wins. The team that gets the early goal probably gets to choose.
Series 07 · West  ·  Advanced

Vegas at Utah

Series Final
Knights 4 — Mammoth 2
  • Vegas 5, Utah 1. The Knights closed without drama after two games of pure chaos — game 4's 5–4 OT win after blowing 3–0, Game 5's 5–4 2OT thriller on Howden's SHG. Game 6 was different: structured, controlled, decided early. This is the team the publication had been tracking as Tier 1 since Day 9.
  • Mitch Marner finally took over a Vegas closeout game: 2 goals + 1 assist, impact across even strength and the power play. The depth narrative — Howden, Sissons, Dorofeyev — has been the series story, but in the closeout it was the top-end talent that drove the result. Tier 1 teams have both layers, and tonight Vegas had both layers.
  • The defining sequence: Utah cut it to 2–1 with a brief moment of crowd-back-in-it momentum. Vegas immediately responded. That's the series in one shift — Utah creates moments, Vegas kills them. The trailing team's third-period push that defined Games 4 and 5 never had room to build in Game 6.
  • Carter Hart's 21-save performance was the third straight game where he's been calm rather than spectacular. Vegas's defensive structure handled the work; Hart didn't need to steal the game. The Game 5 of stealing was Howden's SHG; Game 6 was system.
  • Vegas now has the most complete profile in the bracket: they won chaos (Games 4–5), they won structure (Game 6), they won depth scoring (Howden's two SHGs), they won star scoring (Marner tonight, Dorofeyev's hat trick Wednesday). Multiple game scripts, multiple ways to win. That's championship DNA.
  • Utah's exit confirms what the publication flagged Day 9: they win moments. They don't win games. They had Vegas on the ropes in Games 4 and 5 — blown 3–0 lead in Vegas's home, late lead in Game 5. They couldn't close either one. The next step in their development is learning to finish what they start.
Vegas survived early chaos, adjusted structurally, and closed with control. They won every type of game the series demanded.
What This Was
A Tier 1 team finishing a Round 1 series in the way Tier 1 teams do — without drama, without wasted energy, without giving the opponent a final flicker of belief. Vegas blew a 3–0 lead in Game 4 and won. Vegas survived a 2OT marathon in Game 5. Game 6 was the calm closeout. That's exactly the resilience the publication's Day 9 article called out.
What This Says
Vegas is fully Tier 1. No caveats. They have the goalie (Hart), the depth (Howden, Sissons), the stars (Marner, Eichel, Theodore, Dorofeyev), the system (defensive structure), and the experience (the 2023 Cup is still in this dressing room). Their Round 2 matchup with Anaheim is the most analytically interesting series in the bracket — two evolving Tier 1 candidates, opposite playing styles, both still figuring out what they really are.
Round 2 Awaits
Vegas vs Anaheim. Two Tier 1 candidates, two different paths to the same status. Vegas built theirs on experience and structure; Anaheim built theirs on speed and chaos that evolved into structure mid-round. The matchup answers the question: can a young, fast, evolving team break a team that doesn't break? If Anaheim wins it, they're real. If Vegas wins it, the Tier 1 status is unimpeachable.

§ 04

The only series still alive.

Tampa-Montreal Game 7 Saturday at the Bell Centre. The series has flipped four times in six games. The seventh decides everything — and Round 1 ends with the bracket's most volatile matchup as its final word.

Series 05 · East  ·  Game 7

Tampa at Montreal

Game 7 · Saturday · Bell Centre
All or nothing
  • The series has flipped four times in six games. Tampa won 1–2. Montreal won 3–4. Tampa flipped Game 4. Montreal flipped Game 5. Tampa flipped Game 6. Game 7 is the series' first non-flip outcome — somebody is finally going to close.
  • Bell Centre's home record this series: Montreal 3–0 at home, all in tight finishes including OT in Games 1 and 3. The crowd is a measurable variable — the publication has been tracking it since Day 11. Saturday's Game 7 is the building's biggest moment in over a decade.
  • The structural matchup: Tampa's veteran experience and Vasilevskiy in elite form vs Montreal's speed, Hutson, and the home crowd. Tampa has now shown they can win any kind of game. Montreal has shown they can win speed games but struggle in pure structure.
  • The unsolved Montreal problem: the Suzuki-Caufield-Slafkovský line at 5v5. Six games, zero 5v5 goals. Caufield's PP goal in Game 5 was the only top-line tally of the series. Game 7 demands either a structural breakthrough from that line or a definitive third-line moment from someone else.
  • The Hagel factor: NHL playoffs goal-scoring leader through Round 1, Tampa's "straw that stirs the drink" per Cooper. If Hagel-Kucherov-Point produces, Tampa wins. If Cirelli-Gourde shuts down Suzuki, Tampa probably wins. If neither happens, Montreal probably wins.
  • Vasilevskiy vs Dobes is the goalie matchup that decides everything. Vasilevskiy stole Game 6 with a 30-save shutout. Dobes has been steady in the series. Whoever wins the goalie battle Saturday wins the series. Vasilevskiy's track record in winner-take-all games is the kind of variable that doesn't show up on box scores until the final whistle.
Game 7 Verdict
Coin flip with slight Bell Centre lean to Montreal — but Tampa has the better Game 7 résumé and Vasilevskiy is in form. Whoever scores first probably wins. Tied at 0–0 entering the third, advantage Tampa. Tied at 1–1 entering the third, advantage Montreal. Score difference of 2+ either way means whichever team got there is going to Round 2. Round 1 has been about which teams close. Saturday is the closeout test.
The Take
Round 1 is one game from over. Seven teams have advanced — Carolina, Colorado, Philly, Minnesota, Anaheim, Buffalo, Vegas — and they've all closed by playing identity-first hockey. Each team won by being the version of themselves that survives playoff pressure. Buffalo dictated speed. Vegas controlled chaos and structure. Carolina absorbed without breaking. Colorado paced. Philly disciplined. Minnesota dominated 5v5. Anaheim evolved. The team that wins Saturday's Game 7 will join the field by doing the same — finding the version of themselves that closes when the moment narrows. Whoever it is, Round 2 is going to be a clinic.
