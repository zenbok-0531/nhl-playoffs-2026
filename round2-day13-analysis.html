The Final Four.

Two games. Two closeout-adjacent moments. Montreal beat Buffalo 6–3 to take a 3–2 series lead — Dobes stopped 32 of his last 32 after a 3-on-4 start. Three of four Conference Final teams are now confirmed: Carolina, Colorado, and Vegas. Montreal can join them Saturday at Bell Centre. The Western Conference Final is set — Game 1 Wednesday in Denver.

§ 01

Dobes survived. Then dictated.

St. Louis stuck with his rookie after 3 goals on 4 shots. Dobes paid him back with the save of the series on Thompson's breakaway. Montreal then erupted for three second-period goals. The Habs top line finally arrived 5v5. Buffalo's home-ice problem became a season-defining one.

Round 2 · Series 02 · East  ·  MTL Leads 3–2

Montreal at Buffalo

Series Standing
Canadiens 3 — Sabres 2
  • Montreal 6, Buffalo 3. Habs scorers: Caufield (5v5 top-line breakthrough), Anderson, Evans, Suzuki (PP), Demidov (1st career playoff goal), Texier. Sabres: Zucker, Doan, Helenius (all on first 4 shots). Habs lead series 3–2. Game 6 Saturday at Bell Centre.
  • The structural truth: Buffalo scored on 3 of their first 4 shots. Dobes looked shaky. The Helenius goal was particularly ugly — a spinning shot from 30 feet that went clean through Dobes' legs. Per St. Louis postgame: "The first period was very chaotic. Very chaotic. Leaving Dobes in, you have to remove the emotion. We talked to our goalie coach Marco (Marciano) and asked for his opinion. He said to leave him in. So we decided to do that. Dobes proved it was the right decision."
  • The save of the series: Thompson on a breakaway, 3:46 into the second period, with Buffalo leading 3–2. Made one move too many. Dobes said no. Per St. Louis: "The save by Dobes on Thompson on the breakaway was the turning point. After that, we started to flip the momentum." One save changed an entire series.
  • Montreal then scored three in the second period. Anderson tied it 3–3. Evans made it 4–3 when a Demidov shot snuck through Luukkonen. Suzuki scored on the PP late after a Thompson cross-checking penalty. Habs went to the dressing room up 5–3. KeyBank Center was silent.
  • Dobes finished with 32 straight saves after the rough start. Postgame: "I told him, 'Thank you for leaving me in.' Trying to prove myself, it's a big part of it, having the trust of your coach. I will never disrespect it."
  • Buffalo goalie change: Luukkonen pulled after the second period (5 goals on 23 shots). Lyon back in to start the third. 4 of the 5 Luukkonen goals were on high-danger chances per Natural Stat Trick, so the defensive structure broke too. Lyon should start Game 6 — he's been the stronger playoff goalie.
  • The Habs PP went 2-for-2 after going 1-for-7 in Game 4. The Suzuki PP goal in the second was the dagger. The structural PP problem from Game 4 just resolved itself in 48 hours.
  • Demidov's first career playoff goal made it 6–3 in the third. Calm finish from the prospect Habs fans have been waiting on. Another piece of the Habs' young core elevating in the playoffs.
  • Identity stats that matter:
    • Montreal 5–0 in these playoffs after a loss (and 10–0 going back to the regular season).
    • Montreal 5–2 on the road this postseason. Better road team than home.
    • Buffalo 2–4 at home this postseason after a 26-10-5 regular-season home record.
  • Ruff's tell on Buffalo's home-ice problem: "When we come back, we're gonna pretend we're on the road." A coach publicly acknowledging his team plays better as an underdog than as a favorite.

What This Was
Montreal's identity game. Survived an objectively bad start, stabilized via coaching trust in a young goalie, got the save of the series at the pivot moment, and erupted offensively because the trust held. The publication has now seen three identity-confirming wins in Round 2: Carolina's Day 4 OT recovery, Colorado's Day 11 Response, Montreal's Day 13 Trust. Three different teams, same structural truth: composure produces leverage.
The Top-Line Breakthrough
The Suzuki-Caufield-Slafkovsky line had not scored a 5v5 goal in the entire postseason until tonight. Then combined for 7 points. The structural question hanging over Montreal for two rounds was whether the top line could finally produce 5v5. Tonight they did. Saturday night is the test of whether that's repeatable in an elimination-leverage game at Bell Centre.
Series Outlook · Updated
Game 6 Saturday at Bell Centre. Montreal can clinch a Conference Final berth at home for the first time in this era. The Habs are 10–0 after losses going back to the regular season. They're 5–2 on the road. They're getting top-line production for the first time. The structural math: Buffalo needs to win two in a row, with one of them on the road, against a team operating as a structural unit.
§ 02

Vegas closed it.

Marner scored 62 seconds in. Howden tied Wayne Gretzky's NHL record for short-handed goals in a single postseason. Vegas led 3–0 after the first and never looked back. Anaheim never recovered.

Round 2 · Series 04 · West  ·  VGK Advances 4–2

Vegas at Anaheim

Series Final
Golden Knights 4 — Ducks 2
  • Vegas 5, Anaheim 1. Knights scorers: Marner (62 seconds in), Howden (SHG), Theodore (PP), Dorofeyev (×2 in the third). Ducks: Granlund (PP). VGK wins series 4–2. To the Western Conference Final.
  • The first period was the series in 20 minutes. Marner scored on the first shot of the game — Vegas's 14th time Dostal allowed a goal on the first shot between regular season and playoffs. By the end of the first period, Vegas led 3–0 and Honda Center was booing.
  • Howden's short-handed goal was the dagger. Backdoor one-timer on a Marner feed during a Vegas PK. His 8th playoff goal. His third short-handed goal of the postseason — ties the NHL record for most SHG in a single postseason, joining Wayne Gretzky and 6 other players. Howden has 3 SHG in his last 9 games. He had 3 SHG in his entire previous 489-game NHL regular-season career.
  • Theodore added a PP goal 5 seconds into a Vegas power play. 3–0 after the first. Dostal stayed in despite the rough start.
  • Marner's playoff numbers are staggering: 18 playoff points. NHL playoff scoring leader. 11 points in 6 games against Anaheim alone. Per Athletic: "This might be the best playoff hockey of his career." The Toronto-to-Vegas redemption narrative the publication has been tracking since Day 8 is now structurally complete.
  • Hart was elite again. 31 of 32 saves. "Vegas never needed him to steal games constantly. They needed stability. He gave them exactly that."
  • The Dorofeyev game-icer was the series in microcosm. Anaheim made a poor decision trying to break the puck out, the puck landed on Dorofeyev's stick in front, he buried it before Dostal could react. Vegas waited for Anaheim to make a misstep with the puck and made them pay for it. That was the structural identity of the entire series.
  • Anaheim's young-core elevation was the editorial counterpoint. Sennecke tied Bobby Ryan's franchise rookie record at 5 G. 15 different Ducks made playoff debuts this run. Per Athletic: "This was not failure. This was a young contender learning the final layer."

Vegas's Identity
Per the Athletic: "Vegas may not always control games. They almost always control the critical moments." First shot of Game 6: goal. First Anaheim PP: short-handed goal against. Game-tying chance: forced turnover, immediate Vegas goal. That's precision hockey at championship level — and the structural identity that Colorado will have to break next Wednesday.
Vegas's Franchise Math
5th Final Four appearance in 9 seasons — most of any team since 2017. 70 postseason wins since franchise inception — also most. Vegas's last Cup was 2023. After R1 elimination by Dallas in 2024 and R2 elimination by Edmonton in 2025, the Golden Knights are back where their organizational baseline says they belong.

The Western Conference Final

  • Colorado vs Vegas, Game 1 Wednesday May 20 at Ball Arena. The bracket's first contender-vs-contender confrontation. Colorado's territorial overwhelm meets Vegas's situational precision. The Athletic called it "the true Stanley Cup Final before the Stanley Cup Final."
  • Colorado: 16 different goal scorers in the Minnesota series. MacKinnon legacy-tier under pressure. Makar with a right-arm injury concern after his late Game 5 collision with Zuccarello. The publication's "complete team" framing since Day 11.
  • Vegas: Marner leading NHL playoff scoring at 18 pts. Howden tied Gretzky's SHG record. Hart structurally anchoring at .920+ across two rounds. Stone potential return after missing all of Round 2. The publication's "fewest mistakes under pressure" framing.
  • Rest math: Colorado gets 7 days off; Vegas gets 5. Both teams at maximum readiness heading in. No fatigue advantage either way.
  • Structural variables: Makar's right-arm status. Stone's return timeline. Bednar's Wedgewood-vs-Blackwood call in Game 1. Three variables that could swing the series.
§ 04

Saturday at Bell Centre.

Montreal's first chance in this era to clinch a Conference Final berth at home. Buffalo facing elimination in the building they've lost 4 of 6 playoff games in.

  • Saturday May 16 · Buffalo at Montreal, Game 6 · 4 PM ET, Bell Centre. Habs win = Eastern Conference Final vs Carolina. Buffalo win = Game 7 Monday in Buffalo.
  • The Bell Centre factor: Montreal is 4 home wins through the playoffs (Tampa R1 series-clinching environment + this run). The roar of an Eastern Conference Final closeout in front of a full Bell Centre is the loudest environment in playoff hockey right now.
  • The Habs' identity stats: 5–0 after losses in these playoffs, 10–0 going back to the regular season, 5–2 on the road. The publication has not seen a Montreal loss followed by a Montreal win — they've literally never been in a position to lose two straight this playoff run.
  • Buffalo's structural problems: top line still missing (Tuch 0/minus-8 in the series, Krebs 0/minus-5). Home record 2–4 this postseason. Lyon to start in net Saturday. Per Ruff: "When we come back, we're gonna pretend we're on the road." That's a coach publicly admitting the team's home-vs-road inversion.
  • If Habs close Saturday: Eastern Conference Final begins Tuesday or Wednesday May 19/20 vs Carolina, who will have had 10–11 days off. The publication has been calling Carolina the "structural standard with championship trajectory" since Day 1. A Habs-vs-Hurricanes ECF is the bracket's most stylistically opposite Eastern matchup possible — Habs' speed and emotional volatility vs Carolina's structural domination and 70%+ xG share.
  • If Buffalo wins Saturday: Game 7 Monday May 18 at KeyBank Center. Buffalo is 2–4 at home this postseason. The historical math is brutal — but Game 7 is its own universe.

Montreal can join them Saturday at Bell Centre. If they do, the four Conference Final teams will be the four teams the publication's Day 5 power rankings effectively predicted — Colorado #1, Carolina #2, Vegas #3, Montreal #4. The Western Conference Final is set: Colorado vs Vegas. Pressure-application vs pressure-resistance. The structural matchup the bracket has been pointing toward since the Round 2 power rankings dropped. Tonight Montreal got their identity-confirming win on the back of one of the best coaching-trust moments of Round 2. Vegas got their veteran precision back on display in front of a Honda Center crowd that ended the night booing. The bracket is now exactly two more games — and probably one elimination weekend — from being fully set. The Cup race is now structurally between four teams that all have a real argument.
