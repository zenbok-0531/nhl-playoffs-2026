Vegas Golden Knights advance to the Stanley Cup Final.

Third Western Conference title in nine seasons of existence. Awaits the winner of Carolina vs Montreal in the Stanley Cup Final.
WCF · Vegas def. Colorado · 4–0 · 3rd WCF title in 9 seasons

Inevitability.

Colorado was 26 points clear of Vegas in the regular-season standings. The clear class of the NHL from puck drop in October. Presidents' Trophy winner. And Vegas demolished them in four games. Marner's 21 playoff points (NHL lead) is a career best by 7 over his entire Toronto career. Howden's 10 G ties for NHL lead, and his 3 SHG tie Gretzky's single-postseason record. The Knights crowded the neutral zone, layered shooting lanes with bodies, and absorbed Colorado's possession until the Avalanche's territorial dominance became "nothing more than window dressing." The publication's adversity-asymmetry thesis from Day 5 just delivered its structural conclusion: Vegas built resilience earlier; Colorado got the wrong kind of adversity at the worst possible time.

Vegas won only 11 of 38 regular-season games against playoff opposition. Finished outside the league's top 10. Fired Bruce Cassidy — their Stanley Cup-winning coach from 2023 — with eight games remaining in the regular season. And then John Tortorella took over March 29, reinvigorated the dressing room without major strategic alterations, and the team has now won 11 of its last 13 games — a feat it didn't accomplish at any point during the regular season. The publication's editorial through-line across Round 3 has been the adversity asymmetry — Vegas built resilience earlier; Colorado spent 82 games being the NHL's best team and got no practice handling adversity until Vegas introduced it in the Conference Final. Tonight in Game 4 the verdict was delivered: Stone breakaway 4:54 into the game off a McNabb long-range pass. Cole Smith insurance off a Coghlan deflection 5:45 left. Hart 20-of-21. 15 blocked shots. Sweep clinched. Colorado, the highest-scoring team in the NHL all year (3.63 GPG), scored 7 goals total in the entire series. Players without a goal: MacKinnon, Necas, Nelson, Lehkonen, Makar. The structural definition of demolition. Vegas is headed to its third Stanley Cup Final in nine years.

§ 01

The series in four moments.

Each game in this series produced its own editorial framing across Round 3 Days 1, 3, and 5 — Blueprint, Stranglehold, Onslaught. Game 4's framing was the Smother. Together they form the structural autobiography of a championship-tier playoff team.

Game 1 · The Blueprint · May 20

Vegas found the structural answer.

VGK 4 · COL 2 · Denver

Vegas survived Colorado's opening 5-minute storm, then took over the middle 25 minutes. Coghlan scored his first NHL goal in 1,615 days. Dorofeyev's NHL-leading 10th playoff goal off a Marner cross-seam feed. Howden's 9th. Hart 36 saves. 22 blocked shots. The framing: compress middle ice, layer shooting lanes, punish turnovers, stay emotionally calm. Per the analyst: "Vegas may have found the blueprint against Colorado."

Game 2 · The Stranglehold · May 22

Vegas wins a 2nd way.

VGK 3 · COL 1 · Denver

Colorado played better than Game 1 — generated more pressure, held a 1-0 lead into the 3rd. Vegas became the FIRST team all season to mount a 3rd-period comeback against Colorado. The Avs' 45-0-0 record with a 3rd-period lead this year died. Eichel surgical TG. Barbashev GWG off a forecheck turnover. COL PP: ZERO shots on goal across 2 PPs. The structural truth that scared Colorado most: Vegas can win their way AND Colorado's way.

Game 3 · The Onslaught · May 24

Vegas erases a 3-0 deficit.

VGK 5 · COL 3 · T-Mobile

Colorado got their dream first period — 3-0 lead, transition flowing. Vegas scored 5 unanswered. First 3-goal playoff comeback in franchise history. Stone returned and scored PP goal 19 seconds into the 2nd. Hertl walked through Malinski for the GWG in transition (almost cruel irony). MacKinnon went down blocking a Theodore slap shot — knee injury, status unknown postgame. Bednar: "Everything fell apart. The first nine minutes was pretty much an onslaught."

Game 4 · The Smother · May 26

Vegas closes it out.

VGK 2 · COL 1 · T-Mobile

Stone breakaway 4:54 in, off a long McNabb feed, deked Blackwood inside near-side post. Vegas built a wall of defenders around their crease. 15 blocked shots. Hart 20-of-21. Blackwood was sensational in his start (24 saves, multiple sprawling robberies of Dorofeyev) but couldn't single-handedly save the series. Cole Smith insurance goal off a Coghlan point shot deflection. Landeskog redirect ended Hart's shutout bid late. Sweep clinched. Cup Final beckons.

§ 02

Marner's career renaissance.

The publication began tracking Mitch Marner's Toronto-to-Vegas redemption arc in Round 2 Day 8 ("The Standard"). Five weeks later he leads the entire NHL postseason in points with a personal career best — and he still has at least 4 games left.

Mitch Marner · Vegas · NHL Playoff Leader

21 points. 7 goals. More playoff goals than his last 3 Toronto playoffs combined.

Marner has 21 playoff points through 16 games — NHL lead. His previous career high over nine seasons with the Toronto Maple Leafs was 14 (in 2023). He's now 7 points past that — and his team has just begun the Stanley Cup Final.

His 7 playoff goals are more than his last 3 playoff runs in Toronto combined. He has as many GWG in 16 playoff games as he had in 81 regular-season games for Vegas. Per Granger: "He has dazzled with a between-the-legs breakaway goal. He has impressed his coaches and teammates more with his otherworldly efficiency in the grunt work: staying above the puck, killing penalties, winning stick battles along the boards."

The Marner-Howden-Dorofeyev top-line ecosystem has produced the NHL's two playoff goal leaders. Howden's 10 goals are tied for the league lead (more than his entire 49-game pre-2026 playoff career). His 3 SHG ties Wayne Gretzky's single-postseason record. Dorofeyev has 10 goals himself.

The structural truth: Toronto's Marner was supposed to be the player who wilted under pressure. Vegas's Marner is the player Vegas hopes lifts the Cup. Same player. Different ecosystem. Different result.

The Knights' multi-headed monster

Brett Howden

3 SHG ties Gretzky's record.

10 playoff goals — tied for NHL lead. More than his entire 49-game pre-2026 playoff career combined. His 3 SHG ties Wayne Gretzky's single-postseason record (and 6 other players). 12 regular-season goals. 10 in 16 playoff games. The publication has tracked him since Round 2 Day 10 ("The Field") — every game added another layer of structural certainty.

Mark Stone

The captain's return arc.

Out from Round 2 Game 3 through WCF Game 2. Returned for WCF Game 3 — scored PP goal 19 seconds into the 2nd period of the 3-goal comeback. Game 4: breakaway opener 4:54 in off a long McNabb feed, deked Blackwood inside the post. His 5th goal of the postseason in 2 games back. Per Karlsson: "He's been leading this team for a long time, and he's going to continue doing that."

Pavel Dorofeyev

Quiet 10 goals.

10 playoff goals — co-leads NHL with Howden. Brilliant finisher off Marner's playmaking. Didn't score in Game 4 (a rare quiet night) but was robbed twice by Blackwood on sprawling sprawling sprawling saves during a 2nd-period PP. He's the cleanest finisher Vegas has had in years.

Jack Eichel

Captaincy by absence.

Drove Vegas's top line for 6 weeks while Stone was out. WCF Game 2 surgical TG. Vegas's centers (Eichel, Hertl, Karlsson, Howden, Dowd) outplayed Colorado's vaunted center group (MacKinnon, Kadri, Nelson, Roy, Drury) — the structural matchup advantage Colorado supposedly had at the trade deadline reversed completely.

Carter Hart

Steady at the right moments.

36 saves in Game 1. 22-of-22 after going down 3-0 in Game 3. 20-of-21 in Game 4 close-out. Hart didn't steal the series — Vegas's skaters won it — but he was the structural definition of stability. Out-dueled both halves of Colorado's Jennings-winning Wedgewood/Blackwood tandem.

Tomas Hertl

The drought-breaker arrived.

Game 3 GWG in transition — walked through Malinski, roofed a backhand past Wedgewood. Vegas's power-forward acquisition was snakebitten through Round 1 and most of Round 2 (the 29-game drought from Day 10 of R2 framing). Hertl scored when it mattered most — the dagger goal in the comeback game that broke Colorado's belief.

§ 03

The adversity verdict arrives.

The publication's Day 5 thesis: "Vegas underachieved all year but experiencing adversity earlier may be to their advantage. Colorado is having the wrong kind of adversity — injury problems." Game 4 delivered the structural conclusion.

Round 3 Thesis · Delivered

Vegas's adversity built resilience. Colorado's adversity built fragility.

Vegas's regular season was structurally difficult. 11 wins in 38 games against playoff opposition. Outside the league's top 10. Fired Bruce Cassidy (the 2023 Cup-winning coach) with 8 games remaining. Cassidy out → Tortorella in. Per Granger: "Tortorella reinvigorated the Golden Knights with a blend of positivity and reassurance when he took over for Bruce Cassidy on March 29."

Vegas's front office kept pushing chips in during the struggle. Acquired Rasmus Andersson from Calgary in January (Whitecloud + prospect + 2027 first-round pick + conditional 2028 2nd that becomes a 1st if Vegas wins the Cup). Trade deadline: Nic Dowd from Washington, Cole Smith from Nashville. Vegas had as many losses as wins at the deadline. They kept building anyway.

Colorado was the structural opposite. Presidents' Trophy winner. 45-0-0 when leading after two periods. Highest-scoring team in the NHL all year (3.63 GPG). 9-0 sweep of Round 1 and Round 2. Then Vegas introduced adversity in the Conference Final and Colorado's injuries compounded: Makar missed Games 1 and 2 (PP went 0 shots on 2 PPs in Game 2). MacKinnon injured Game 3. Nichushkin out Game 4. Wedgewood replaced by Blackwood.

Adversity has a timing component. Vegas spent 82 games building adversity-resilience. Colorado spent 82 games being the best team in the league. When playoff adversity arrived, only one team was prepared for it. Vegas had 21 regular-season comeback wins and added 5 more in the playoffs — including the first 3-goal playoff comeback in franchise history in WCF Game 3.

Per the Athletic: "The typical anxiety that accompanies playing from behind with the season on the line seems to have no effect on the Vegas bench."

Vegas's structural identity (final form)

  • Heavy. Layered. Patient. Territorial. Emotionally flat (in a good way). Vegas doesn't beat teams by dazzling them. They lean on you, wear down your breakout, kill your 2nd and 3rd options, force you into longer defensive shifts, and punish mistakes late.
  • Adaptable in every game style. The publication's "two ways" framing from Day 3 became "every way" by the end of this series. Vegas can win low-event games, comeback games, forecheck games, rush games, special-teams games, and emotional games. They have proven each. The terrifying versatility Colorado lacked.
  • Depth in the right places. Cole Smith insurance goal in Game 4 (4th line, trade-deadline acquisition). Dylan Coghlan's 1,615-day-drought goal in Game 1 (recent AHL call-up). Nic Dowd empty-netter in Game 1 (deadline addition). McNabb assist on Stone's Game 4 breakaway (defenseman with 3 assists this postseason). The publication has been tracking depth contributions since the VGK-ANA Series Recap. Every name compounds.
  • Penalty kill that's nearly inactive shorthanded. VGK PK during this series: 4 of 5 (80%). Marner and Howden are constant short-handed threats. Colorado's PP managed zero shots on 2 PPs in Game 2. The bracket's best top-line PP unit got structurally broken.
  • Goaltending that doesn't have to be exceptional — just stable. Hart was excellent at the right moments but not asked to steal games. He stopped 36 in Game 1, 22-of-22 after a Game 3 3-goal hole, 20-of-21 in Game 4 closeout. Stability over heroics — the championship-tier model.

Colorado's structural reckoning

  • The highest-scoring team in the NHL all year scored 7 goals in 4 games. Players without a goal: MacKinnon, Necas, Nelson, Lehkonen, Makar. Almost the entire top six. That's a structural collapse, not a slump.
  • The 45-0-0 record died in Game 2. And then died again in Game 3 (where they led 3-0 after the 1st and lost). Colorado's "we never lose with a lead" identity dissolved across two consecutive games against the same opponent.
  • Per Landeskog postgame: "They're moving on and we're not. Whether that's in four games or seven games, it hurt just as much." Captain naming the structural reality — the path to a 4-2 or 4-3 series didn't exist.
  • The Avs' transition identity got dismantled. Vegas crowded the neutral zone. Layered shooting lanes with bodies. Forced dump-ins. Punished turnovers. Colorado's territorial dominance became "nothing more than window dressing for the underlying numbers" — Granger's framing.
  • The hard questions Colorado now faces: Goaltending tandem (Wedgewood/Blackwood neither carried the load). Top-six finishing. Defensive depth. Coaching adjustments. Plus injury recovery. Per the Athletic: "the NHL's most shocking sweep since the 2019 Columbus Blue Jackets — coached by current Vegas coach John Tortorella — knocked off the Presidents' Trophy-winning Tampa Bay Lightning in the first round."
§ 04

The Cup Final waits.

Vegas advances to its 3rd Stanley Cup Final in 9 years of franchise existence. The Eastern Conference Final continues — Carolina leads Montreal 2-1 with Game 4 tonight in Montreal.

· Stanley Cup Final 2026 · Vegas vs ECF Winner ·

The Knights wait.

Vegas finishes Round 3 with at least 6 days of rest before the Stanley Cup Final opens. The Eastern Conference Final continues — Carolina leads Montreal 2-1 heading into Game 4 tonight in Montreal. If Carolina wins, they need at least 2 more games to clinch (potential to extend to 7). If Montreal wins, the ECF goes minimum 6, possibly 7.

The publication's Round 3 prediction zone gets tested: The Day 2 "Counter" framing said Montreal exposed a real structural seam in Carolina. The Day 4 "Reset" framing said Carolina's identity was restorable AND it still required overtime. The ECF G3 update (which we'll cover when available) will tell us whether Carolina's adjustments traveled or Montreal's home-ice identity reasserted.

For Vegas, the structural advantage of waiting: Stone heals further. Whoever else is banged up heals. The system stays sharp via internal practice and video. Tortorella is on record from Day 1 that this team doesn't need extensive on-ice practice in late playoffs — they like the rest. Per the Day 3 framing: "We're very comfortable in the third period. All year we played a lot of comebacks."

The publication's Cup Final favorite is now structurally Vegas. Healthier. Deeper. More versatile. Coming off a 4-game sweep of the Presidents' Trophy winner. The publication's Day 5 thesis — that Vegas's regular-season adversity built playoff resilience — is now structurally confirmed. Whether Carolina or Montreal advances next, they will face the most complete remaining team in the bracket.

Vegas in context · franchise milestones

  • 9th banner in 9 seasons of existence. Vegas has now hung division titles (5), conference titles (3), and the 2023 Stanley Cup. They have more division titles, more playoff games, more playoff series than any other NHL team since their 2017 inception. Sustained excellence at a level the league rarely sees from an expansion franchise.
  • 3rd Stanley Cup Final appearance. 2018 (lost to Washington). 2023 (won vs Florida). 2026 (vs TBD). Of those three runs, this current team may be the most structurally complete. The 2018 team was a Cinderella. The 2023 team had peak Eichel/Stone. The 2026 team has Marner career-best AND depth AND Tortorella's identity AND adversity-tested resilience.
  • 11 of last 13 games won — including the 4-game sweep of the Presidents' Trophy winner. Per Granger: "Under the brightest lights, against the stiffest competition, the team has come to life." That's a structural read — not running hot, peaking at the right time.
  • The Cassidy → Tortorella story is structural editorial. Cassidy won the 2023 Cup but was fired with 8 games remaining this regular season. Tortorella reinvigorated the room without major strategic alterations. Now Tortorella may be 4 wins from his first Stanley Cup as a head coach since 2004 (Tampa Bay). The publication won't editorialize on the Cassidy firing — but the result speaks structurally.
The Take
Vegas spent 82 games being NOT the best team in the league. They won 11 of 38 games against playoff opposition. They fired their Stanley Cup-winning coach with 8 games remaining. They finished outside the league's top 10. Colorado, meanwhile, won the Presidents' Trophy, posted a 45-0-0 record with a 3rd-period lead, and was the clear class of the NHL from October through the start of the Conference Final. And then Vegas demolished them. The Avs' highest-scoring offense in the NHL produced 7 goals in 4 games. MacKinnon, Necas, Nelson, Lehkonen, and Makar combined for zero. Vegas's adversity-resilience built across 82 regular-season games delivered the structural conclusion. Marner is now NHL points leader with a career-best 21 points — more than his last 3 Toronto playoffs combined. Howden ties Gretzky for SHG in a single postseason. Stone returned from injury and scored in both games back. The Knights have won 11 of their last 13 games. The Cup conversation has compressed to one team being the structural favorite — Vegas — with the Eastern Conference winner having to prove they can match the most complete team in the bracket. The publication's Day 5 thesis is now Round 3's editorial conclusion: adversity at the right time builds champions; adversity at the wrong time breaks them.
