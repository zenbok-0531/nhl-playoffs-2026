Colorado over Minnesota · 4–1 · To the Western Conference Final

The Climb.

A series that ended the way Colorado has done everything else this postseason: by absorbing pressure longer than the opponent could apply it.

Colorado climbed back from a 3–0 second-period deficit to win Game 5 4–3 in overtime. Brett Kulak — first goal since January 19, 2026 — scored the series winner 3:52 into OT on a Necas feed. Earlier, MacKinnon's tying snipe with 1:23 left and the goalie pulled was the legacy moment. Series 4–1. Colorado has now won every game script playoff hockey offers, across two rounds and a perfect-record fight-back — and is now to the Western Conference Final for the 8th time since 1995.

Carolina's been to the Eastern Conference Final since Saturday. As of last night, Colorado is to the Western Conference Final. The Avs eliminated the Minnesota Wild in five games, and they did it the most Colorado way possible: by trailing 3–0 midway through the second period of the closeout game, then climbing back to win 4–3 in overtime on a Brett Kulak goal — his first goal since January 19, when he was still playing for the Pittsburgh Penguins. This was the defining game of Colorado's postseason so far, because it confirmed the structural truth the publication has been tracking since the Day 3 "Track Meet" framing: this team can win in every possible mode. Comeback. Overtime. Elimination. Structural domination. Track meets. Tight defense. Star-led. Depth-led. Goalie-anchored. Wild-open. They've now done all of them in two rounds. Carolina is alone at 8–0. Colorado is alone in the "complete team" category. The Cup race is now a structurally two-team conversation, with Vegas or Anaheim still trying to make it three.

· · · Western Conference Final · Colorado Advances · · ·

Colorado climbs.

The Avalanche win the Western Conference semifinal 4–1 and become the second team through to a Conference Final this postseason. They'll face the Vegas-Anaheim winner; Vegas leads that series 3–2 with Game 6 in Anaheim Thursday night. Colorado moves on to its 8th Conference Final since relocating to Denver in 1995–96. This was the first time the Avalanche won a playoff series on home ice in 18 years — since 2008, against this same Wild franchise, when Joe Sakic and Peter Forsberg still wore the burgundy.

§ 01

The climb.

Down 3–0 midway through the second period. Goalie pulled in the third. MacKinnon's tying snipe with 1:23 left. Kulak's OT winner on a Necas feed. The third time in 53 tries that Colorado has overcome a 3-goal deficit to win a playoff game since moving to Denver.

Round 2 · Game 5 · Series Clincher

Minnesota at Colorado

Game · Series Final
COL 4 — MIN 3 (OT) · COL wins 4–1
  • Colorado 4, Minnesota 3 in overtime. Avs scorers: Drury (3:33 left in regulation), MacKinnon (1:23 left, goalie pulled), and Kulak (3:52 OT GWG). Wild scorers: Johansson (34 seconds in), Nick Foligno (×2 — gave MIN a 3–0 lead by midway through the second). Avs win series 4–1. To the Western Conference Final.
  • The structural truth of the night: Minnesota played the perfect desperation game early. Goal 34 seconds in. 3–0 lead midway through the game. Home crowd if they'd been in St. Paul, but in Denver they had to absorb it. And it still wasn't enough. That's devastating psychologically — the Wild executed exactly what an underdog elimination plan demands, and lost.
  • The Avalanche overcame a 3-goal deficit to win a playoff game for just the 3rd time in 53 tries since moving to Denver. The Wild had been 21–0 when leading a playoff game by at least 3 goals before this elimination loss.
  • Bednar's goalie call again proved decisive. Mackenzie Blackwood pulled after the first period (3 goals on 7 shots). Scott Wedgewood entered, made 7 saves, allowed nothing. The Athletic's analytical read: "Pulling Blackwood could have destabilized Colorado. Instead it settled them. Calm. Controlled. No extra goals allowed." Huge coaching move.
  • MacKinnon's tying goal with 1:23 left is the legacy moment. Empty net behind him. Sent the puck from the left side past Wallstedt and into a small space in the top left corner. Elite execution under maximum pressure. When Colorado needed calm, belief, and skill, MacKinnon delivered the only kind of goal that scenario allows.
  • The Kulak OT winner is the depth story. Necas took the puck, glided behind the net and back out front, found an open Kulak. One-timer past Wallstedt. Kulak's first goal since January 19, 2026 — when he was still with Pittsburgh. Kulak became the 16th different Avalanche player to score in the Minnesota series. 16 different goal scorers in 5 games. That depth profile is almost impossible to defend over four rounds.
  • Kulak's path is its own editorial: started the season with Edmonton. Traded to Pittsburgh in December. Traded to Colorado on February 24 in the deal that sent Samuel Girard the other way. Three teams in one regular season. One regular-season goal. The series-clinching OT winner that sent his current team to the Conference Final.
  • Cale Makar injury concern. Collided with Mats Zuccarello late in the game, grabbing his right arm. Went down the tunnel before returning to the ice. No postgame confirmation yet of his status. He's the structural variable for the Conference Final, and the Avs cannot lose him.
  • Wallstedt finished with 30 saves but the structure cracked. The Athletic: "Wallstedt was great again. The structure in front of him wasn't quite enough." Boldy and Sturm each had 2 assists. The Wild were missing Joel Eriksson Ek and Jonas Brodin for the entire series.

What This Was
Colorado's full championship demonstration. The publication has tracked them as Tier 1 since Day 3. Tonight they showed they can absorb a 3-goal elimination-game deficit, change goalies mid-game without destabilizing, get a legacy snipe from their superstar, and end the series with a depth player who had one regular-season goal. This is what the Athletic analytical breakdown called "championship behavior" — not just elite talent, but elite emotional stability.
The Suffocation Truth
Minnesota started fast, played emotionally, expended huge energy early. Colorado controlled puck, extended shifts, wore them down. By the late third, Minnesota looked exhausted. That's exactly what elite puck-possession teams do — they apply pressure longer than most teams can survive it. Eventually mistakes happen, legs go, structure cracks. And Colorado punishes instantly. That's the structural identity of a Cup team.
The Makar Variable
Cale Makar collided with Zuccarello late, grabbing his right arm. Went down the tunnel, returned to play. The publication's Day 3 framing tracked Makar through a leg-injury return; tonight's collision is a new variable. If Makar is anything less than 100% for the Conference Final, Colorado's structural ceiling drops noticeably. He's the connective tissue of their breakout game.

Five games. Five distinct editorial frames. Colorado won four of them. The series ran the full spectrum of playoff hockey — track meet, structural blowout, opponent comeback, decisive response, championship-level elimination-game climb. Across two rounds (Kings in 4, Wild in 5), Colorado has played 9 games and won 8 — only one short of Carolina's 8–0 record, with one more series under their belt. The structural through-line: the publication's Day 4 "Composure" framing for Carolina now belongs to Colorado too. Different team, same identity.

§ 03

The storylines.

MacKinnon's playoff legacy moment. Kulak's improbable arc. The 16-scorer depth. The Wallstedt heroic loss. The injuries that defined Minnesota's ceiling. The 18-year drought that just ended.

Storyline · Superstar

MacKinnon's legacy moment.

The tying goal in Game 5 — empty net behind him, 1:23 left, top-left-corner snipe from a tough angle — is the kind of clip that lives in highlight reels for a decade. This is the playoff moment that turns a star into a champion. Across the series MacKinnon had 3 straight 3-point games (only the 6th player in 40 years to do that in a single playoff run), drove pace in every game, and delivered the most important regulation goal of Colorado's postseason. The publication's Day 5 framing called him the bracket's most dangerous individual performer. Day 13 confirms it.

Storyline · Depth

Kulak the 16th scorer.

Brett Kulak became the 16th different Avalanche player to score in the Minnesota series — and he did it on the OT GWG to clinch it. His first goal since January 19, when he was still wearing a Pittsburgh sweater. Three NHL teams this season: Edmonton to start, Pittsburgh by December, Colorado at the trade deadline. "I'm not the guy everyone's looking down the bench like 'go win it for us.'" That's the depth story: when Colorado needs the 16th option to deliver, the 16th option delivers.

Storyline · Goaltending

Bednar's goalie carousel.

The publication tracked Wedgewood's 6–0 start through Day 5. Then he was pulled in Game 3. Bednar started Blackwood in Game 4 — Blackwood won. Then Bednar started Blackwood again in Game 5, pulled him after 3 goals on 7 shots, brought Wedgewood in cold to close it. Wedgewood allowed nothing. Across the series Bednar made two structural goalie calls under pressure. Both worked. That's the kind of coaching adaptability championship teams need — and most don't have.

Storyline · Heroic Loss

Wallstedt kept Minnesota in it.

The Wild rookie goaltender allowed 8 goals in Game 1 and got pulled. Bounced back in Game 2 (didn't play). Made his return in Game 3 and stole it with 34 saves to end Colorado's streak. Across Games 3–5 he stopped 30+ shots per game. The Athletic's framing in Game 5 was direct: "Wallstedt was great again. The structure in front of him wasn't quite enough." That's the truth of his series — he did his job. His team's roster was missing two of its core pieces. The math caught up.

Storyline · Roster

Eriksson Ek and Brodin out.

Joel Eriksson Ek (the Wild's top defensive center) missed all 5 games with a lower-body injury from Round 1 Game 6. Jonas Brodin missed all 5 games too. Two of Minnesota's core defensive players unavailable for the entire Colorado series. The publication flagged Eriksson Ek as the structural matchup variable from Day 3. Without him, Minnesota's checking matchup against MacKinnon never functioned the way Hynes needed. The Wild were brave; the math without their captain was always going to be tight.

Storyline · Historical

The 18-year drought.

The last time the Avalanche clinched a playoff series on home ice was 2008 — against this same Wild franchise. That team had Joe Sakic and Peter Forsberg. The 8th Conference Final appearance since relocating to Denver in 1995–96. Across 18 years and multiple eras of Colorado hockey, the franchise has won plenty of series — just always finished them on the road. Last night they finally finished one at home. The MacKinnon-Makar era now has its first home series-clinching moment.

The bracket math

  • Western Conference Final · Colorado vs the VGK-ANA winner. If Vegas closes in Anaheim tonight, the WCF is Colorado vs Vegas — the two Tier-1 Western teams the publication has been tracking since Round 1. If Anaheim wins Game 6 and then Game 7, the WCF is Colorado vs Anaheim — the bracket's most surprising team against its most complete.
  • Eastern Conference Final · Carolina vs the BUF-MTL winner. Buffalo and Montreal are tied 2–2 with Game 5 tonight in Buffalo. Carolina has been resting since May 9 — by the time the East Final starts, they'll have had 10+ days off.
  • The Cup race is now structurally a two-team conversation. Carolina at 8–0 is the perfection narrative; Colorado after this series win is the completeness narrative. The publication's Day 5 Round 2 power rankings had Colorado #1 and Carolina #2. After the Day 11 "Response" and tonight's "Climb," both teams sit at the top of the bracket together. The Conference Finals matter for matchup math, but the Cup conversation is now functionally between two teams.
  • The Makar status is the biggest structural variable for Colorado's Cup ceiling. Right-arm injury concern from a late Zuccarello collision in Game 5. He returned to play. No postgame status update. If he's anything less than 100% for the WCF, Colorado's structural ceiling drops noticeably.
  • Rest math: Colorado gets at least 4 days off (assuming Vegas closes Thursday). Carolina gets at least 10 days off. The team that wins each Conference Final will face a much-better-rested opponent in the Cup Final — unless the Eastern series ends quickly enough to compress the schedule.
The Take
The publication began tracking Colorado as a Tier 1 team on Day 3 of Round 2 — when they won a 9–6 track meet in Game 1 against the Wild. Five games later, they've won every type of playoff game hockey produces. Track meets. Structural blowouts. Comeback wins. OT wins. Goalie-anchored wins. Depth-driven wins. Last night's elimination-game climb from 3–0 down — with a goalie change that worked, a MacKinnon snipe with 1:23 left, and a 16th-scorer OT winner from a player who'd been on three NHL teams this season — was the structural championship demonstration. The Athletic's analytical read was direct: "Colorado deserves to be considered the clear Stanley Cup favorite right now." The publication's read is slightly more careful — Carolina is 8–0, and perfection beats completeness when the records are this clean. But the Cup race is now structurally a two-team conversation. Carolina vs Colorado for the Stanley Cup is now the bracket's most likely outcome. Vegas or Anaheim has to prove otherwise starting Thursday.
