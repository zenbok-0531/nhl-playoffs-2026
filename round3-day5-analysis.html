· · · Historical Context · 0–3 Series Comebacks · · ·

Vegas is one win from the Stanley Cup Final.

Only four teams in NHL history have come back from down 0-3 to win a playoff series: the 1942 Toronto Maple Leafs (Stanley Cup Final), 1975 New York Islanders (quarterfinals), 2010 Philadelphia Flyers (conference semifinals), and 2014 Los Angeles Kings (1st round). In Conference Finals specifically, it has happened ZERO times in the modern format. Colorado is facing structural and historical impossibility — with MacKinnon's knee status unconfirmed and Vegas peaking. Game 4 Tuesday at T-Mobile Arena. The Stanley Cup Final is on the line.

§ 01

The first 3-goal comeback in franchise history.

Vegas was down 3-0 in the first period of a Conference Final at home. They scored 5 unanswered. That's the structural definition of a championship-tier team — and the structural definition of an aura dying.

Round 3 · WCF · Game 3  ·  VGK Leads 3–0

Colorado at Vegas

Series Standing

  • Vegas 5, Colorado 3. COL scorers (all 1st period): Landeskog, Kadri, Drury (breakaway off Parker Kelly clearance). VGK scorers: Stone (PP, 19 sec into 2nd, off Marner feed), Karlsson (bouncing puck), Kolesar (tied 3-3, mid-2nd, in front), Hertl (GWG mid-3rd, transition through Malinski, backhand roof), Howden (EN, 59 sec left). Vegas leads series 3-0. Game 4 Tuesday at T-Mobile Arena — Vegas can clinch the Stanley Cup Final berth on home ice.
  • Colorado opened with their dream first period. 3 goals. Open ice. Transition flow. Pace. For the first time all series, Colorado got the game environment they wanted — and they took full advantage. The Avalanche looked like the team that dominated the regular season wire-to-wire. Vegas looked rattled.
  • The disallowed Dorofeyev goal was the 41-second moment that ALMOST flipped the series back. On the PP trailing 2-0, Dorofeyev had a backhander stopped by Wedgewood. The rebound fluttered up. Dorofeyev knocked it in with a two-handed bunt. Goal waved off. Replays showed the puck hit the very corner of his right glove and the shaft of his stick (probably more stick than glove, but rules are clear that pucks can't be batted in with the glove). After a lengthy review, the call on the ice stood. 41 seconds later, Drury beat Hart on a breakaway after a Kelly pitchfork clearance got behind the 5-forward Vegas PP unit. Instead of 2-1, it became 3-0.
  • Then Vegas's emotional stability took over. Per Marner postgame: "You're just proud of your team for responding the way they did." Per Hanifin in Day 3: "We're very comfortable in the third period. All year we played a lot of comebacks." That's not just a quote — it's structural identity. Vegas had 21 comeback wins in the regular season and 5 in the playoffs. Adversity is in their DNA.
  • The Stone return changed the emotional temperature of the game completely. PP goal 19 seconds into the 2nd period. Backdoor tap-in off a Marner cross-seam feed. Per Stone postgame: "It felt like (on Saturday) there was no reason to not jump back in there. Tonight felt great." His first appearance since leaving Round 2 G3 vs Anaheim. He was on the ice for 2 first-period goals against, but he found his rhythm after his goal. Per Karlsson: "That's what he does. He's been leading this team for a long time, and he's going to continue doing that."
  • The Hertl GWG was symbolic. Mid-3rd period. Transition play. Pulled the puck through Malinski onto his backhand. Roofed it past Wedgewood. Per Marner: "He was snakebitten there for a while. He had some amazing looks throughout the last couple of games there that he wasn't able to finish, but that's what he does. He makes those big plays when you need them." Hertl's drought-breaker came in transition — the exact area Colorado is supposed to dominate. Almost cruel irony.
  • Marner's NHL-leading 20th playoff point is his career best. His previous career high through 9 Toronto seasons was 14 (in 2023). The assist on Stone's goal was his 10th primary helper of the playoffs — also NHL lead. The publication has tracked his "Toronto-to-Vegas redemption arc" since Round 2 Day 8 ("The Standard"). It's now a full career renaissance.
  • Hart's line: 3 goals allowed on first 11 shots. Stopped final 22. That's the structural definition of a goalie holding his team in long enough to win. Hart didn't steal Game 3 — Vegas's skaters won it — but he stopped Brock Nelson on multiple late chances (Nelson missed high on a clear look at 12:40 of the 2nd, hit the crossbar on a breakaway sprung by Manson at 15:16).
  • Wedgewood's series is in trouble. SV% this series dropped to .877. Per Lazerus: "Wedgewood hasn't been Colorado's biggest issue in this series, but he's the easiest one to replace. Don't be surprised if Blackwood starts Game 4." The Blackwood-vs-Wedgewood call the publication has been tracking since the COL-MIN Series Recap is now structurally inevitable.
  • Cale Makar returned to Colorado's lineup for Game 3. The Norris-tier driver who missed Games 1 and 2 was back on the back end. Colorado got their dream first period largely because the structural transition pieces were finally functional with Makar restarting breakouts. The cruel irony: Colorado finally got their best defenseman back — and lost their best forward (MacKinnon) on a knee block in the same game.
  • Colorado's first period was the structural reminder of what they CAN be. Pace, transition, finishing, dominance — the Avs that led the NHL wire-to-wire. Per the analyst: "Colorado played like the league's best team for 20 minutes. Vegas played like the better playoff team for the remaining 40. That's the series."

§ 02

MacKinnon goes down.

Colorado's engine took a Theodore slap shot to the right knee at 12:15 of the 2nd. Crumpled to the ice. Played sparingly the rest of the game. Was not in the postgame locker room. The series may now be over.

Colorado · Nathan MacKinnon · Right Knee · Status Unknown

Colorado lost their engine on a shot block.

At 12:15 of the second period, MacKinnon blocked a Shea Theodore slap shot off the right knee. Crumpled to the ice in pain. Briefly got to one knee and cleared the puck out of the defensive zone before falling back to the ice. Lay prone for about a minute before trudging off under his own power, doubled over in pain. He stayed on the bench initially. Didn't miss a shift.

His first shift back lasted 32 seconds. His second was 16 seconds. When he got back to the bench, he limped down the tunnel and didn't return for the start of the third period. He finally made it back to the bench at the 2:24 mark of the 3rd and immediately went in for a 4-on-4 shift, looking like he was laboring significantly. He didn't take another shift until a power play midway through the period — played all 2 minutes but "had none of his usual burst and wasn't handling entries. He mostly coasted, looking for one-timers or trying to dish out a centering feed from the half-wall."

Bednar postgame: no update on MacKinnon's status. MacKinnon was not in the postgame locker room when reporters entered. That's typically a bad sign — players who are confident about playing the next game are usually available to media. Players whose status is uncertain often aren't.

Valeri Nichushkin was also injured in Game 3. Missed the entire third period. Bednar postgame: "You lose Nate and Val, too. You're running a shorter bench. Not ideal circumstances for sure."

§ 03

Two kinds of adversity.

Vegas spent the regular season under-achieving and learning to win under pressure. Colorado spent the regular season being the league's best team. Tonight revealed which kind of adversity makes a team championship-tier — and which kind breaks them.

Vegas · The Right Kind of Adversity

Earlier resilience-building.

Vegas underachieved for stretches of the regular season. They were never the league's best team. They had to grind through Edmonton in Round 1, beat Anaheim in 6 in Round 2 without Mark Stone for half of it, and now face down a 3-goal first-period hole in WCF Game 3.

21 regular-season comeback wins. 5 playoff comeback wins. The first 3-goal playoff comeback in franchise history happened tonight. Per Hanifin in Day 3: "We're very comfortable in the third period. All year we played a lot of comebacks." That's not bravado — it's earned structural identity.

Vegas learned how to handle adversity BEFORE the playoffs. Now they're in their preferred environment. Down 3-0 on home ice in a Conference Final game? Vegas's reaction was: settle, score, take over. The adversity built earlier in the season just paid off in the biggest moment of their year.

Verdict · Adversity early → resilience late
Colorado · The Wrong Kind of Adversity

Injury cascade.

Colorado was the NHL's best team wire-to-wire. Presidents' Trophy. 45-0-0 record with a 3rd-period lead this season. Through Round 1 and Round 2 they cruised — going 9-0 in the first two rounds. They had no adversity until they got to Vegas.

And now the adversity they're getting is the wrong kind: injuries. Makar missed Games 1 and 2 (PP zero shots in Game 2). MacKinnon went down on a shot block tonight (right knee, status unknown). Nichushkin missed the entire 3rd period. Manson banged up. Wedgewood SV% .877 in the series — Blackwood likely starting Game 4.

Resilience to injury is different than resilience to game-state. Vegas built resilience to game-state earlier in the season. Colorado is now learning resilience to injury — but injury-resilience requires depth that Colorado may not have at the levels needed. MacKinnon is irreplaceable. Makar is irreplaceable. Stone is replaceable; Vegas proved it for 6 weeks. Vegas's adversity made them stronger. Colorado's adversity is making them broken.

Verdict · Adversity late → fragility late

The structural truth — adversity has a timing component: Teams that face significant adversity in the regular season AND survive develop the resilience to handle bigger adversity in the playoffs. Teams that have no adversity in the regular season are often unprepared when the playoffs introduce it. Vegas spent 82 games becoming adversity-proof. Colorado spent 82 games being the best team in the league. Tonight in the second period — when Vegas erased a 3-0 deficit in roughly 9 minutes — that asymmetry revealed itself completely.

Mitch Marner · Vegas's Toronto-to-Vegas Career Renaissance

Marner just set a career-best playoff total — at the Conference Final stage.

20 playoff points through 14 games. NHL lead. His previous career high over 9 seasons with the Toronto Maple Leafs was 14 (in 2023). He's now 6 points past that — and his team has 1 game left to make the Cup Final.

10 primary helpers in the playoffs — also NHL lead. Per Granger: "The way he has weaved through traffic and bought time with the puck to set up his linemates, Marner looks like a player that was built for playoff hockey." He has played all 3 forward positions. He's Vegas's best PP QB in years. He has 4 SHA on the playoff PK.

The publication's Round 2 Day 8 framing ("The Standard") said Marner was finally playing free of Toronto's emotional burden. Tonight he set up Mark Stone's series-changing goal and provided the pass that broke Colorado emotionally. He may be the most important player in the entire 2026 postseason.
