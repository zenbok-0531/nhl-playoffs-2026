Belief.

A series Montreal won not by territorial control, not by structural dominance, but by emotional resilience and one player who has now made history twice.

Alex Newhook scored 11:22 into overtime to send Montreal to the Eastern Conference Final. He is now the SECOND player in NHL history to score multiple Game 7 series-clinching goals in a single postseason — joining Nathan Horton in 2011, the year Boston won the Stanley Cup. The Habs are 6–0 after losses this entire postseason. They are the youngest team to reach a Conference Final since the 1993 Montreal Canadiens — who won the Stanley Cup. The Final Four is locked. Colorado vs Vegas Wednesday at Ball Arena. Carolina vs Montreal Thursday in Raleigh.

The Final Four is set. Carolina (since May 9). Colorado (since May 13). Vegas (since May 14). And tonight in overtime at KeyBank Center: Montreal. Alex Newhook scored 11:22 into OT off a Jake Evans setup, and the Canadiens edged the Sabres 3–2 to win their second straight 7-game series. Newhook is now the second player in NHL history to score multiple Game 7 series-clinching goals in a single postseason — joining Nathan Horton, who did it for Boston in 2011 on their way to the Cup. The Habs are the youngest team to reach a Conference Final since the 1993 Canadiens. They are 6–0 after losses this postseason. They never lost back-to-back games the entire run. That's not luck. That's identity. The publication has been tracking that pattern since Day 13. Tonight it paid off at the only level that mattered — getting through.

· · · Eastern Conference Final · Montreal Advances · Final Four Set · · ·

Canada's team survives.

Montreal becomes the fourth and final Conference Final team. They've now played 14 playoff games in 30 days, survived two grueling 7-game series (Tampa and Buffalo), and won both Game 7s on the road. The Final Four is officially locked: Carolina vs Montreal in the East starting Thursday in Raleigh, Colorado vs Vegas in the West starting Wednesday in Denver. The publication's Day 5 Round 2 power rankings predicted exactly this quartet — Colorado #1, Carolina #2, Vegas #3, Montreal #4. Five weeks later, all four prediction teams are alive.

§ 01

The decider.

Montreal opened 2–0 to take the Buffalo crowd out of it. Buffalo dominated the next 40 minutes — 56-26 shot attempts, 28-17 scoring chances through 2 periods. Dobes made 37 saves to keep his team in. Dahlin tied it 6:27 into the third. Newhook ended it 11:22 into OT.

Round 2 · Game 7 · Series Clincher

Montreal at Buffalo

Series Final
MTL 3 — BUF 2 (OT) · Habs win 4–3
  • Montreal 3, Buffalo 2 in overtime. Habs scorers: Danault (off Anderson forecheck and Guhle redirect, 4:30 of the first), Bolduc (PP, midway through 1st), Newhook (OT GWG, 11:22). Sabres: Greenway (Samuelsson deflection, 2nd period), Dahlin (tying snipe, 6:27 of 3rd). MTL wins series 4–3. To the Eastern Conference Final.
  • The Habs scored first for only the second time in the entire series. Per Guhle pregame: "Try to take the crowd out of it early. It's going to be loud in there, their building's going pretty good. Just keep it simple." Guhle followed his own advice — threw the puck on net, it bounced off Danault's skate past Luukkonen at 4:30 of the first. Bolduc made it 2-0 on the PP 10 minutes later. KeyBank Center fell into nervous energy.
  • Buffalo dominated the middle. Through 2 periods, the Sabres had a 56-26 advantage in shot attempts at all strengths and a 28-17 advantage in 5v5 scoring chances. In the second period alone, 19-8 chance advantage. Per Athletic: "If not for a few highlight-reel saves from Dobes, the Sabres could have been leading after two periods."
  • The Dobes save on Thompson was the structural pivot of the game. End of 2nd period. Thompson wrist shot from point-blank range. Dobes denied it. Score stayed 2-1 entering the third. Earlier in the period: pair of saves on Tuch. The 37-save night that mattered.
  • Greenway made it 2-1 in the second on a Samuelsson shot that deflected in off his skate. Buffalo's possession dominance had to produce a goal somewhere — that was it.
  • Dahlin tied it 6:27 of the third. Quick-release wrist shot, the kind that makes him one of the league's most dangerous offensive defensemen. By midway through the 3rd: Buffalo had a 33-12 shot attempt advantage and 15-6 scoring chance advantage during Dahlin's 5v5 minutes alone. He had a 5-point night in Game 6 AND was all over the ice in Game 7. The Sabres captain finally arrived, but his team still lost.
  • The Newhook OT winner. 11:22 into overtime. Evans setup. "I wasn't sure if Jake touched it, so I kind of looked at him to see if he was going to celebrate or not. It's a crazy feeling. A lot of emotion, obviously. It was a war all series long." His goal sent Montreal to the Eastern Conference Final.
  • The historic context: Newhook becomes the SECOND player in NHL history to score multiple Game 7 series-clinching goals in a single postseason. The first was Nathan Horton in 2011 — the year Boston won the Stanley Cup. The Mr. May framing the publication assigned to Newhook on Day 10 just earned its historical anchor.
  • Both goaltenders overcame doubt entering Game 7. Dobes was pulled in Game 6 after 6 goals. Luukkonen was inconsistent all season. Dobes finished with 37 saves; Luukkonen with 22. Per Athletic: "Dobes was particularly strong during the Sabres' second-period push. Luukkonen stopped both Texier and Anderson on breakaways while making back-to-back saves on Caufield in the first to keep the Canadiens' lead at 2-0."
  • The Habs played a "Tampa Game 7" template. Per Basu: "Once the Canadiens went up 2-0 in the first, they seemed to sit back in a shell, content to defend and try to keep the Sabres to the outside while they possessed the puck in their zone for shift after shift." They beat Tampa in Game 7 with an NHL record-low 9 shots on goal. Tonight they let Buffalo possess but stayed structurally tight. Different teams, same Habs blueprint.

What This Was
The publication's "belief" thesis at its purest. Montreal was outshot, outchanced, and out-territoried for 40 minutes by a Buffalo team that had played its best hockey of Round 2 since Game 6. They won anyway. Because they got a 2-0 first-period lead that took the crowd out, because Dobes made 37 saves including a sensational denial on Thompson, and because Newhook did what Newhook now does in Game 7 — score the deciding goal in overtime. This was not the prettier game, but it was the only game that mattered.
Buffalo's Reality
Per Ruff postgame: "This one game doesn't define our season for us." The Sabres won the Atlantic Division, ended a 15-year playoff drought, won 5 of 6 road games this postseason, and pushed Montreal to a Game 7 OT after being down 3–2. They fell to 2–5 at home in the playoffs — the season-defining statistical anomaly. Ruff is on an expiring deal. Alex Tuch is on an expiring deal. Buffalo's offseason just became one of the most consequential in the league.
Dahlin's Series
5-point night in Game 6. Dominant 5v5 metrics in Game 7. The tying goal at 6:27 of the third. The Sabres captain put up a series of his career — the same player who lost in 4 straight Game 7s at the World Championship level and finally got a real NHL playoff Game 7 in his hometown's loudest building. He played as well as a captain can. His team still lost. Sometimes the structural truth of playoff hockey is exactly that brutal.

Seven games, seven different stories, and a road team that won 5 of them. The series produced one of the strangest home-vs-road inversions in recent playoff memory. Both teams played freer on the road, less burdened emotionally, and the structural truth of the matchup was that whichever team was at home tended to play worse. Game 7 in Buffalo confirmed it. Montreal hit the road, played a Tampa-Game-7 template, got 37 saves from Dobes, and survived.

§ 03

The storylines.

Newhook's two-Game-7 record. Dobes' redemption from the Game 6 pulling. Dahlin's series of his career. Montreal's never-lose-back-to-back identity. The road-warrior anomaly. Buffalo's offseason of decisions.

Storyline · Historic

Newhook joins Horton.

Alex Newhook becomes the second player in NHL history to score multiple Game 7 series-clinching goals in a single postseason. Round 1: Game 7 OT winner in Tampa to eliminate the Lightning. Round 2: Game 7 OT winner in Buffalo to eliminate the Sabres. The first player to do this was Nathan Horton in 2011 — the year Boston won the Stanley Cup. The historical parallel writes itself. The publication's Day 10 framing called Newhook "Mr. May." That earned an asterisk tonight: now confirmed, with a historical anchor that places him in Cup-winner territory.

Storyline · Redemption

Dobes took it personal.

Pulled in Game 6 after 6 goals. Then made 37 saves in Game 7, including the season-defining stop on Tage Thompson at the end of the second period. Postgame: "Me getting pulled at home was kind of a wake-up call, and I took it personal. And we won today, so I'm really happy." The Day 13 "Trust" framing for St. Louis's faith in Dobes has now compounded across two series. Tampa Game 7: 25 saves of brilliance. Buffalo Game 7: 37 saves of grit. The Habs' rookie netminder is now structurally inseparable from this run.

Storyline · Identity

Never back-to-back.

Montreal never lost back-to-back games the entire postseason. 6-0 after losses. That's not a fluke statistic. That's structural identity. Every time they got punched — blown out 8-3 in Game 6, goalie pulled in Game 6, momentum swings, hostile road buildings — they stabilized emotionally instead of unraveling. This is the dividing line the analyst flagged: between talented teams and legitimate contenders. The Habs just earned the second category.

Storyline · Captain

Dahlin's series of his life.

5-point night in Game 6. Tying goal in Game 7. Dominant 5v5 metrics across both elimination games. Per Athletic: "Nobody on this team has been waiting longer to play in these moments than Dahlin, and he saved his best stuff for the biggest moments of Buffalo's season." The Sabres captain's transformation from steady defensive anchor to series-defining offensive driver is the editorial Buffalo gets to take into next year. He just isn't quite enough on his own — which is the structural reality of every captain whose team doesn't have championship depth around him.

Storyline · Roster Math

Buffalo's offseason of decisions.

The Sabres won the Atlantic Division. Made the playoffs for the first time in 15 years. Pushed Montreal to a Game 7 overtime. And now both Lindy Ruff and top-six winger Alex Tuch are on expiring deals. The structural question for Buffalo's front office: do you double down on the Ruff-led identity that just took you to within a single OT goal of the Eastern Conference Final, or do you reset? The publication's read: keep Ruff, keep Tuch, address the top-line minus-record problem, and run it back. This team was much closer to ECF than the final score suggests.

Storyline · Pattern

The road-warrior anomaly.

Final series totals: Road team won 5 of 7 games. Combined Montreal + Buffalo: 11–3 on the road this postseason, 4–8 at home. The publication has been tracking this since Day 13. It got stranger every game. Per Ruff: "It was strange in the East, the disparity with the home and road." The structural reality: both teams played freer on the road than at home, less burdened by expectation. This is now one of the strangest playoff trends of recent memory — and the only series in modern NHL history where the road team won EVERY game of a 7-game series didn't quite happen, but it came shockingly close.

Eastern Conference Final · Thursday in Raleigh

Carolina · East #1

Carolina vs Montreal

The most structurally dominant team left. Relentless forecheck. Layered pressure. Elite shot suppression. Per analyst: "They don't need chaos to score. They can win low-event, territorial grind, transition, or special teams. That versatility is why many see them as Cup favorites."

  • 8–0 postseason. Only team with a perfect record left.
  • 13 days rest by ECF Game 1 — right at the rust threshold.
  • Stankoven 7G · co-leading playoff goal-scorer entering ECF.
  • The "earn a seat" structural pivot the publication's been tracking since Round 1.
Montreal · East #4

The belief team

The structural opposite of Carolina. Opportunistic. Emotional. Road warriors. Young. Per analyst: "They don't overwhelm opponents — they survive them and then strike. They've become this postseason's belief team. Nobody thought they'd survive Tampa. Nobody thought they'd survive Buffalo."

  • Newhook 2nd in NHL history with multi-G7 OT clinchers.
  • Suzuki line finally producing 5v5 (G5 breakthrough).
  • Hutson Norris narrative still active across rounds.
  • Dobes 37 saves tonight after Game 6 pulling. Road-Game-7 redemption.

The structural matchup question: Can Montreal survive Carolina's discipline-driven pressure cycle? Buffalo pressured aggressively too, but often with emotional volatility and risk. Carolina pressures with discipline. That's different. Montreal's path: elite goaltending, survive early surges, capitalize on rush chances, drag games into coin-flip territory late. If Carolina dictates pace consistently, this could end quickly. If Montreal's belief identity holds, they're still dangerous.

Western Conference Final · Wednesday in Denver

Colorado · West #1

The complete team

Pressure-application identity. Per analyst: "When Colorado is rolling, opponents stop touching the puck." Speed through neutral zone. Transition attack. Elite top-end talent. Defense activating offensively. Ability to score in waves.

  • 16 different goal scorers in MIN series.
  • MacKinnon legacy-tier under pressure (G5 tying snipe).
  • Makar Norris-tier; right-arm injury concern.
  • Wedgewood/Blackwood goalie rotation working structurally.
Vegas · West #2

The precision team

Pressure-resistance identity. Per analyst: "They suffocate space, wear teams down physically, manage momentum swings, and force opponents into emotionally exhausting hockey. They are psychologically hard to eliminate."

  • Marner leading NHL playoff scoring at 18 pts.
  • Howden tied Gretzky's SHG record (3 in single postseason).
  • Hart structural anchor (.920+ across two rounds).
  • Stone potential return for Game 1.

The structural contrast: Colorado tries to overwhelm you. Vegas tries to slowly erase you. If Colorado's pace wins, the series tilts fast. If Vegas slows the game into trench warfare, it becomes dangerous for the Avs. The Athletic called this matchup "the real Stanley Cup Final in terms of pure hockey quality." It's also the publication's structural Cup favorite if both teams advance — pressure-application meets pressure-resistance in its purest bracket form.

⚠ Fatigue zone

The two extremes are in the East matchup. Carolina at 13 days rest is right in the historical rust-risk window (10+ days). Montreal at 2 days is in legitimate fatigue territory after 14 playoff games in 30 days. The argument for rest: healing time, fresh legs, mental reset, special teams prep. The argument for rust: timing loss, rhythm loss, mental softness, lack of game pressure. Historical data is genuinely mixed — Tampa 2020 won the Cup after 8 days off; the 2018 Predators struggled coming off 9 days. The structural truth: rest helps if you needed it (Carolina hasn't been pushed). Rust hurts if you've lost the rhythm of playoff hockey (Carolina has now played 0 games in 13 days). Whichever way Game 1 breaks Thursday, the publication will have its first read.

The Take
Belief is a strange thing to measure. It doesn't show up in shot attempt percentages or scoring chances or possession metrics — all of which Buffalo dominated tonight and across this series. What it does show up in is a team that never loses back-to-back games across 30 days and 14 playoff games. A goaltender pulled in Game 6 who makes 37 saves in Game 7. A 24-year-old named Newhook who becomes the second player in NHL history to score multiple Game 7 OT clinchers in a single postseason — joining Boston's Nathan Horton from the year the Bruins won the Stanley Cup. The Final Four is now locked. The Cup favorites are Colorado and Carolina, by every structural read. The publication has been saying that since Day 5. But the bracket has also produced two Conference Final teams whose path here was supposed to be impossible — Vegas's veteran precision overcoming Anaheim's youth, Montreal's belief overcoming Tampa and then Buffalo. Two Conference Finals in 48 hours. Pressure-application meets pressure-resistance in the West. Structural standard meets belief team in the East. The publication's Day 5 power rankings predicted exactly these four. Now we get to find out which one actually wins.
