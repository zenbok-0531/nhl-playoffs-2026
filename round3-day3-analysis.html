The Stranglehold.

Vegas became the first team all season to mount a third-period comeback against Colorado. The Avs were 45-0-0 when leading after two periods this year — including 4-0 in this postseason. That perfect record died at Ball Arena tonight. Eichel tied it midway through the third with a surgical wrist shot. Barbashev scored the GWG just over two minutes later off a Vegas forecheck-forced turnover. Colorado's PP managed ZERO shots on goal across two power plays. Both Stone and Makar still out. Vegas heads home with a 2–0 series lead — the first team all season to break Colorado's structural aura.

Colorado played better tonight than they did in Game 1. They generated more pressure. They had stretches of urgency and pace. They led 1-0 going into the third on a Ross Colton goal. And then Vegas calmly stole the game anyway. Jack Eichel tied it midway through the third with a surgical wrist shot — backed his defender off, delayed, used traffic in front of Wedgewood to disguise the release. Two minutes and twelve seconds later, Vegas's forecheck forced a Colorado turnover in the offensive zone, Eichel found Barbashev for a wrist shot that beat Wedgewood high. Dowd added an empty-netter. 3-1 Vegas. 2-0 series. Colorado's 45-0-0 record with a third-period lead this season is dead. Game 1 was Vegas outplaying Colorado. Game 2 was Vegas surviving Colorado playing better. Both ended with Vegas winning. That's the structural truth that should terrify the Avalanche: Vegas has now proven they can win in TWO completely different ways.

Round 3 · WCF · Game 2  ·  VGK Leads 2–0

Vegas at Colorado

Series Standing
Golden Knights 2 — Avalanche 0
  • Vegas 3, Colorado 1. VGK scorers: Eichel (TG, wrist shot high on Wedgewood midway through 3rd), Barbashev (GWG, off Vegas forecheck turnover, wrist shot over the blocker), Dowd (EN). COL: Colton (1st period, lucky bounce off a Burns point shot). Vegas leads series 2-0. Game 3 Sunday at T-Mobile Arena.
  • The Eichel tying goal was surgical playoff hockey. Per Hanifin: "He backed his defender off, then cut back and delayed, holding his release until the traffic entered Wedgewood's sightline, then fired it past the goalie." That's a captain showing why he's an elite playoff superstar — patience, spatial reading, goalie manipulation. The kind of goal championship-tier teams get from their best player in elimination-leverage moments.
  • The Barbashev GWG was the series in one play. Vegas forecheck forced an Avalanche turnover in the Colorado end. Eichel found Barbashev. Wrist shot from distance over Wedgewood's blocker and under the crossbar. That's the entire Vegas playoff identity in 4 seconds: forecheck → forced turnover → quick release → punished breakdown.
  • The Colorado PP was a structural catastrophe. ZERO shots on goal across two power plays. Only TWO shot attempts total. Without Makar quarterbacking, Colorado's puck movement slows, seam passes disappear, and Vegas's aggressive PK pressures entries and attacks passing lanes. Vegas has now killed 37-of-43 PPs this postseason (86%) — 4 of 5 in this series (80%).
  • The Marner-Howden SHG threat continues. Both threatened short-handed in Game 2. The publication's tracking of Howden's record-tying 3 SHG postseason continues — and Vegas's PK is now structurally one of the most aggressive in postseason hockey.
  • Colton was the Colorado bright spot. His shot from Burns gave the Avs a 1-0 lead late in the first. Bednar's framing of Colton: "There's a ferociousness to the way that he's playing right now. He's physical, he's hard and I think the urgency in his game on both sides of the puck is there."
  • The structural problem Bednar named without naming: Colorado's two most dangerous players in Games 1 and 2 have been Ross Colton and Logan O'Connor — bottom-six grinders. Not what you want from a team that has MacKinnon, Necas, Rantanen, Lehkonen, Landeskog, and the deepest forward roster in the league. Colorado needs its top-six driving the bus. Vegas is currently containing them.
  • The Bednar line blender accelerated. Open Game 2 with the usual top line (MacKinnon-Landeskog-Necas vs Eichel's line). 2nd period: Lehkonen up with MacKinnon-Landeskog, Necas down with Nelson and Nichushkin. 3rd period: Kadri spotted in top six. Per Bednar: "I won't wait as long to mix it up." That's a coach chasing matchups in real time. Not lacking confidence — but necessarily reactive.
  • The McNabb injury scare was real but brief. Laid a hip check late in the 1st, hunched over, hobbled down the tunnel. Sat out the entire 2nd period. Returned for the third. Theodore, Andersson, and Hanifin all took elevated minutes. Coghlan nearly doubled his Game 1 minutes — handled them well.
  • Manson left too. Crashed head-first into the boards while laying a hit on Barbashev in the 2nd. Returned for the third. Both defensemen toughed it out. The kind of veteran detail signaling "playing hurt" is now common across this matchup.
  • Wedgewood was forced to bail Colorado out early. Marner breakaway save (and the follow-up). Dorofeyev late in the 1st after a Kadri turnover. The Vegas forecheck still produced first-period breakaways even though Colorado generated more sustained pressure tonight. Vegas's pace simply doesn't go away.

What This Was
Vegas demonstrating Cup-level playoff versatility. Game 1 they won by being structurally cleaner. Game 2 they won by being more composed. The Athletic's analytical framing: "Right now, Vegas looks like the more complete playoff machine — deeper, calmer, structurally tighter, more emotionally composed, better defensively. Colorado still looks more explosive. But Vegas currently looks more sustainable over 7 games."
The Makar Hole
Two games in and the Makar absence is structurally crippling Colorado. PP collapse (zero shots on 2 PPs). Slower breakouts. Less dynamic transition. Inability to get shots through from the point. Per Lazerus: "That's obviously a specialty of Makar's, and has been missed badly through the first two games of the series." Makar isn't just a great defenseman — he's Colorado's escape valve, transition engine, offensive activator, and pressure breaker. Without him, Colorado is genuinely a different team.
Eichel's Captaincy
The tying goal was Eichel's defining playoff moment so far. Backed the defender off. Cut back and delayed. Used traffic to disguise the release. That's not a freelance superstar moment — that's a captain reading the moment with surgical precision. Stone is missing; Eichel has elevated. Marner is the NHL playoff points leader. Dorofeyev is the NHL playoff goal leader. Howden's tied Gretzky's SHG record. Vegas's top-six pile is structurally elite.
Series Outlook · Updated
Vegas leads 2-0 with the series shifting to T-Mobile Arena. Game 3 Sunday is functionally a survival game for Colorado. If Vegas wins, the series effectively becomes a 4-game closeout assignment. If Colorado wins, the door reopens — but only with Makar returning. Both Stone and Makar are getting closer; neither was healthy enough for Game 2. The next 48 hours of medical updates may decide the rest of the series.

The Take
For 82 regular-season games and 9 playoff games, Colorado had built an aura. Wire-to-wire Presidents' Trophy winner. 45-0-0 when leading after two periods. Best record in the NHL by a wide margin. Tonight Vegas became the first team all year to mount a third-period comeback against them — and they did it without Stone, in Colorado's building, on a night Colorado played better than Game 1. That's the structural reality the Avalanche have to face: there is no game style they've shown they can use to beat this Vegas team. Game 1 they got outplayed. Game 2 they outplayed Vegas and lost anyway. The Cup favorite for 8 months has now been outclassed in 2 different ways in 48 hours. Makar's return is the only obvious lifeline. Without him, Colorado's PP doesn't function, breakouts slow down, and Vegas's forecheck compounds across every shift. With him, the matchup math reverses immediately. The Cup conversation 72 hours ago was Colorado vs Carolina. Tonight it's Vegas vs everybody. Game 3 Sunday at T-Mobile Arena is now functionally a Colorado survival game — and Vegas hasn't lost at home this postseason.
