Machine.

Carolina was 113 points in the regular season — the Eastern Conference leader. Lost Game 1 to Montreal 6-2 (with 12 days of rust). Then won four straight, the last two by a combined score of 10-1. The Habs' offense disappeared completely — managed ONE goal across the final two games. Per the analyst: "Carolina looked less like a hockey team and more like a system operating exactly as designed." That's the editorial truth of this series: Carolina is a machine. The Stanley Cup Final matchup the publication's Day 5 power rankings predicted in Round 2 — Carolina vs Vegas — is now confirmed.

§ 01

The series in five moments.

Carolina lost Game 1 with 12 days of rust and structural exposure. Then won four straight by progressively increasing margins — until the final two games were 4-0 and 6-1. The arc of a contender finding its identity in real time.

G1 · The Counter · May 21

Montreal exposed it.

MTL 6 · CAR 2

4 unanswered Habs goals in 11 minutes. 10 first-period HDCs (most CAR allowed any period all postseason). 12 days of rust + real structural exposure. Rust dissipated. Structural seam was real.

G2 · The Reset · May 23

Carolina restored it.

CAR 3 · MTL 2 OT

26-12 shot advantage. Hard-match Staal vs Suzuki worked — top line 1 SC, 4 SOG in regulation. Ehlers 2G inc OT GWG off Jankowski feed. Identity restored — but still needed OT.

G3 · Bell Centre · May 25

Identity traveled.

CAR def. MTL

Carolina takes a 2-1 series lead in Habs' building. The structural truth crystallized: Carolina's pressure works regardless of venue. Montreal's belief identity began to crack on home ice for the first time.

The full machine.

3 goals in 2:47. 43-18 SOG. MTL had 1 SOG over 32:57 of game time. Bell Centre chanted "Shoot the puck!" — St. Louis: "They're not wrong." Men against boys.

§ 02

The Olé moment.

Lenovo Center borrowed the Bell Centre chant midway through Game 5's second period. By the closing minutes, they had added their own: "We want the Cup." Two chants that capture the editorial truth of this series.

Lenovo Center · Game 5 · Olé olé olé olé

Carolina fans borrowed Montreal's chant and made it theirs.

Midway through the 2nd period of Game 5, with Carolina leading 4-0, Lenovo Center fans started chanting "Olé, olé olé olé." That chant is the trademark of the Bell Centre crowd — the rhythmic celebratory call that Habs fans use when their team has a game in hand. Two nights earlier, the Bell Centre had chanted "Shoot the puck!" at their own team. Tonight, Carolina's crowd took the Habs' signature chant for themselves.

By the closing minutes, they had added a new chant: "We want the Cup." That's the editorial perfection of Game 5 — Carolina's fans not just celebrating but consciously claiming the cultural symbols of the opponent. The Habs' belief identity had carried them through Tampa and Buffalo. Tonight that identity was stolen by Carolina's crowd in a coronation game.

Per the Athletic: "To the victors go the spoils — and fans at Lenovo Center were more than willing to borrow the trademark chant from the Bell Centre in Montreal, early and often, through a game that turned into an extended coronation for the Eastern Conference champs."

Sometimes you gotta find the body blows to get to the head. When you can stack shifts and set up the other guys for success, good things will continue to happen. — Logan Stankoven · post-Game 4 · captured the entire series

The Stankoven framing from Day 6 captured the entire series structurally: Games 1-3 were body blows. Game 4 was the head shot. Game 5 was the kneel-down — the controlled possession of a series that the opponent could no longer threaten. Carolina won this series the way machines win — by repeating the same correct action until the opponent broke.

§ 03

The machine parts.

Forecheck. Veterans. Depth. Goaltending. The hard-match defensive identity. K'Andre Miller. Every piece of Carolina's structural identity earned its place in this series — and is now ready for Vegas.

The Hall-Stankoven-Blake Line

43+ combined points · the engine.

All three at 5+ playoff goals. All three on the scoresheet in Game 5 (Hall 3 pts, Stankoven goal, Blake goal). The publication tracked this line since Day 2 — when its Day 1 framing as "58% of CAR R1-R2 goals" was challenged by the ECF G1 Habs win. By Game 5 they were the structural engine of Carolina's Cup Final clinch.

Logan Stankoven · Breakout

9 playoff goals · team lead.

Carolina's missing offensive game-breaker arrived at the right time. Per the analyst: "This series may have been his national breakout." Stankoven's Day 6 "body blows" quote captured the entire series identity. His 9 playoff goals lead the team — Carolina's structural model finally has a finisher to match its system.

Frederik Andersen · Goaltending

Conn Smythe candidate through 2 rounds.

.950 SV% in R1-R2 (.946 on high-danger, 15+ saves above expected). Dropped to .846 vs Montreal but didn't need to be Conn Smythe-elite — Carolina protected him. Game 5: 23 of 24. Per Gentille: "His size and steady demeanor are some of his greatest strengths." Played Game 5 in the shadow of personal loss — his longtime agent Claude Lemieux passed the day before.

K'Andre Miller · The Offseason Bet

Carolina's defensive activation.

Offseason NYR acquisition (1st + 2nd + Scott Morrow). Primary assist on Staal Game 4 GWG. Per the Athletic: "Erases scoring chances by all three of Carolina's postseason opponents." Elite skating + reach. The structural piece that may make Carolina Cup-Final-ready against Vegas's transition forwards.

The Forecheck

Best in hockey · broke Montreal completely.

Montreal had 7 failed zone exits in opening 8 minutes of G4. ONE SOG over 32:57 of game time across G3-G4. ONE goal across the final two games. The structural pattern: pressure every retrieval, force turnovers, create exhaustion, deny clean exits. Vegas will face the same pressure starting June 2.

4-Line Depth

Carolina's defining identity.

The 4th line (Robinson-Jankowski-Carrier) tilted the ice in G2 (60% xG share, OT shifts) and produced G5's Robinson breakaway goal. Consistently rolling four groups of forwards is a major part of Carolina's identity. That depth pressure wears teams down over long series. Vegas has similar depth — the deepest-team-wins debate is now structurally the Cup Final question.

Carolina by the numbers

  • 113 regular-season points — Eastern Conference leader. Carolina entered the playoffs as the East's best team and is finishing the playoffs as the East's confirmed best team. The structural read held across 13 playoff games.
  • 13-1 playoff record. Swept Ottawa (4-0). Swept Philadelphia (4-0). Lost Game 1 to Montreal. Won four straight to clinch. The most dominant non-Vegas playoff run of 2026.
  • First Eastern Conference title since 2006. That 2006 team won the Stanley Cup. The structural symbolism isn't lost on Carolina's veterans — Staal in particular has waited 20 years for this conference final breakthrough.
  • Scored first in all 5 ECF games. The pattern that proved Carolina's identity wasn't about reactionary play. Setting the terms of every game shift from puck drop.
§ 04

Vegas waits.

The publication's Round 2 Day 5 power rankings predicted Carolina + Colorado + Vegas + Montreal as the Final Four. Colorado was wrong. The other three were right. The Cup Final the publication has been pointing toward since Round 2 is now confirmed: Vegas vs Carolina, June 2.

The Day 5 Round 2 power rankings ("Maturity") called Carolina + Colorado + Vegas + Montreal as the Final Four. Colorado was wrong (got swept by Vegas in the WCF). The other three made it. The Cup Final the publication has been pointing toward since early Round 2 — Vegas vs Carolina — is now confirmed.

The structural truth: Carolina is the Eastern Conference Final winner. Vegas is the Western Conference Final winner. These are arguably the two most complete teams left in the postseason — not the flashiest, not the most talented, but the deepest, most structurally sound, and most playoff-ready.

Per the analyst's Cup Final preview: "Carolina tries to win every shift. Vegas tries to win every moment. That's the Stanley Cup Final."

Game 1 is June 2 in Raleigh — Carolina has home-ice advantage as the higher seed (113 regular-season points vs Vegas's record). The full Cup Final preview is now available.

The Take
Carolina played 13 games in the 2026 postseason. They won 12 of them. The only loss was Game 1 of the ECF, when 12 days of rust combined with Montreal's structural answer to produce a 6-2 home defeat. From that moment forward, Carolina played the most complete and disciplined playoff hockey of any team in the league. They restored identity in Game 2. Traveled it in Game 3. Suffocated Montreal in Game 4 (43-18 SOG). And coronated themselves in Game 5 — with Lenovo Center fans borrowing the Bell Centre's signature "Olé olé" chant and adding "We want the Cup." Per the analyst: "Carolina looked less like a hockey team and more like a system operating exactly as designed." That's the editorial truth. Carolina is a machine.
