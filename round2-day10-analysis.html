The Field.

Sunday gave the bracket its answer to Day 9's separation. Montreal beat Buffalo 6–2 at Bell Centre to take a 2–1 series lead. Anaheim beat Vegas 4–3 at Honda Center to even theirs at 2–2. Both home teams answered. Hutson delivered a Norris-caliber game; Sennecke scored for the third straight night. The 7-goal playoff lead is now a three-way tie. Carolina remains alone at 8–0, but every other series is real, and every other series just got a team that hit back.

Day 9 was about Carolina separating from the bracket. Day 10 is the parallel inverse — the field below Carolina answering. Sunday afternoon, Montreal beat Buffalo 6–2 at Bell Centre on the strength of a Lane Hutson masterclass, three slump-busters (Caufield, Slafkovsky, Thompson on the Buffalo side), and Alex Newhook's continuing playoff breakout — five goals in his last four games. Sunday night, Anaheim beat Vegas 4–3 at Honda Center to even the series, breaking what had been an elite Vegas penalty kill (26-for-27 entering, 11-for-11 against Anaheim in this series) with two second-unit PP goals. Both home teams won. Both losing teams from Day 6's inversions just got their answer. Brett Howden scored to join the playoff goal lead — making it a three-way tie at 7 with Boldy (MIN) and Stankoven (CAR). Carolina remains alone at 8–0. But every other series in the bracket has a team that just hit back, and the bracket has refused to let one team run away with it.

§ 01

Hutson drove it.

The Athletic's analyst called Lane Hutson "arguably the best player on the ice." Caufield and Slafkovsky broke out of slumps in the same period. Newhook continued his run. Buffalo's identity got inverted in the building they couldn't quiet.

Round 2 · Series 03 · East  ·  MTL Takes Lead

Buffalo at Montreal

Series Standing
Canadiens 2 — Sabres 1
  • Montreal 6, Buffalo 2 at Bell Centre. Habs scorers: Caufield (PP, slump-buster), Bolduc, Slafkovsky (PP, slump-buster), Dach, Newhook (2 G, including EN). Tage Thompson opened scoring 53 seconds in (his own 7-game drought-buster); Dahlin added a PP goal. Habs lead series 2–1. Game 4 Tuesday in Montreal.
  • The structural truth of the night: Lane Hutson was the best player on the ice. Two assists, but the impact was bigger — deception at the blue line, puck retrievals, escape under pressure, quarterbacking the PP. The Caufield setup — deking around a stickless Jordan Greenway, delaying perfectly, dropping a pass into a wide open lane — is Norris-caliber playoff highlight material.
  • Three slump-busters on the same night. Caufield broke a 6-game goal drought (first of the series). Slafkovsky broke a slump on a PP deflection. Thompson broke a 7-game drought 53 seconds in. All three are scoring stars who needed it. Montreal's two top young scorers and Buffalo's top finisher all delivering in the same night says the offensive talent is showing up; structure is the question.
  • Alex Newhook continues to be Montreal's breakout playoff narrative: 2 goals tonight (one EN). Five total in his last four games. Per the Athletic analysis, his speed is creating real matchup problems for Buffalo's larger D corps — Logan Stanley specifically called out as a slow-recovery target. Newhook is attacking gaps before Buffalo's defensive layers can set.
  • The PP finally clicked. Habs went 2-for-5 on the man advantage — both Caufield and Slafkovsky goals came with the extra man. After two games of a quiet PP, Montreal's structural identity (puck-movement, Hutson at the point, Caufield as triggerman) finally showed up. Buffalo's PK lost structure repeatedly: overcommitting low, failing to clear screens, leaving weak-side seams.
  • The pivotal moment: Beck Malenstyn ran over Dobes in front of the Montreal net, triggering a massive scrum and a Habs power play. Slafkovsky's deflection followed. Buffalo's bottom-six aggression, their structural identity through Round 1, just turned into the penalty that put the game out of reach.
  • Buffalo's identity inversion is the deeper truth. The Sabres are best when they transition quickly through the middle and selectively activate defensemen. Tonight they had to chase, force offense, and got hemmed in their own zone. They came into Round 2 as the team imposing in Game 1. Two games later, they're the team being imposed on.
  • Buffalo lost their first road game of the postseason. They were strong on the road through Round 1 vs Florida and split Games 1–2 at home. Alex Lyon allowed 6 on 32 shots — his second straight loss after surrendering 5 in Game 2. The "Lyon as structural anchor" framing from Round 1 is genuinely in question.
  • The Bell Centre returned. First full second-round atmosphere in years. Kirk Muller carried the torch. The crowd factor was real — Montreal played emotionally but mostly controlled; Buffalo unraveled for stretches with retaliatory penalties and chaotic defensive coverage.

The Hutson Storyline
This is now a Norris-tier playoff conversation. Hutson has primary assists on goals in both Game 2 and Game 3. The Caufield setup tonight, deking around a stickless defender and delaying for the slot pass, is the kind of play that wins postseason awards. The publication has been tracking Hutson since Day 8 as a playmaking force; tonight he was named the best player on the ice in a 6–2 Montreal win. He's a second-year defenseman driving series in his first deep playoff run.
Buffalo's Problem
Buffalo's identity has flipped from Game 1's imposer to Game 3's chaser. They cannot trade chances with Montreal in this building, and they have to win Game 4 to avoid going down 3–1 with Game 5 back in Buffalo as the only thing keeping their season alive. The Lyon question is now legitimate (two straight losses, 11 goals against). The PK lost structure repeatedly. The team that came into Round 2 with the structural identity advantage has now lost it.
The Newhook Track
Five playoff goals in his last four games. Speed differential vs Buffalo's larger D corps is the structural matchup truth. The Athletic's analytical breakdown specifically called out Logan Stanley as a slow-recovery target Newhook keeps beating. Newhook has the Tampa G7 winner from Round 1, the 2-goal Game 2 in this series, and now 2 more goals in Game 3. He has gone from "Mr. May potential" to "Mr. May confirmed" inside a week.
Series Outlook · Updated
Game 4 Tuesday is now a structural test for Buffalo. Win or fall to 3–1. If they lose, Game 5 in Buffalo becomes a literal elimination game. If they win, the series resets at 2–2 with home ice back. The bracket and the publication's read both pivot meaningfully on that game.
§ 02

Anaheim broke it.

Vegas came in with an elite PK: 26-for-27 overall, 11-for-11 vs Anaheim. The Ducks scored two power-play goals from their second unit and turned Vegas's physicality into penalty trouble. Quenneville's adjustments worked. Sennecke scored for a third straight night.

Round 2 · Series 04 · West  ·  Series Tied 2–2

Vegas at Anaheim

Series Standing
Knights 2 — Ducks 2
  • Anaheim 4, Vegas 3. Ducks scorers: Sennecke (PP, 3rd straight game), Granlund, Killorn (PP), Ian Moore. Vegas: Dorofeyev, Howden (his 7th, ties for NHL playoff goal lead), Hertl (late, 6-on-5). Series tied 2–2. Game 5 Tuesday at T-Mobile.
  • Anaheim never trailed. Sennecke opened scoring at 8:43 of the first on the PP. Got the first goal for just the third time in 10 postseason games. For a team that has had to chase most nights, leading from start was a structural shift.
  • The big one: Anaheim finally broke Vegas's elite PK. Vegas entered the game 26-for-27 overall on the PK, 11-for-11 against Anaheim in this series. Two Anaheim PP goals tonight — both from the SECOND unit (Sennecke, then Killorn). That's not a fluke. That's a unit that found something in video work and executed.
  • The Ducks finally made Vegas pay for the physicality. Vegas's penalty kill had been their structural answer to absorbing chippy aggressive play. Tonight, with Vegas committing both takeable penalties (Coghlan interference, Smith slashing) and reckless ones (McNabb cross-checked Cutter Gauthier head-first into the boards from behind), Anaheim cashed in on the safer ones. Vegas couldn't freely finish every check, and the Ducks gained sustained zone time.
  • Quenneville's lineup adjustments worked. Olen Zellweger made his playoff debut (15th different Ducks player to debut this postseason), paired with Ian Moore (moved back from RW to D in place of injured Helleson). Mason McTavish back in. Zellweger had a secondary assist on Moore's 4–2 goal. The blueline got more activation. The transition game got cleaner. Three changes, immediate result.
  • Beckett Sennecke continues to elevate. Three straight games with a goal. Calder Trophy finalist after a 23-goal, 60-point regular season. Opened scoring on the PP, where he beat Hart after multiple opportunities on the first advantage. His timing on entries, quick releases, and composure around traffic are unusually mature for a rookie in playoff intensity.
  • Vegas's structural issue: The Eichel top line struggled without Mark Stone. Stone is the connective tissue — puck recovery, defensive support, sustained zone possession. Without him, Eichel spent more time defending. Eichel has 13 points (tied with Marner for team lead), but only one goal. Per the Athletic: "For this team to advance, they will eventually need him to be the puck-dominator he's proven to be in the past."
  • The animosity has officially escalated. McNabb's head-first cross-check on Gauthier from behind was a moment Round 2 hasn't seen since Pittsburgh-Carolina-style chippy series in past playoffs. Coghlan interference, Sennecke cross-checking minor, retaliation penalties, post-whistle scrums. The first three games were relatively calm. Tonight crossed into playoff-hatred territory, and Anaheim looked energized by chaos rather than intimidated.
  • Tortorella chose Saad over Reilly Smith to replace Stone. Saad hadn't played since April 11. No SOG tonight. Vegas's third line (Saad-Hertl-Kolesar) played the fewest minutes of any line and was outshot 4–1. Hertl scored late on the 6-on-5 to provide goal-line value. The third line as constructed didn't work tonight.

What This Was
Anaheim's emotional and tactical reset. They couldn't go down 3–1 at home with two games on the road to follow. The Athletic's read: they "answered with urgency, speed, and special teams execution." The Ducks went from chasing the series to leading the conversation in one night.
The Sennecke Storyline
Sennecke is becoming a star inside the postseason. Three straight games with a goal. A Calder Trophy finalist with 23 G/60 pts as a rookie. Now playing like a long-term franchise piece in his first deep playoff run. What stands out is how quickly he processes offensive space — soft ice on entries, quick releases, composure around traffic. Unusually mature offensive instincts under playoff pressure. The Ducks have multiple young players (Sennecke, Gauthier, Zellweger) elevating simultaneously.
Vegas's Mark Stone Problem
Stone is the connective tissue of the top line. Without him, Eichel and the wingers spent more time defending than attacking. Eichel had 2 assists tonight but no 5v5 dominance. Tortorella's quote: "Stone is a big part of our team. We miss him, period, as far as his presence and as far as his play." Vegas has survived without his A-game from Eichel because Marner and Howden have been so strong — but for them to advance, Eichel has to take over a game. He hasn't yet.
Series Outlook · Best of Three
Game 5 Tuesday at T-Mobile is now the pivot. Both teams have proven they can win in the other's building (Vegas in Anaheim, Anaheim in Vegas). The Stone return timeline is the biggest variable. If he comes back for Game 5, Vegas's top line is whole again. If not, Anaheim's pace and depth advantage carry. Best-of-three from here on.

What's next

  • Monday · Colorado at Minnesota, Game 4. Wild down 2–1, at home in St. Paul. Eriksson Ek's return status is the structural variable. Bednar's goalie choice (Wedgewood or Blackwood) is a meaningful signal. If Wild win, series ties 2–2 and Carolina's parallel-streak partner officially exits 30-year company forever. If Avs win, they go 3–1 with Game 5 in Denver to close it.
  • Tuesday · Buffalo at Montreal, Game 4. Sabres down 2–1, must win or fall to 3–1. Habs trying to take a stranglehold with Game 5 back in Buffalo on the schedule. The Lyon question, the Newhook speed thread, and the Hutson Norris narrative all converge.
  • Tuesday · Vegas vs Anaheim, Game 5 at T-Mobile. Tied 2–2. Best-of-three for the West semifinal spot. Stone return timeline is the question — if he plays Tuesday, Vegas's top line is whole again. If not, Anaheim's pace and depth advantage carry.
  • The bracket reality: Carolina rests through Tuesday at minimum while the other three series play through. The Conference Final won't begin until at least mid-week next week. Whoever advances will face a team that hasn't played in 7–10 days.
The Take
Day 9 was the separation. Day 10 is the refusal. Below Carolina, the bracket is alive. Every series in Round 2 now has a team that just hit back: Minnesota broke Colorado's streak, Montreal took the lead on Buffalo, Anaheim evened on Vegas. The publication has been tracking "identity is provisional" as a Round 2 thesis since Day 6. Tonight made it permanent. Carolina is in the rarest 30-year company in hockey, alone at 8–0. The other seven teams have all been the better team in at least one game this round. The bracket has refused to let one team run away with it. That's the structural answer to Carolina's separation — not a challenger yet, but a field that has decided it isn't going quietly.
