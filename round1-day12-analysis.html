§ 02

Two closeouts, two validations.

Minnesota validated the Tier 1 disruptor read by dominating Dallas at 5v5. Anaheim validated their tier evolution by closing Edmonton like a veteran team. Round 2 Bound jumps from 3 to 5 in a single night.

Series 02 · West  ·  Advanced

Dallas at Minnesota

Series Final
Wild 4 — Stars 2
  • Minnesota 5, Dallas 2. Quinn Hughes scored twice including the third-period game-winner after Dallas had tied it. Boldy added two empty-netters to seal it (his fifth and sixth of the series). Tarasenko scored his 50th career playoff goal. The Wild win their first playoff series since 2015.
  • The trade that decided this series: Hughes was the December trade-deadline acquisition from Vancouver — described at the time as the biggest trade in Wild franchise history. He scored the series-winning goal. Bill Guerin's mid-season bet just paid for itself in full.
  • The structural truth nobody can ignore: Minnesota outscored Dallas 17–5 at even strength in the series, including 12–4 at five-on-five. Dallas's elite power play (10 PP goals, league high from Johnston in the postseason) couldn't overcome a foundation that broken. That's the Tier 1 collapse the publication flagged Day 10.
  • Wallstedt finished the series with another calm 22-save performance. The rookie goalie was Minnesota's swing performer all six games — never spectacular, never broken. Exactly the kind of goaltending that wins playoff series for teams without star firepower.
  • Boldy ends Round 1 with six goals — second only to Brandon Hagel's playoff lead from Tampa. His Game 4 OT winner, Game 5 PP go-ahead, and Game 6 doubles cemented him as Minnesota's clutch factor. Same story all series: when the moment narrowed, Boldy delivered.
  • Dallas's exit raises the obvious question: were they ever Tier 1, or did their profile mask their flaws? Robertson scored in 5 of 6 games. Johnston led the league in PP goals. Heiskanen produced. None of it mattered because Dallas got outscored 12–4 at 5v5. The publication had Dallas in Tier 1 (under pressure) Day 9 and slid them to Tier 2 Day 10. Day 12 confirms they didn't belong in either.
Minnesota outscored Dallas 17–5 at even strength in the series, including 12–4 at five-on-five. — Associated Press
What This Was
A team that hadn't won a series in over a decade beating a three-time Western Conference Finalist on structural superiority. Minnesota didn't get hot. They didn't get lucky. They controlled 5v5 hockey for six games against a team that was supposed to control them. That's not an upset — that's a tier reveal.
What This Says
The Hughes trade was a contender move, not a competitive one. Bill Guerin saw a window and bought into it. Hughes, Tarasenko, Zuccarello — three veterans who add the kind of situational maturity a young team needs. The Wild were built to do exactly this, and Day 10's Tier 1 disruptor read was the right one.
Round 2 Awaits
Minnesota gets Colorado — the Presidents Trophy winner with 8+ days of rest. Tier 1 vs Tier 1. Both teams won Round 1 by controlling 5v5 play. The difference: Colorado swept; Minnesota took six games. The Avalanche have rest. The Wild have momentum. That's the most interesting Round 2 matchup in the West, and possibly the entire bracket.
Round 1 Verdict
The Wild are real. They controlled a series against a Tier 1 profile team and never got knocked off their structure. Hughes was the difference-maker the trade promised. Wallstedt held when needed. Boldy delivered at every key moment. Three teams have advanced through Round 1 by playing the same kind of hockey: Carolina, Colorado, Philadelphia. Minnesota just made it four.
Series 08 · West  ·  Advanced

Edmonton at Anaheim

Series Final
Ducks 4 — Oilers 2
  • Anaheim 5, Edmonton 2. The Ducks won their first playoff series since 2017 with a three-goal first period that Edmonton never recovered from. Poehling, Kreider, and Gauthier (PP) put it 3–1 by intermission. Carlsson sealed it with an empty-netter. Round 2 Bound: 5.
  • The series turning point came late in the second period: Hyman appeared to score to make it 3–2, but the goal was disallowed for a distinct kicking motion. Less than 50 seconds later, Carlsson and Terry broke out on a 2-on-1 against Walman; Terry beat Ingram for 4–1. Edmonton's best comeback chance turned into a two-goal Anaheim lead in under a minute.
  • The McDavid storyline that defined the series: another year without a championship for the best player in the world. Two straight Cup Final losses, now a Round 1 exit. McDavid was likely playing through the ankle injury sustained in Game 2. Edmonton was outshot and outchanced dramatically with him on at 5v5; he turned the puck over on the sequence that led to the Kreider goal. The Athletic noted Trouba and LaCombe were specifically tasked with limiting him, and *"once again, they succeeded."*
  • Cutter Gauthier's PP goal was the eighth Anaheim power-play goal in 16 opportunities — 50% conversion through Round 1. The Ducks finished as the most effective power play of the round, and it kept producing in elimination scenarios. That's a Tier 1 trait, not a Tier 2 one.
  • Anaheim's tier evolution is real: they didn't just win games on chaos anymore. Day 1 of this series they were running and gunning. Game 6 they protected a lead like a veteran team — clamped down defensively after going up early, limited McDavid and Draisaitl, controlled the middle of the ice. They've evolved from a chaos team to a complete playoff team mid-round.
  • The under-story: Mattias Ekholm had a series to forget. The 35-year-old defenseman who anchored Edmonton's regular season looked his age, victimized on the Kreider goal when he left the winger alone streaking down the right wing. The age question that seemed answered after a strong regular season is back open.
  • Joel Quenneville, in his first season as Anaheim's head coach: 20-6 in Game 6s, 7-1 in Game 6 closeouts. The Hall of Fame coach delivered exactly what his record predicted.
From there, the Ducks clamped down defensively and shut down a less-than-optimum McDavid and Leon Draisaitl. — The Athletic, Johnston/Stephens/Gentille
What This Was
A team that hadn't won a playoff series since 2017 closing the second-best player in the world's team in six. Anaheim broke Edmonton early, controlled game state, absorbed the third-period push, and closed like a veteran team. The Game 5 desperation Edmonton showed was the exception. Game 6 was Edmonton's series identity returning: stars not at full strength, structure inconsistent, no margin to absorb mistakes.
What This Says
Anaheim is now legitimately a Tier 1 candidate. They proved they can dictate pace AND protect leads — championship traits, not contender-aspirant traits. The Verbeek roster construction (the Zegras-for-Poehling trade looks like a steal in retrospect, even with Zegras thriving in Philly) is paying off in playoff hockey ways. The Ducks are dangerous in multiple game scripts. That's the same observation the publication made about Vegas Day 9.
McDavid's Reality
The transcendent superstar's playoff window keeps narrowing without a Cup. Two straight Cup Final losses to Florida. Now a Round 1 exit. Whether it was the Game 2 ankle injury, structural breakdowns, or both — Edmonton's ceiling has been the same problem for four straight playoff runs: when McDavid can't tilt the ice, the system doesn't have a backup plan. McDavid finished Round 1 as the second-most points scored when trailing in a series in NHL history. That number isn't going to comfort him.
Round 1 Verdict
Anaheim isn't a chaos team anymore. They're now a structurally credible Tier 1 candidate — depth scoring (Poehling, Carlsson, Terry, Kreider all contributing), elite power play, evolving defensive discipline, Hall of Fame coaching, and Dostal stabilizing in goal. Their Round 2 opponent (Vegas or Utah) is going to test whether the evolution is real or a Round 1 mirage. Either way, this team has earned the contender conversation.

§ 04

The patterns that held.

Round 1 is teaching one consistent lesson, and the four teams advancing have all demonstrated it in different ways.

PATTERN / 01

Structure beats profile.

  • Carolina swept Ottawa never trailing once — system before stars
  • Colorado swept LA controlling pace — pace before talent
  • Philly closed Pittsburgh in 1–0 OT — discipline before chaos
  • Minnesota dominated Dallas at 5v5 — foundation before flash
  • Four teams. Same template. Different rosters.
PATTERN / 02

Depth wins what stars can't.

  • Vegas: Howden's two SHGs (depth player decides games)
  • Carolina: Aho, Necas, Jarvis all contributing past Aho line
  • Minnesota: Hughes/Boldy/Tarasenko/Zuccarello — four-deep impact
  • Philly: Konecny driving but Sanheim and Couturier sealing
  • The teams that didn't have to ride one player are the ones still playing.
PATTERN / 03

Goaltending stability is the floor.

  • Wallstedt: rookie, calm, 6 games of stability
  • Vladar: held Pittsburgh to 0 goals in clinching Game 6
  • Andersen/Kochetkov: Carolina's two-headed shutdown
  • Blackwood: Colorado's 4-game lockdown
  • Every advancing team has a goalie who stops the right shots. None of them are riding a hot streak.
Rink Report · Obituary · For favourites that flame out

Edmonton Oilers.

Two-time Stanley Cup Finalists. 93 points. Home ice in the first round. Eliminated in six games by an Anaheim team that finished the regular season at minus-15. McDavid's window: two years left.
"We were an average team all year."
— Connor McDavid, after the Game 6 loss
The Cause of Death

McDavid's quote is the obituary in five words. The captain didn't say "unlucky." He didn't say "robbed." He said average. That cuts through every excuse — bounces, calls, injuries, scheduling — and names the structural truth. Edmonton finished with 93 points (their lowest in a full season since 2018-19) and still got home ice against a Ducks team at minus-15. The regular season told the truth in the end. Their level didn't match the noise around them.

Edmonton didn't lose this series in April. They lost it over three years of asset depletion, goaltending instability, and middle-six gambles that didn't pay. When McDavid couldn't tilt the ice, the system had no backup plan. Anaheim attacked the exact weaknesses three years of roster construction had created.

The injuries were worse than reported. Knoblauch revealed post-series that multiple players were playing through fractures and refused to come out of the lineup. McDavid's ankle (Game 2 onward), Draisaitl, Hyman, and the rotating absences of Dickinson and Henrique. A top-heavy roster with no insulation cannot absorb when the top isn't 100% — and Edmonton's top wasn't even close.

The Holloway/Broberg Loss

August 2024. St. Louis offer-sheeted both — quality young depth pieces that became important long-term core in St. Louis. Edmonton took 2nd and 3rd round picks as compensation. The exact kind of internal pipeline the Oilers have spent every offseason since trying to replace through trades.

The Skinner-for-Jarry Trade

December. Skinner + Kulak + 2nd round pick to Pittsburgh for Tristan Jarry. Jarry posted .857 SV% and lost the job. Skinner started Pittsburgh's Round 1. Kulak is playing top-four for Colorado. Three pieces out for one underperforming goaltender at $5.375M for two more years.

The Walman/Frederic Extensions

Trent Frederic, 7yr × $3.85M. Jake Walman, 8yr × $7M. Frederic was a healthy scratch against Anaheim. Walman has become "an all-defense bottom-sixer at a premium price." Long-term cap commitments locked in for short-term contributions that didn't materialize.

The Special Teams Collapse

Edmonton's identity has always been PP-driven. Against Anaheim, they lost the special teams battle decisively. Eight Anaheim PP goals on 16 chances. Edmonton couldn't draw enough penalties to fight back. When you live by special teams, that's a structural collapse.

The McDavid Clock

Connor McDavid signed his $25M two-year extension last October at below-market value, deliberately picking a 2028 expiry to keep the contention window open. This loss wastes one of those years. Two real shots remain. Per The Athletic: McDavid's burning desire to win in Edmonton remains, but he's "likely going to expect management to deliver on specific roster adjustments this summer, rather than accepting general promises."

What Has To Change

Goaltending solved, not patched. The list of summer options (Skinner returning, Andersen, Vanecek, Rittich, Bobrovsky, possibly a Binnington trade) carries no sure thing. Jarry's contract complicates everything.

Real middle-six scoring. Not depth fillers. Actual contributors who don't disappear when McDavid does.

Defensive mobility. Ekholm at 35 looked his age in this series. Nurse's $9.25M × 4 cap commitment with NMC is the elephant in the room. Per The Athletic: "If the Oilers could clear that cap commitment, it would free up money for other needs. They need to try."

Game management identity. Edmonton blew leads in three of four losses to Anaheim. Closing games is a skill the publication has identified as the trait separating contenders from contender-aspirants. Edmonton doesn't have it.

The Oilers are still built to win games. They are not consistently built to win series. Their captain just called them average. Once Connor McDavid says that, the front office doesn't get to hide from it.
— Edmonton Oilers · Eliminated Round 1 · April 30, 2026 · 14 wins from a Cup
The Take
Round 1 is one game from over. Five teams have advanced — Carolina, Colorado, Philly, Minnesota, Anaheim — and they all closed by playing the same kind of hockey. Different rosters, different conferences, different paths through Round 1, but the template is identical: control 5v5, get steady goaltending, score from depth, close when the moment narrows. Connor McDavid's window narrows again without a Cup. Anaheim emerges as a real Tier 1 candidate. Round 2 is going to be a clinic in which structural team is actually contender material — and based on Round 1, that question has at least five serious candidates.
— Round 1 · Day 12 · Filed after Minnesota and Anaheim both closed · Round 2 Bound: 5
