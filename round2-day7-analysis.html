The Standard.

The Carolina Hurricanes are 7–0 in the Stanley Cup Playoffs. That fact alone tells you most of what you need to know about Round 2. Tonight's Game 3 in Philadelphia was Carolina's fourth different version of a playoff win — power play, penalty kill, even strength, and goaltending all contributed in roughly equal measure. Andersen made the saves. Slavin made the stick play that prevented a multi-goal Philly first period. Chatfield scored the shorthanded GWG. Svechnikov scored his first of the playoffs. Three guys who had zero goals in their first six games combined for three tonight. Carolina has now demonstrated that every part of their roster can decide a game when needed. That's a championship trait. And the bracket has only one other team showing it.

§ 01

Carolina activated depth.

The Stankoven-Blake-Hall line had carried 4 of 6 goals through Game 2. Tonight, Staal, Svechnikov, and Chatfield — none of whom had scored in the first six playoff games — combined for three. That's a sweep in the making.

Round 2 · Series 01 · East  ·  Stranglehold

Carolina at Philadelphia

Series Standing
Hurricanes 3 — Flyers 0
  • Carolina 4, Philadelphia 1. Goals from Staal (PP), Svechnikov (PP), Chatfield (SHG), and Ehlers (5v5). Three on special teams, one at even strength — a model balanced scoresheet. Carolina was 2-for-9 on the power play; Philly was 0-for-5. Special teams decided everything.
  • The turning point came late in the second with the score 1–1: Hall took a boarding minor (initially called a major), Philly went on the power play with a chance to seize their first lead — and gave up the Chatfield SHG instead. Martinook poked the puck past Drysdale at the point, sprung the Staal-Chatfield 2-on-1, Staal threaded a pass through Zegras, Chatfield buried it. The defining sequence of the game came on Philly's power play.
  • The Brind'Amour bench minor that should have killed Carolina: after Chatfield's SHG, Brind'Amour barked at officials and earned an unsportsmanlike conduct call. That gave Philly a 5-on-3 for 1:15 in a 2–1 game with momentum. Philly didn't convert. They didn't really threaten. The Flyers' PP is now 3-for-33 in the playoffs and went 0-for-5 tonight.
  • Andersen continued to be the structural anchor. Multiple key first-period saves on Konecny and Bump kept the score 0–0. The Athletic flagged Carolina's all-situations shot share at 29–12 and high-danger chances 6–4 — but Philly was *fractions of an inch* from a multi-goal first period without those saves. And Slavin's stick save off the goal line with 1:34 left in the first prevented an actual goal.
  • Svechnikov got his first goal of the playoffs in the third on a perfect Aho seam pass. That's one of Carolina's stars finally arriving — and the publication's note from Day 4: when Svechnikov fully activates, Carolina becomes a different team. He'd been quiet in Round 1 and the first two games of this series. Tonight changed that.
  • The depth-scoring story is the most concerning thing for the rest of the bracket: Staal, Svechnikov, Chatfield were a combined 0 goals in 6 games entering tonight. Tonight: 3. Martinook, postgame: "Those three guys [Stankoven-Blake-Hall] have been carrying the load. It's nice when other guys can step up. We are a four-line, six-defensemen, two-goalie hockey team. For us to have success, everybody's gonna be around it."

What This Was
A masterclass in roster depth. Carolina won by getting goals from places Philly didn't expect them. Staal, Svechnikov, and Chatfield combining for three goals after a combined zero in the first six games tells you everything about how Carolina constructs a playoff run. The top line carries when needed. The depth activates when it's their turn. The PK does its job. Andersen does his. Every wheel turning.
The PK That Won The Series
30-for-32 across the postseason. 93.75% kill rate. Carolina killed off a 5-on-3 with momentum and a 1-goal lead in Game 3. They generated a SHG on Philly's power play. That's the kind of structural advantage that wins championships, and Carolina is now demonstrating it consistently across two series and seven games. Philly's PP isn't bad luck — they're 3-for-33 in the playoffs because Carolina's PK is operating at an elite level.
Philly's Reality
The Flyers had legitimate chances and got nothing. Bump had a glorious point-blank look at 1–1 in the second; pulled wide. Martone's line had pop. Zegras got his first point in five games. None of it was enough because Philly couldn't convert on the power play and Carolina's structure didn't break. Tocchet, on the PP: "When the pressure hits, the reads were not there. You can tell guys are nervous." Down 3–0 facing a sweep, the Flyers' season is one game from over.
Series Outlook · Updated
Hurricanes win in 4. Carolina has been the better team in every game of this series — and three of the games haven't been close on the underlying numbers. Game 4 Saturday in Philadelphia is the closeout opportunity. The Flyers will play hard. They competed in Game 2 (an OT loss). They competed for stretches tonight. None of it has produced a win, and there's no reason to think Game 4 changes that. Carolina is operating at a different level entirely.
§ 02

The 7–0 club.

Only the third NHL team in 30 years to start a playoff run on a seven-game winning streak. The list is short. The company is decorated.

Historical Context

Carolina is the third NHL team in 30 years to begin a postseason 7–0.

The Athletic surfaced this stat after Game 3, and it's the most meaningful structural data point of the postseason so far. Starting a playoff run with seven straight wins is so rare it's happened only twice in three decades. Carolina is now the third. The history of teams that win their first 7+ playoff games doesn't guarantee a Stanley Cup — but it places the team firmly in championship company. The bracket has 8 teams left. Carolina has more wins than any of them. They've also given up fewer goals per game, won by larger margins, and now demonstrated 4 different ways to win a playoff game.

§ 03

Tonight: Buffalo-Montreal G2.

Montreal needs a response. Buffalo wants a stranglehold. The series most likely to redefine its identity tonight.

  • Game 2 is the publication's most leveraged series game tonight. Carolina-Philly is essentially decided. Colorado-Minnesota is on pause until Saturday. Anaheim-Vegas is on pause until Friday. Buffalo-Montreal is where Round 2 narrative gets shaped tonight.
  • Montreal's adjustment thesis — Slow the neutral zone. Take away Buffalo's defensive activations. Punish the space they're given. St. Louis: "I'm confident that we can play any style. I'm confident that we can play the game that's in front of us." Tonight is the test.
  • Buffalo's Game 1 won't repeat in Game 2. Open ice doesn't last in playoff series — coaches adjust. The Sabres need to decide whether to push the same identity (and force Montreal to adjust) or absorb tighter hockey (and trust Lyon). The team that imposes pace wins. The publication's Day 6 thesis applied directly.
  • Dobeš and Lyon both swing performers. Dobeš was Montreal's best player for stretches of Round 1. Lyon won Buffalo Round 1. Whichever has the better Game 2 likely tilts the series.
  • The bracket implication — if Buffalo wins, they're 2–0 with home ice and the structural read. If Montreal wins, the series resets to Bell Centre tied at 1, and the "Habs can play any style" claim earns proof. Either result reshapes the East-Atlantic narrative significantly.
The Take
Carolina hasn't lost a playoff game. That fact alone now defines Round 2. Other teams are still learning how to dictate environments and adjust to opponents. Carolina has been doing both for seven straight games — across two opponents, four different game scripts, and now an exclusive 30-year historical club. Round 1 made them the structural standard. Round 2 is making them the team everyone else is being measured against. The bracket has eight teams left. One of them has more wins than the rest combined are away from elimination.
